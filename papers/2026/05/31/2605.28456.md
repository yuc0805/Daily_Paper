# Diffusion Large Language Models for Visual Speech Recognition (DLLM-VSR)

**Arxiv:** 2605.28456 | **Date:** 2026-05-31 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
DLLM-VSR is the first diffusion-LLM framework for visual speech recognition (lip reading from video). It formulates transcription as iterative masked denoising with flexible decoding order: high-confidence positions are committed first, then used as bidirectional context to refine ambiguous tokens. A two-stage masked-denoising training scheme separates visual-to-text content alignment from length modeling, and a length-guided candidate decoder uses video duration to propose plausible transcript lengths and rerank candidates. It reaches a state-of-the-art 19.5% word error rate on LRS3 using only its labeled training data.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM denoises continuous image pixels through a fixed forward-noise process, this paper denoises discrete masked text tokens conditioned on video, so the same iterative-refinement idea moves from pixel space into sequence decoding with confidence-ordered unmasking. Against the left-to-right autoregressive decoders that dominate VSR, the diffusion formulation removes the constraint of committing to ambiguous tokens before later context arrives. Audio Mamba sits nearby as another way to model speech signals, but through linear-time recurrence rather than masked denoising.

## Why Leo should care
The confidence-based unmasking idea is a candidate for transfer into bio-sensing. It is an unported opportunity: no paper in graphify Community 4 or 5 applies diffusion-style masked denoising to wearable sensor streams. Transfer hypothesis: treat gaps in a multivariate physiological stream as masked positions and recover them by confidence-ordered denoising, which suits block-structured (non-random) sensor dropout better than left-to-right imputation.
