# Aligning LLMs with Human Uncertainty: A Beta-Bernoulli Calibrator for LLM Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2605.27668 | **Date:** 2026-05-31 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
The paper introduces the Beta-Bernoulli Calibrator (BBC), a lightweight, model-agnostic, post-hoc layer that converts a single point-probability forecast from any LLM into a Beta distribution over the event likelihood. It is trained with two supervision signals at once: realized binary outcomes and human forecasts of the same events. The Beta mean is the recalibrated point forecast, and the Beta variance is an epistemic uncertainty estimate that predicts when the forecast is likely to be wrong.

## Delta from parents
Where TS-Agent made the reasoning steps that produce a forecast verifiable, and the survey catalogued how LLMs are coupled to time series, this paper leaves the forecast-production step untouched and adds a calibration layer on top of it. The contribution is orthogonal to the integration strategy: BBC is a post-hoc wrapper that recalibrates the output number and attaches a usable epistemic-variance signal, so it can sit on top of a tool-using forecaster rather than competing with it. The key empirical point is that learned epistemic variance predicts error better than verbalized confidence.

## Why Leo should care
LLM forecasting and calibrated uncertainty both matter for time-series work, and the wrapper design means it could be tested on a wearable-derived forecaster without retraining the base model. On the gap-watch axis: this imports post-hoc probability calibration into the LLM-forecasting setting (graphify Community 4), so the generic "add calibration to an LLM forecaster" move is now closed off. The open part is the multivariate, continuous-horizon case, which a scalar Beta distribution does not cover.
