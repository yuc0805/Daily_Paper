# Superposition Is Not Necessary: Mechanistic Interpretability of Transformer Representations for Time Series Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2605.05151 | **Date:** 2026-05-06 | **Area:** time-series, interpretable-ml

## What it does
Applies sparse autoencoders (SAEs) from mechanistic interpretability to probe PatchTST internal FFN representations. Finds that expanding SAE dictionary size from 0.5x to 4.0x produces negligible performance change (0.214% average). Overcomplete dictionaries remain largely inactive. Causal interventions on dominant latent features cause minimal forecast perturbation. Concludes that standard TS forecasting benchmarks do not require superposition.

## Delta from parents
Where PatchTST showed that patched transformers outperform prior TS transformers, and DLinear showed that linear models remain competitive, this paper explains why: PatchTST's FFN layers do not use superposition on forecasting benchmarks. The representations are sparse and low-dimensional by nature, which means the transformer's capacity for rich compositional representation is underutilized. This resolves the debate empirically rather than architecturally.

## Why Leo should care
This directly informs whether to use transformers vs linear models for TS tasks. The implication is that transformers earn their keep on tasks with richer structure (cross-modal, multi-task health prediction) but not on standard univariate forecasting. For Leo's bio-sensing work, this suggests that transformer capacity is justified when the task requires it (e.g., cross-modal health prediction as in the Tier A paper) but not for simple forecasting benchmarks.
