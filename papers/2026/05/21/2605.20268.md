# Chronicle: A Multimodal Foundation Model for Joint Language and Time Series Understanding

**Arxiv:** 2605.20268 | **Date:** 2026-05-18 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
Chronicle is a 324M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained from scratch on both natural language and time series within a single architecture. Both modalities share the same transformer blocks, attention, and residual stream. It matches Gemma-3-270M-PT on 19 NLU tasks, achieves state-of-the-art frozen-embedding time series classification on 24 UCR/UEA datasets, and beats all supervised fusion baselines on Time-MMD multimodal forecasts.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [VSCNJG5J] ChatTS (Xie 2025) — Showed that fine-tuning a pretrained LLM to accept multivariate TS input improves alignment and reasoning over time series.
- [N2JLZBY3] LLMs for TS Survey (Zhang 2024) — Categorized five paradigms for combining LLMs with time series; Chronicle introduces a sixth (native joint pretraining).
- [ELYUE3NF] Sensor2Text (Chen 2024) — Bridged sensor data and language via student-teacher distillation from vision-language models, a post-hoc adaptation approach.

## Delta from parents
Where ChatTS (Xie 2025) adapts a pretrained LLM to accept time series as a new input modality, Chronicle trains both modalities from scratch, arguing that post-hoc adaptation inherits representations that never saw temporal data. Where the LLMs for TS survey (Zhang 2024) catalogued prompting, quantization, alignment, vision bridging, and tool use as the five integration paradigms, Chronicle demonstrates that native joint pretraining is viable at small scale. Where Sensor2Text (Chen 2024) distills visual-language knowledge into a sensor encoder, Chronicle avoids the intermediate vision bottleneck entirely by sharing all parameters between text and time series from initialization.

## Why Leo should care
This is the first model jointly pretrained on text and time series from scratch, which could change the design space for sensor-language models in health applications. If the approach scales, it removes the dependency on adapting a frozen LLM and could produce better-aligned representations for wearable data. The paper's evaluation against unimodal foundation models (not just multimodal baselines) sets a useful standard.
