# Beyond Reasoning: Reinforcement Learning Unlocks Parametric Knowledge in LLMs

**Arxiv:** 2605.07153 | **Date:** 2026-05-08 | **Area:** llm, reasoning

## What it does
This paper shows that RL can improve LLM factual recall, not just reasoning. In a controlled zero-shot, closed-book QA setting with no chain-of-thought, RL yields roughly 27% average relative gains across three model families. Mechanistically, RL redistributes probability mass over existing knowledge — moving correct answers from the low-probability tail into reliable greedy generations — rather than acquiring new facts. The hardest examples (18% of training data whose answers never appear in 128 pre-RL samples) drive roughly 83% of the gain.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 and the broader RLVR literature focused on reasoning improvements, this paper shows RL has a second mode of action: redistributing probability mass to surface latent parametric knowledge. The mechanism is different from reasoning improvement — it is not about learning new inference steps but about making already-known facts more accessible during greedy decoding. This broadens the understanding of what RL actually does to LLM weights.

## Why Leo should care
The mechanistic insight — RL as probability mass redistribution rather than knowledge acquisition — is a clean finding worth knowing. If Leo ever applies RL to fine-tune models for health QA or sensor-to-text tasks, this suggests RL can help surface knowledge the model already has but fails to produce reliably.
