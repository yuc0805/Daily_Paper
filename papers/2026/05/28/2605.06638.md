# Can RL Teach Long-Horizon Reasoning to LLMs? Expressiveness Is Key

**Arxiv:** 2605.06638 | **Date:** 2026-05-07 | **Area:** reasoning, llm

## What it does
This paper introduces ScaleLogic, a synthetic logical reasoning framework with independent control over proof depth (horizon) and logical expressiveness (from simple if-then to first-order logic with conjunction, disjunction, negation, and universal quantification). It shows that RL training compute follows a power law with respect to reasoning depth (T proportional to D^gamma, R^2 > 0.99), and the scaling exponent gamma increases monotonically with logical expressiveness, from 1.04 to 2.60. Training on more expressive logics yields larger downstream gains (up to +10.66 points on math and reasoning benchmarks) and more compute-efficient transfer.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated RL-trained reasoning at scale but on natural, uncontrolled tasks, ScaleLogic provides the controlled synthetic environment to measure exactly how RL compute scales with task difficulty. The key finding — that what the model trains on (expressiveness) matters more than how much it trains — extends the conversation beyond the "scale compute" narrative that R1 represented. Where Wei 2023 showed that chain-of-thought prompting helps multi-step reasoning at inference time, ScaleLogic shows that RL training on deeper chains follows predictable power laws, and training on richer logical structures transfers better.

## Why Leo should care
The power-law scaling result is clean and useful for anyone deciding how to allocate RL compute for reasoning tasks. The finding that training data expressiveness matters more than quantity has a potential analogy in time-series: training on more complex temporal patterns (multi-modal, multi-scale) may transfer better than training on more of the same simple patterns.
