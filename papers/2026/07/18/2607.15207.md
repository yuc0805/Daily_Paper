# BadWAM: When World-Action Models Dream Right but Act Wrong

**Arxiv:** 2607.15207 | **Date:** 2026-07-18 | **Area:** world-model-rl

## What it does
BadWAM defines and evaluates World-Action Drift Attacks against world-action models (WAMs), which couple action generation with prediction of the future world. Small visual perturbations break the alignment between what a WAM imagines and what it executes. An action-only variant drives the model toward task-failing actions (success dropping from 96.5% to 43.1% in one setting); a stealthier imagination-preserving variant keeps the predicted future close to the clean one while still hijacking the action.

## Delta from parents
Where the world-model line treats the learned or explicit model as a source of robustness and interpretability, BadWAM treats it as a new attack surface, because coupling action and imagination creates a seam an adversary can pry apart. Where From Pixels to States argued explicit state improves control, BadWAM shows that if imagination and action are only loosely coupled, the imagination can look correct while the action is already wrong, so the "check the action against the imagined future" safety story does not hold.

## Why Leo should care
This is a safety-and-robustness result about coupled predictive models. For his own work it is a caution: any system that pairs a prediction head with an action or decision head (including physiological forecasting plus intervention) cannot assume the two stay aligned under small input shifts. The imagination-preserving attack is the transferable insight — a plausible-looking forecast is not evidence that the downstream decision is safe.
