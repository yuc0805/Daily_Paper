# Morphing into Hybrid Attention Models (FlashMorph)

**Arxiv:** 2606.30562 | **Date:** 2026-07-03 | **Area:** mamba

## What it does
Hybrid attention models cut the cost of long-context Transformers by keeping full attention in only some layers and replacing the rest with linear attention. The open question is which layers should keep full attention. FlashMorph frames this as a budget-constrained subset selection problem instead of a heuristic. It builds a morphable model where every full-attention layer also has a linear-attention branch, freezes the weights, and jointly learns per-layer gates on synthetic long-context retrieval data with a regularizer that pushes toward linear attention. The gates are then discretized under a full-attention budget, followed by distillation and long-context fine-tuning.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)

## Delta from parents
Where Mamba replaces attention wholesale with a state space model, FlashMorph keeps a chosen subset of full-attention layers and converts only the rest, treating the choice as a joint optimization rather than a fixed pattern. Where earlier hybrid methods score layers one at a time, FlashMorph optimizes all the gates together, because layer importance is interdependent under a global budget. The claim is that this finds better hybrid configurations at lower selection cost while keeping long-context recall.

## Why Leo should care
Efficient long-context modeling is a method Leo watches for porting. FlashMorph is a principled way to decide where to spend full attention versus a cheap linear operator, and long wearable-sensor windows are exactly the setting where that budget matters. See the Gap Watch: this layer-selection idea is not yet applied to time-series or sensing encoders.
