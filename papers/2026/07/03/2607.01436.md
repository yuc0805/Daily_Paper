# Discrete Diffusion Language Models for Interactive Radiology Report Drafting

**Arxiv:** 2607.01436 | **Date:** 2026-07-03 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
The paper takes a diffusion language model (DiffusionGemma-26B, a mixture-of-experts model with 3.8B active parameters) and fine-tunes it for medical visual question answering, comparing it head-to-head against its autoregressive sibling Gemma-4-26B under an identical LoRA recipe. Diffusion language models write text by denoising a whole token canvas at once instead of emitting one token left to right. The finetuned diffusion model matches or beats the autoregressive one on VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and VQA-Med, decodes 3.5 to 4.4 times faster, and adds a capability autoregression does not have: any-order infill, where a radiologist pins the parts of a report they are sure of and the model fills the gaps using context from both sides.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [GX7WR7KA] DDPM — the denoising diffusion formulation that discrete diffusion language models adapt from images to text.
- [9UA578GP] DDIM — faster deterministic diffusion sampling, the line of work behind the speed claim.
- [PZATM3SC] LoRA — the parameter-efficient fine-tuning recipe held identical across the two models so the generative paradigm is the only variable.
- [ATEFZQA4] Med-VQA Hallucination — prior evidence on medical VQA answer quality, the benchmark setting.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM and DDIM denoise continuous image pixels, this paper works in the discrete token space of text and shows the approach is competitive with autoregression on a medical task, not just on open-domain text. Where medical foundation models are almost all autoregressive, this paper keeps the LoRA training identical and swaps only the generative paradigm, so the comparison is clean. The new capability is interactive infill: because the canvas is denoised from both sides, the model can fill text between fixed fragments, which autoregression handles poorly.

## Why Leo should care
This is a diffusion method (a generative-CV community idea) crossing into clinical text drafting. For Leo it is a concrete case of a community-3 method entering llm-health. The any-order infill property is the interesting transfer: bidirectional denoising as a way to let a human clamp known values and have the model fill the rest is a pattern that could apply to gap-filling in sensor records, not only reports.
