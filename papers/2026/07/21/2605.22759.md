# SensorFM: A Wearable Health Foundation Model Pretrained on One Trillion Minutes of Sensor Data

**Arxiv:** 2605.22759 | **Date:** 2026-07-21 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
SensorFM is a self-supervised foundation model for wearable health data. It ingests 34 one-minute aggregate features from five sensors (PPG, accelerometer, EDA, skin temperature, altimeter) and is pretrained by masked reconstruction on more than one trillion minutes of data from about 5 million people. Downstream tasks attach light heads to the frozen encoder. The largest variant beats conventional baselines on 34 of 35 tasks, with an average +9% AUC on classification and +21% Pearson on regression, and retains 99.7% step-count and 99.9% deep-sleep accuracy when 60 contiguous minutes are ablated.

## Delta from parents
Where Yuan 2024 pretrained on a single sensor family (accelerometer) at 700K person-days, SensorFM applies the same masked-prediction recipe across five sensor families at roughly a thousand times more data, because the open question those parents left was whether one encoder could fuse heterogeneous sensors rather than model each in isolation. Gu 2025 described the design space; SensorFM instantiates the largest point in it and reports the missing empirical numbers. Against GLOBEM (community 5), which supplied the multi-year behavior data and the finding that cross-year transfer is hard, SensorFM supplies the pretrained representation that such behavior tasks were lacking. This extends the community 1 self-supervised-health cluster toward the community 5 wearable-behavior cluster.

## Why Leo should care
This is the current strongest model in Leo's primary working area: one self-supervised model over multi-sensor wearable time series at a scale no academic group can match. The imported method is the masked autoencoder from self-supervised vision (community 1), now applied at population scale to wearables, so that particular transfer is closed off as low-hanging fruit. The remaining opening is the input granularity: SensorFM uses one-minute aggregate features, not raw waveforms, so a raw-waveform foundation model is the obvious next step.
