# HEARTS: Benchmarking LLM Reasoning on Health Time Series

**Arxiv:** 2603.06638 | **Date:** 2026-07-21 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
HEARTS is a benchmark, not a model. It integrates 16 real-world datasets across 12 health domains and 20 signal modalities and defines 110 tasks grouped into perception, inference, generation, and deduction. Evaluating 16 state-of-the-art LLMs on more than 20K samples, it finds that LLMs underperform specialized models, lean on heuristics, and fail at multi-step temporal reasoning, with performance degrading as sequences lengthen and sampling frequency rises. Input format (text, image, raw file) shifts absolute scores but keeps the relative difficulty ordering fixed.

## Delta from parents
Where TS-Agent and ChatTS proposed methods and reported wins on their own task sets, HEARTS builds the shared adversarial testbed that measures where those methods break, because the field had claims of LLM time-series reasoning with no common yardstick. Against Liu 2023, which established that LLMs can read health signals at all, HEARTS quantifies how far that ability generalizes and where it collapses. It turns the Zhang 2024 survey from a map of methods into a measurable failure taxonomy across domains, modalities, and sequence lengths.

## Why Leo should care
HEARTS is directly in Leo's primary area and gives a reusable measuring stick for any health-signal reasoning method he builds. It does not import a new CV/NLP method; instead it exposes where existing LLM reasoning methods fail on signals, which is where new work should aim. The finding that longer sequences and higher sampling rates hurt most points at a concrete opening: architectures that keep temporal reasoning stable as resolution grows.
