# Signal or Noise? Understanding Generative Models for Real-World Sensor Time Series

**Arxiv:** 2607.04245 | **Date:** 2026-07-25 | **Area:** time-series, generative-cv

## What it does
The paper introduces SensorGen, a controlled study of generating real-world sensor time series. It spans 14 settings across 4 domains, 7 datasets, and 12 signal modalities, and evaluates five families of generative models under one protocol, measuring both signal fidelity and downstream usefulness. It reports that flow-matching models are the strongest overall, that signal properties decide which design choices help (demographic covariates for longitudinal generation, time-frequency modeling for high-frequency signals), and that synthetic signals improve downstream prediction, not only visual realism.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM and Diffusion Beats GANs settled the generative-quality question for images and found diffusion the strongest family, this paper asks the same question for sensor time series and finds a different answer: flow-matching, not diffusion, is the strongest default. The reason is that sensor signals carry measurement noise and time-frequency structure that natural images do not, so the design choices that help images do not transfer directly. Where Gu 2025 surveyed biosignal models and named the lack of systematic comparison as a gap, this paper fills it with a 14-setting protocol. The work extends the generative line inside community 3 (the CycleGAN-to-DDPM bridge) across into the time-series and wearable communities (communities 4 and 5).

## Why Leo should care
This is a direct map of the generative toolbox onto sensor time series, Leo's primary area. It names the family to default to (flow-matching), the conditioning that pays off (demographics for slow trends, time-frequency for fast components), and the fact that synthetic sensor data is worth generating for augmentation. On the porting question: it imports flow-matching and diffusion from the CV/generative community into sensor data and evaluates them head to head, which closes off "apply generative family X to sensor data" as easy, unclaimed ground — the systematic comparison now exists.
