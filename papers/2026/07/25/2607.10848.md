# Predictive Divergence Masks for LLM RL

**Arxiv:** 2607.10848 | **Date:** 2026-07-25 | **Area:** reasoning, llm

## What it does
The paper repairs the direction criterion in trust-region masks used for reinforcement learning of language models. PPO-style RL masks tokens using a sampled-token importance ratio for two tests: a proximity test (has the policy moved too far) and a direction test (does the update push it farther). The recent DPPO method replaced the proximity test with a probability divergence but kept PPO's ratio-based direction test. The authors show that the single-sample ratio can disagree in sign with the change of the divergence the proximity test uses, and propose a predictive divergence mask that asks whether the next gradient step will raise or lower that divergence, derived in closed form for softmax policies. Two lightweight top-K estimators handle the truncated vocabulary that rollout engines expose, and the masks improve RL training across model scales and precision settings.

## Delta from parents
Where PPO clips updates on a single-sample importance ratio, this paper replaces the direction test with a predicted change in the trust-region divergence itself, because the ratio is a noisy proxy that can point the wrong way. Where DeepSeek-R1 and Latent-GRPO rely on this RL machinery to train reasoning behavior, this paper sharpens the machinery underneath them rather than changing the reward or the task. The closed-form prediction for softmax policies and the top-K estimators are the new technical parts.

## Why Leo should care
Reasoning and RL are a secondary watch area. The idea worth filing is that a trust-region direction test should predict the change in the same quantity the proximity test measures, rather than using a separate single-sample proxy — a consistency argument that applies to any clipped policy-gradient method. This connects the world-model-RL side of Leo's library (PPO) to the reasoning side (DeepSeek-R1, Latent-GRPO).
