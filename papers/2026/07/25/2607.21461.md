# AREX: Towards a Recursively Self-Improving Agent for Deep Research

**Arxiv:** 2607.21461 | **Date:** 2026-07-25 | **Area:** agent, reasoning

## What it does
AREX is a family of deep-research agents built on a discovery-verification asymmetry: finding an answer that satisfies many constraints is costly, but checking a candidate splits into cheap constraint-wise tests. The agent alternates an inner loop that gathers evidence and drafts a provisional answer with an outer loop that audits the draft constraint by constraint and launches targeted follow-up research. It learns a context-update tool that compresses interaction history into a compact state of verified evidence and open constraints, and is trained with agentic mid-training and long-horizon reinforcement learning. It ships as a dense 4B model and a 122B-A10B mixture-of-experts model, and outperforms comparable-scale baselines on BrowseComp, WideSearch, DeepSearchQA, and Humanity's Last Exam.

## Delta from parents
Where LATS searches a tree of actions and backtracks within a single pass, AREX adds an outer self-improvement loop that verifies intermediate answers and re-plans from the partially verified state, because deep research rewards constraint-checking more than searching longer. Where ToolkenGPT learned to call external tools, AREX learns a context-update tool that manages its own growing memory without an external model. This extends the planning-decomposition bridge already in Leo's library (Adapt to LATS) toward verification-guided refinement over long horizons.

## Why Leo should care
Agents and reasoning are a secondary watch area. The transferable idea is the discovery-verification asymmetry: when checking is cheaper than searching, spend compute on auditing intermediate answers and re-planning, not on longer single-shot search. The learned history-compression tool is a general long-horizon trick that could apply to any agent that must hold a growing state, including multi-step analysis over sensor or health data.
