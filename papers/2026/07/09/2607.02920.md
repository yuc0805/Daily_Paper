# CLeaD: Layer-wise Cross-Lingual Depression Detection from Speech

**Arxiv:** 2607.02920 | **Date:** 2026-07-09 | **Area:** llm-health (Community 1: Health AI & Self-Supervised)

## What it does
Proposes CLeaD, a supervised contrastive alignment framework that maps WavLM speech embeddings from English and Mandarin into one shared clinical space for depression detection, without parallel data or target-language fine-tuning. On 52 Mandarin speakers under leave-one-speaker-out evaluation it beats the baseline modestly (F1 0.640 versus 0.622) and improves depressed-class recall at encoder layers 7 to 8. It also shows that segment-level random splits leak speaker identity and inflated earlier Mandarin F1 to 0.954.

## Delta from parents
Where HeAR learned strong health-acoustic features within a single language and the Biosignals survey mapped within-domain models, CLeaD asks the transfer question those works left open: do such representations carry across languages? It answers with a supervised contrastive alignment into a shared space and, more usefully, exposes the speaker-leakage artifact that made prior cross-lingual numbers look far better than they are. This paper extends the Sensor2Text bridge (chen2024_sensor2text), which already crossed bio-sensing and llm-health in the library, pushing that bridge toward clinical speech and honest cross-lingual evaluation.

## Why Leo should care
This is a direct primary-area result: it imports cross-lingual supervised contrastive alignment (a speech and NLP method) into clinical bio-acoustics, and it documents an evaluation pitfall (speaker-grouped splits) that applies to any wearable or physiological classifier. Cross-lingual contrastive alignment for health speech is now occupied ground.
