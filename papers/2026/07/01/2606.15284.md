# CAP: Towards PPG Universal Representation Learning with Patient-level Supervision

**Arxiv:** 2606.15284 | **Date:** 2026-07-01 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
CAP learns a reusable encoder for photoplethysmography (PPG) signals by anchoring them to patient-level clinical text rather than to the signal alone. The authors build a paired PPG plus electronic health record dataset for 2,279 patients, where a language model distills fragmented diagnosis codes and notes into one coherent patient-level record. Pretraining runs cross-modal contrastive alignment between the PPG encoder and this clinical text, together with a morphological reconstruction loss and a physiological stability loss. On four downstream tasks it reports an average relative gain of +26.7 percent and up to +87.6 percent relative on respiratory rate over the best prior method.

## Delta from parents
Where the biosignal survey line of work learned PPG representations from the signal alone, CAP adds patient-level clinical text as the supervision anchor, because two waveforms can look alike while coming from patients in different physiological states. Where Sensor2Text aligned activity sensors to natural-language activity descriptions, CAP aligns PPG to structured patient-level clinical semantics distilled from records, which is a stronger and more clinical supervision signal. CAP keeps a masked reconstruction objective in the spirit of the masked-autoencoder analysis, but pairs it with the clinical anchoring and a longer temporal window so the encoder captures long-horizon physiological drift rather than only short segments. The net change is a PPG encoder supervised by clinical state, not waveform shape.

## Why Leo should care
This is in the bio-sensing area. It imports cross-modal contrastive alignment (the CLIP or SigLIP style of pairing a signal with text, already present in your library through Sensor2Text) into PPG and grounds it in patient-level EHR text. For the porting watch, contrastive signal-to-clinical-text alignment for PPG on standard cardiorespiratory tasks is now demonstrated and is closing as an opportunity. What remains open is other wearable modalities (accelerometer, IMU behavioral streams), multimodal sensor fusion, and process-level reasoning over the aligned representation. This paper also extends the Sensor2Text to Health-LLM bridge in your library from activity captioning toward clinical grounding of a raw physiological signal.
