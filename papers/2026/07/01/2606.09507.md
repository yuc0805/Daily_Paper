# Prisma-World: Camera-Controllable Multi-Agent Video World Model

**Arxiv:** 2606.09507 | **Date:** 2026-07-01 | **Area:** generative-cv

## What it does
Prisma-World generates multi-agent video where overlapping views must agree on one shared scene. It processes all agent videos in a single full-attention sequence, uses a multi-agent rotary position design to keep agent identities separate while sharing a synchronized time axis, and injects relative camera geometry into attention so overlapping viewpoints are biased toward the same scene evidence. Training uses an overlap-decaying curriculum and minimap-conditioned structural guidance. The authors release PrismaDataset, a synthetic (UE5) set with panoramic capture and exact camera and action labels. One model then generates high-fidelity multi-agent video with flexible agent counts, camera control, and better cross-view consistency.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [YJ9TK993] DiT: Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers (Peebles 2023) — the transformer diffusion backbone and full-attention design.
- [GX7WR7KA] DDPM: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Ho 2020) — the denoising process, here made geometry-aware and joint across views.

## Delta from parents
Where DiT denoises one view, Prisma-World puts all views in one full-attention sequence and injects explicit relative camera geometry so the denoising is joint and geometry-aware. Compared to MetaWorld, released the same month, Prisma-World relies on geometry-conditioned full attention and a synthetic dataset with exact camera labels, while MetaWorld learns cross-view coupling from real single-view video. Both add cross-view consistency on top of the DiT and DDPM stack, by different means.

## Why Leo should care
Prisma-World is the geometry-conditioned counterpart to MetaWorld for the same consistency problem. For the porting watch, the transferable idea is the same: treat multi-channel physiological recordings as multiple views of one state and enforce consistency, here through geometry-style conditioning rather than learned cross-attention.
