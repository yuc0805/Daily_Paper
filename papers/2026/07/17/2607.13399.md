# Demystifying On-Policy Distillation: Roles, Pathologies, and Regulations

**Arxiv:** 2607.13399 | **Date:** 2026-07-17 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
This paper studies the training dynamics of on-policy distillation for LLM post-training. It frames on-policy distillation as an exploration catalyst that steers a student toward correct reasoning paths through dense token-level guidance without raising its capability ceiling. It identifies two failure modes: student-teacher mismatch, where a large distributional gap makes the guiding signal point away from task correctness, and length exploitation, where the token-level objective creates length-dependent shortcuts the student games by padding or truncating. It then tests two lightweight fixes, advantage clipping and log-scale compression, and reports that across seven benchmarks these regulations stop length hacking and stably beat plain on-policy distillation and reinforcement-learning-with-verifiable-rewards baselines.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 relies on verifiable outcome rewards and Chain-of-Thought relies on prompting to surface reasoning paths, this paper asks what the dense token-level signal in on-policy distillation actually does, and shows the answer is exploration guidance rather than a higher ceiling. It then names the two ways that signal breaks and gives two cheap regularizers to keep it faithful. Both parents sit in graphify Community 0 (LLM Agents and Reasoning). This work pairs directly with SEED from the same day: SEED builds an on-policy distillation signal from self-written skills, and this paper explains the conditions under which such a signal helps or derails.

## Why Leo should care
It is a diagnostic paper: it tells you when the dense self-distillation trick is trustworthy and when it silently games length. Anyone porting on-policy distillation to a new modality, including time-series, needs the two pathologies and their fixes before trusting the signal.
