# Read It Back: Pretrained MLLMs Are Zero-Shot Reward Models for Text-to-Image Generation

**Arxiv:** 2607.11886 | **Date:** 2026-07-15 | **Area:** generative-cv

## What it does
The paper proposes SpectraReward, a training-free reward for reinforcement-learning fine-tuning of image generators. Instead of asking a multimodal language model to score an image or answer checklist questions, it measures how well the original prompt can be recovered from the generated image in a single image-conditioned, teacher-forced forward pass. The average image-conditioned prompt log-likelihood becomes the reward, reusing the model's pretrained image-text alignment with no preference labels and no reward-model training. It is tested across two diffusion models, three RL algorithms, nine language-model backbones from 4B to 235B parameters, and five out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM and DiT define how to sample images, this paper defines how to grade them during RL fine-tuning, and it does so without training a separate reward model. Prior reward approaches either fine-tuned a scorer on human preferences or decomposed the prompt into verification questions; SpectraReward instead reads the prompt back out of the image and uses the recovery likelihood, because a well-aligned multimodal model already assigns higher likelihood to prompts that match the image. SigLIP, the natural image-text-alignment anchor, is not in your library yet, so the closest neighbors here are the community-3 generative anchors DDPM and DiT.

## Why Leo should care
The mechanism is a clean, label-free reward built only from a frozen model's own likelihoods. That pattern transfers: any domain with a paired encoder-decoder alignment model could use prompt-recovery likelihood as a reward signal. See the Gap Watch for the time-series version of this idea.
