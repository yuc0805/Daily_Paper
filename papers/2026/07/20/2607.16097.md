# Understanding Reasoning from Pretraining to Post-Training

**Arxiv:** 2607.16097 | **Date:** 2026-07-20 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper uses chess as a controlled testbed to study reasoning across the full language-model pipeline: pretraining on human games (5M to 1B parameters), supervised fine-tuning on synthetic reasoning traces, then RL on chess puzzles with a verifiable reward. Because the domain is closed and the reward is exact, it can attribute behaviors to pretraining versus RL and sweep both stages. It finds that post-RL performance at a given RL compute level is well predicted by the pretraining loss, and the slope of the RL reward curve rises roughly linearly with pretraining tokens. It also shows RL does not merely sharpen the fine-tuned policy: on hard puzzles RL surfaces correct moves that were nearly absent before. The pattern reproduces on a 1B model trained on mathematics text.

## Delta from parents
Where Wei showed reasoning could be drawn out of a frozen model by prompting, and DeepSeek-R1 showed it could be trained in through RL, this paper measures how the earlier pretraining sets the ceiling and the speed of that RL stage, because neither parent separated the contribution of pretraining from the contribution of RL. It replaces the "RL just works" framing with a predictive relationship: pretraining loss predicts post-RL reward, and pretraining tokens set the RL slope. ## Why Leo should care
This is the pretraining-to-RL seam Leo watches for porting. If pretraining loss predicts post-RL gains for a forecaster, a lab can decide how much to pretrain a numeric time-series model before spending scarce reward budget on RL fine-tuning. The "hard cases surface new correct behavior" finding is a testable claim for time-series reasoning on rare events. The method (a controlled testbed to separate pretraining from RL) is itself the transferable idea, not yet applied to time-series or bio-sensing.
