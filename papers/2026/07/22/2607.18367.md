# AlayaWorld: Interactive Long-Horizon World Modeling

**Arxiv:** 2607.18367 | **Date:** 2026-07-22 | **Area:** generative-cv

## What it does
AlayaWorld is a video world model that generates interactive environments from text, an image, or video. It produces 24-fps video at 540p and 720p from a 15B video diffusion transformer, generating short latent chunks one after another under a given camera trajectory and switchable text prompts. To hold the scene together over long runs it uses a bounded visual context: a persistent sink frame, compressed temporal history, geometry-aligned spatial memory, and recent-frame conditioning. To limit drift it trains on corrupted histories and on prediction residuals collected from the model's own rollouts. A discrete autoregressive distillation step cuts inference from about 30 sampling steps to four per chunk, and the model reports the best long-horizon score on iWorld-Bench.

## Delta from parents
Where DiT showed a transformer can replace the U-Net inside a diffusion model for single images, AlayaWorld extends that backbone to autoregressive video chunks with an explicit memory so frames stay consistent over long horizons. Where DDPM defined a fixed multi-step denoising process, AlayaWorld distills it to four steps per chunk so the model can respond at interactive speed. Both parents sit in the Vision-Language and Generative community (community 3) of Leo's library, and the graphify bridge from CycleGAN to DDPM already links image translation to diffusion there; AlayaWorld pushes that generative line into interactive, memory-conditioned video.

## Why Leo should care
Multimodal and generative modeling is a secondary watch area. The interesting transferable parts are the drift-control tricks: training on corrupted histories and on self-generated residuals, plus a geometry-aligned memory that keeps a long sequence coherent. Those are general sequence-stability ideas that could apply to long-horizon physiological signal generation, not only to video.
