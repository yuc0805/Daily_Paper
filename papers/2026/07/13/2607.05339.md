# TREK: Distill to Explore, Reinforce to Refine

**Arxiv:** 2607.05339 | **Date:** 2026-07-13 | **Area:** reasoning (also world-model-rl)

## What it does
TREK addresses a failure of Group Relative Policy Optimization: it stalls on hard prompts whose correct solutions lie outside the student's current sampling range. TREK finds near-zero-pass-rate prompts, queries a teacher (external, or the same model with extra context) for verified solutions, keeps the top candidates by student likelihood, runs a short forward-KL phase to pull those modes into the student's range, then returns to on-policy reinforcement learning. With DeepSeek-V4 proposals, Qwen3-8B improves AIME 2025 from 36.9 to 40.3 and AIME 2024 from 47.9 to 51.1; ALFWorld rises 75.8 to 82.8, ScienceWorld 12.5 to 26.7.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 and the GRPO family improve reasoning by rewarding trajectories the model already samples, TREK first imports verified off-support solutions through a short distillation phase, then reinforces, because the reward is useless when the model never samples a correct answer. It is a targeted fix for the exploration failure those parents share, and it works with a black-box teacher because it consumes only verified output trajectories.

## Why Leo should care
Distillation-for-exploration is a general recipe for any reinforcement-learning task with sparse correct samples, including reasoning agents over physiological data where correct trajectories are rare. Method imported: staged forward-KL warm start before on-policy reinforcement learning.
