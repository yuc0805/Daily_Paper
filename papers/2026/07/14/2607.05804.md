# TurnOPD: Making On-Policy Distillation Turn-Aware for Efficient Long-Horizon Agent Training

**Arxiv:** 2607.05804 | **Date:** 2026-07-14 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
TurnOPD improves on-policy distillation for multi-turn agents by budgeting supervision across turns. It finds two wastes in plain on-policy distillation: full-length rollouts spend wall-clock time on late turns that give weak, noisy supervision, and a trajectory-level objective concentrates loss on shallow early tokens so deep decision turns stay under-trained. It adds adaptive rollout-depth budgeting (probe statistics set how many turns to collect) and progressive turn-normalized loss budgeting (loss weighting moves from token-level to turn-balanced over training). On ALFWorld, WebShop, and Multi-Hop Search it reaches higher validation accuracy for the same wall-clock budget.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 shaped one reasoning trace with reinforcement learning, TurnOPD works one level up, at the multi-turn agent trajectory, and asks where in that trajectory supervision should be spent. Where ADaPT split a long task into sub-tasks, TurnOPD keeps the task fixed but budgets training effort across its turns, because the bottleneck it targets is training efficiency under a wall-clock budget rather than task decomposition. The contribution is the pair of turn-level controllers that reallocate rollout depth and loss weight.

## Why Leo should care
Long-horizon agent training that reads and reasons over many turns is close to how a monitoring agent would process a long stream of sensor windows. The turn-budgeting idea (spend supervision where the decision is hard, not on every step) is a training-efficiency lever that could transfer to sequence models over long physiological data. It is a method paper rather than a signal paper, so it sits in the secondary watch list.
