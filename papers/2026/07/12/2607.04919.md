# When Do Foundation Models Pay Off? A Break-Even Analysis of Pretrained Time Series Forecasters

**Arxiv:** 2607.04919 | **Date:** 2026-07-12 | **Area:** time-series (Community 4: Time Series + LLM Integration)

## What it does
Measures when a pretrained time-series foundation model beats a classical forecaster as a function of how much task data you have. Across 30 benchmark datasets it compares zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned foundation models (Chronos, Moirai, Lag-Llama) against Naive, ETS, ARIMA, and XGBoost at six training-set sizes from 2 percent to 100 percent of available data. On 15 of 30 datasets foundation models win at every training fraction; on 6, classical methods already win with as little as 2 percent of the data (21 to 2,768 samples); on the remaining 9 the break-even sits between 24 and 8,361 samples. The authors distill one training-free rule: if the series has fewer than 700 training points and clear seasonality, use the foundation model zero-shot and skip fine-tuning. They also report that LoRA fine-tuning can lower accuracy on short series.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos and Moirai each argued for their own model by reporting aggregate zero-shot wins, this paper stops advocating for any single model and asks the deployment question directly: given N task samples, does the foundation model earn its GPU cost over XGBoost? Where PatchTST fixed the supervised architecture, this work fixes the decision procedure, because the useful quantity for a practitioner is not the best average score but the data threshold at which the choice flips. The result is a two-step rule (compute series length and seasonality strength, run a 5 to 10 percent pilot only if needed) rather than a new model.

## Why Leo should care
This is a primary-area result that speaks straight to wearable and physiological forecasting, where labeled task data is usually small. The n<700-plus-seasonality rule is directly testable on PPG, heart-rate, and activity streams, and the finding that LoRA can hurt short series is a warning for the low-data clinical setting. No new CV or NLP method is imported here; it is a measurement paper, so the contribution is a decision tool rather than a ported technique.
