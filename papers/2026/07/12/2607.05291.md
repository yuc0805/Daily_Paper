# Forecasting Realized Volatility with Time Series Foundation Models: A Comparison with Econometric Benchmarks

**Arxiv:** 2607.05291 | **Date:** 2026-07-12 | **Area:** time-series (Community 4: Time Series + LLM Integration)

## What it does
Tests whether zero-shot time-series foundation models beat standard econometric volatility models. Using the VOLARE dataset it compares nine zero-shot foundation models against eight econometric specifications, including the Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) family, across 50 assets in equities, foreign exchange, and futures and three forecast horizons. Foundation models do not deliver a uniform gain: pooled losses favor them, but the edge is concentrated in a few outlier assets, and after averaging each asset's loss ratio to a Log-HAR benchmark only one small model, Tiny Time Mixers, beats the benchmark at every horizon, and only narrowly. A Mincer-Zarnowitz recalibration shows most of the short-horizon advantage is better scaling, not better prediction of volatility dynamics. An equal-weight average of Tiny Time Mixers and Log-HAR enters the Model Confidence Set for 98 to 100 percent of assets.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos reported broad zero-shot wins on general benchmarks, this paper puts the same class of model against well-specified domain baselines (the HAR family) on a single hard task, realized volatility, and finds the advantage mostly evaporates once you stop letting a few assets dominate the average. The reframing matters because it separates two things a pooled score hides: genuine gain in predicting dynamics versus a scaling correction that a simple recalibration also supplies. The practical output is not a new model but a finding that architecture choice among foundation models matters more than the foundation-versus-econometric split.

## Why Leo should care
It is a second, independent primary-area data point, alongside the break-even paper, that pretrained forecasters do not automatically beat a well-tuned domain baseline, and that averaging protocol changes the verdict. For bio-signal work the transferable lesson is methodological: report per-subject loss ratios rather than pooled loss, because a few subjects can otherwise carry an apparent foundation-model win. The equal-weight ensemble result also suggests a cheap hedge when the best model is unknown in advance.
