# Listening Forward: Next Patch Embedding Prediction Enables Scalable Audio Learners (NAPE)

**Arxiv:** 2608.19863 | **Date:** 2026-08-23 | **Area:** self-supervised

## What it does
NAPE pretrains an audio encoder by having a causal Transformer predict the next log-mel patch embedding from the preceding ones, using causal masking and a stop-gradient as the only training signal. There is no reconstruction decoder, no acoustic tokenizer, no student-teacher pair and no auxiliary regularization loss. Across six audio and speech benchmarks it reaches state-of-the-art fine-tuning performance on several tasks, scales consistently as encoder size grows, and gives strong linear-probe numbers. The authors also report that structured attention patterns appear without any supervision that asks for them.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [65EDEWIJ] Audio MAE (Huang 2022) — established masked patch reconstruction on log-mel spectrograms as the default audio self-supervision recipe.
- [6INGKIJV] How Mask Matters: Masked Autoencoders Theory (Zhang 2023) — analysed what the masking objective actually supplies to the learned representation.
- [3LA8GNCU] HeAR: Health Acoustic Representations (Baur 2024) — carried the masked-spectrogram recipe into health acoustics.

## Delta from parents
Where Audio MAE masked random patches and asked a decoder to reconstruct pixels of the spectrogram, NAPE removes the mask and the decoder and asks a causal encoder to predict the next patch *embedding*, because audio is temporally ordered and the next-element objective is the one that scaled in language and, more recently, in vision. Where the masked-autoencoder analysis in Zhang 2023 treated the mask ratio and the reconstruction target as the knobs that determine representation quality, NAPE removes both knobs and reports that a stop-gradient is sufficient to prevent collapse. The practical difference is the count of moving parts: the recipe drops four components that current audio SSL systems all carry, and still holds its ranking as encoder size grows, which is the part that matters for whether the simplification survives scaling.

## Why Leo should care
This is the clearest unported opportunity on the list. Biosignal foundation models in Leo's library, including HeAR and the SSL-for-HAR line, are all masked-reconstruction systems in graphify Community 1; none of them use causal next-embedding prediction. Physiological signals are at least as temporally ordered as audio, and PPG or accelerometer windows patch as naturally as a log-mel spectrogram, so the transfer is close to mechanical. The concrete test is to swap the log-mel front end for a per-channel PPG or ECG spectrogram, keep the causal masking and stop-gradient exactly as published, and check whether it holds up under the sensor noise and missingness that audio does not have.
