# TinyCast: Probabilistic Zero-Shot Forecasting with Computed Periodicity

**Arxiv:** 2608.15767 | **Date:** 2026-08-23 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
TinyCast is a zero-shot time-series forecaster with 146,505 parameters and no attention anywhere in the model. A spectral detector with zero learned parameters reads the dominant periods out of the context window, the context is folded on the phase of those periods, and a dilated convolutional encoder plus a block-autoregressive quantile decoder model whatever is left. The working premise is that at this parameter budget the periodic structure of a series is worth computing directly rather than spending capacity to learn it. On GIFT-Eval it is the smallest zero-shot entry whose parameter count can be established, and among entries that declare no test-data leakage it is the only model below 1.4M parameters that emits a predictive distribution rather than point forecasts. On Chronos-ZS and fev-bench every neural model that scores ahead of it carries at least 28 times its parameters. Because every learned operation is a convolution, a matrix multiplication or a normalization, it exports to static INT8 and runs a full forecast on a Cortex-M7 in 4.08 seconds inside 731 KB of RAM, at a cost of roughly 2% of point accuracy.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [HLTA8MDK] TimesNet (Wu 2023) — established FFT-based dominant-period detection and folding a 1D series into a periodic 2D layout as the core inductive bias for time-series backbones.
- [72DFULQQ] Chronos (Ansari 2024) — established that a pretrained sequence model can forecast unseen series zero-shot, and set the scale-first template for time-series foundation models.
- [5I6GAKC4] TimeGPT-1 (Garza 2024) — established the zero-shot forecasting API as a product-shaped object, with probabilistic output as a headline feature.

## Delta from parents
Where TimesNet computed the dominant periods with an FFT and then handed the folded representation to a learned attention-and-convolution stack, TinyCast keeps the computed-periodicity step and removes attention altogether, because at 146K parameters the attention path buys less than the parameters it costs. Where Chronos and TimeGPT-1 answered the zero-shot question by scaling a pretrained sequence model until it generalizes, TinyCast answers it by making the seasonality prior explicit and free, so the learned capacity is spent only on the residual structure. The claim being tested is a specific one about the size-accuracy frontier: the paper argues that most of what a mid-sized zero-shot forecaster learns is seasonality it did not need to learn, and it supports that with a model 28 times smaller than the nearest neural competitor on Chronos-ZS. The probabilistic output is the part that separates it from the usual small-model story, since the small end of the leaderboard is otherwise point forecasts only.

## Why Leo should care
This is time-series, Leo's primary area, and it is the one result today that changes a deployment constraint rather than a benchmark number. A 731 KB INT8 forecaster running end to end on a Cortex-M7 puts probabilistic forecasting inside the wearable itself, which removes the upload-then-infer round trip that most bio-sensing pipelines assume. The imported method is WaveNet-style dilated causal convolution, which was already available in time series, so nothing new is being ported from CV or NLP here; what is new is the argument that computed seasonality substitutes for learned capacity. The transferable question for bio-sensing is whether the same substitution holds for PPG and accelerometer signals, where the dominant periods are cardiac and gait rather than daily and weekly, and where the spectral detector would need to survive motion artifact.
