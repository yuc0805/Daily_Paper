# The Embedder's Dilemma: LLMs Are Better, but at What Cost?

**Arxiv:** 2608.12875 | **Date:** 2026-08-23 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
A cost-controlled comparison of ten LLMs across six families against 26 dedicated embedding models from 118M to 14B parameters, on 37 tasks covering classification, semantic textual similarity, clustering, pair classification and retrieval. In aggregate the two paradigms tie: the best LLM scores 77.6 and the best embedding model 77.2. The strengths split by task type, with LLMs ahead on reasoning-heavy retrieval, embedding models ahead on classification, and the two level on clustering, STS and pair classification. The cost gap is the result: up to 1,431x more expensive for an LLM at comparable quality (USD 154 against USD 0.11 per benchmark pass), and 2.5x to 736x slower token throughput on the same GPU for the open LLMs tested. Reasoning tokens are 28 to 81% of LLM inference cost, and cutting the reasoning budget preserves or improves retrieval quality for most models.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [WQRB4TUT] BERT (Devlin 2019) — established the pretrained bidirectional encoder as the standard source of text representations.
- [ZT8Y3ABF] GPT-3 (Brown 2020) — established the decoder-only generative model as a general-purpose interface that can be asked to do representation tasks too.

## Delta from parents
Where BERT and GPT-3 established the two model families, neither answered the practical question of which one to point at a representation task once inference cost is counted. This paper holds the task suite and the hardware fixed and measures the trade directly, and the answer is a division of labour rather than a winner: use embedding models for similarity, classification and clustering, and reserve LLMs for retrieval that requires reasoning. The reasoning-token finding is the most actionable piece, since it says a large share of LLM embedding cost buys nothing on retrieval and can be turned down.

## Why Leo should care
This is an infrastructure decision rather than a research result, and the numbers are specific enough to act on. Any pipeline that uses an LLM to embed text for clustering or classification, which includes most literature-triage and note-organisation setups, is paying up to three orders of magnitude more than needed for no measured benefit. The Pareto frontier holds only the leading embedding models plus one LLM, so the default choice for non-retrieval work is clear.
