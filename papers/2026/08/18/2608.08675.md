# SCALER: Efficient Test-Time Scaling for LLM-based Time Series Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2608.08675 | **Date:** 2026-08-18 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
SCALER splits LLM-based forecasting into two stages. A small non-LLM forecaster first predicts a coarse future shape at low resolution, and a pretrained LLM then refines that shape into a full-resolution forecast through a fixed number of multi-scale residual passes. Because the LLM is handed an explicit shape target rather than a long natural-language description of the series, it processes far fewer tokens per refinement step, and because the refinement schedule is fixed, the method does not need a reward model to select among sampled trajectories. The authors report gains over strong baselines on long-term, short-term, and zero-shot benchmarks, with average inference roughly 7 times faster than standard test-time-scaling forecasters. The paper is in the KDD 2026 proceedings.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [MKICLA63] Time-LLM (Jin 2024) — established that a frozen LLM can forecast by reprogramming the series into text-like prompt tokens.
- [6QQNUTL7] GPT4TS (Zhou 2024) — showed a frozen pretrained transformer generalises across time-series tasks with only light input and output adaptation.
- [W7RPRTCH] TTT for Abstract Reasoning (Akyurek 2024) — established that spending extra compute at inference, rather than at training, recovers large accuracy gains.
- [VSCNJG5J] ChatTS (Xie 2025) — graphify id xie2025_chatts, community 4, established LLM-native handling of multivariate series through description prompts.

## Delta from parents
Where Time-LLM and GPT4TS spend the LLM budget on a single forward pass that maps a prompted series directly to a forecast, SCALER spends it on iterative refinement of a shape that a cheap model has already committed to, because a single pass gives the model no place to correct itself and iterative refinement without a shape anchor drifts. Where TTT for Abstract Reasoning scales inference compute by adapting weights per instance, SCALER scales it by adding resolution levels, which costs no gradient steps at test time. Where ChatTS carries series semantics in long description prompts, SCALER carries them in a numeric coarse-shape vector, which is what cuts the token count and produces the 7x speedup. The result is that the cost of test-time scaling for forecasting is decoupled from prompt length, which was the term that dominated earlier LLM forecasters.

## Why Leo should care
This imports the test-time-scaling idea from LLM reasoning into time-series forecasting, and it does so in the specific form that was still open: coarse-to-fine refinement with a fixed step budget rather than sample-and-rerank. That particular transfer should now be treated as closed off. The two things it leaves open are worth noting. The coarse forecaster is univariate in shape, so nothing in the method constrains cross-variable structure, which is exactly the gap CvLoss and the Forecast Collapse paper are attacking from the other side. And the method has not been tried on physiological waveforms, where the coarse shape is a much weaker summary than it is for load or traffic series.
