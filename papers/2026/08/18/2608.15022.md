# Gathered, Not Admitted: How Attention Brings a Latent Variable into Verbalizable Form

**Arxiv:** 2608.15022 | **Date:** 2026-08-18 | **Area:** interpretable-ml

## What it does
The paper tests the common claim that a language model has a workspace with a gate deciding which internal quantities become reportable. Using Jacobian lenses on open-weight models and a benchmark whose five arms share an identical context, the author finds no gate at the position the account predicts. One shared linear probe decodes the latent variable from every arm, including the arm that never needs it, at 6.4 to 9.0 times its selection-corrected floor, so demand changes visibility rather than presence. Separating patch depth from readout depth localises the mechanism to attention-mediated gathering in a mid-depth window, at least 17 times above anywhere shallower, with no tested MLP output contributing positively inside it. The window sits at the same fractional depth in a 64-layer hybrid and a 62-layer dense model from a different family, and an arm with no downstream use of the variable concentrates sevenfold less, so the effect is demand-specific.

## Delta from parents
Where the original transformer paper introduced attention as a routing mechanism and left its functional role to later work, this paper measures the routing directly and reports that transport of a latent variable into readable form is carried by attention within a bounded depth band, with MLPs contributing nothing positive there. Where SHAP assigns credit to inputs under an additive model and treats the readout as a proxy for use, this paper shows the readout is not calibrated for that purpose: three components move the readout to within 12 percent of each other while differing 7.4 times in their effect on the answer. That is a warning about a class of interpretability measurement, not about one method. The paper also crosses the graphify boundary between community 2, transformer architecture, and community 6, interpretability and fairness, which are separate clusters in Leo's library with little linking them.

## Why Leo should care
The negative result is the useful part: an interpretability score that moves does not license a claim that the model used the feature, and this holds for the attribution family Leo's interpretable-ml collection is built around. The mid-depth window finding also holds across two model families at matched fractional depth, which makes it a candidate hypothesis to test on time-series foundation models, where nobody has asked at what depth a series-level latent such as a period or a regime label becomes readable.
