# Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO

**Arxiv:** 2608.27351 | **Date:** 2026-08-29 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper studies evolution strategies as a post-training method for reasoning rather than as a memory-saving substitute for gradient-based reinforcement learning. It shows theoretically that verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon diversity across the evolution-strategies population raises Pass@K, and empirically that evolution strategies improve Pass@1 while avoiding the entropy collapse that group relative policy optimisation exhibits. It also reports that although the whole model drifts substantially in parameter space, the task gains come from a sparse subset of large-magnitude updates, and that held-out evaluation shows no catastrophic forgetting. A sequential GRPO-then-ES schedule combines the Pass@1 strength of one with the Pass@K strength of the other.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)

- [KQTIJC4U] PPO (Schulman 2017) — the policy-gradient ancestor whose ratio-clipping structure GRPO inherits, and whose entropy behaviour is the object of comparison.
- [U4ZPM5DN] Latent-GRPO (Deng 2026) — a recent variant that modifies where the GRPO objective acts, but keeps the same gradient estimator.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 and its descendants estimate a gradient from token-level log-probability ratios within a group of rollouts, evolution strategies perturb parameters directly and never form that gradient, so the entropy-collapse mechanism that concentrates a GRPO policy onto one solution path has no analogue. The paper's contribution is to say why this matters in terms Leo can use: population diversity, measured through the verifier, is what buys Pass@K, and Pass@K is what determines whether a downstream search or verifier has anything to work with. The functional-sparsity result is the second useful piece — large parameter movement is not the same as widespread functional change, which weakens the usual argument that gradient-free methods must be destructive at scale.

## Why Leo should care
The Pass@1 against Pass@K distinction is the same tension as calibration against coverage in a diagnostic model: a system tuned to be right most often is not the system you want when a human or a verifier will review several candidates. The finding that a gradient-free method preserves coverage where a gradient-based one collapses it is worth carrying into any setting with a cheap verifier and an expensive label, which describes several bio-sensing tasks. The memory profile is also relevant for small-model work on limited hardware.
