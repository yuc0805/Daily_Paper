# The Impact of Temporal Context Length and Encoding Strategies on Self-Supervised ECG Representation Learning

**Arxiv:** 2608.12695 | **Date:** 2026-08-29 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
A controlled ablation on Icentia11k single-lead ECG that varies only two things while holding the Transformer backbone and training protocol fixed: the input horizon (16 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes, 10 minutes) and the front end (continuous convolutional patch embeddings against fixed vector-quantised tokens). Representations are scored by downstream abnormal rhythm detection and by patient-level retrieval across sessions. Longer context wins, with the 5- and 10-minute models strongest, and continuous patch embeddings beat discretised tokens at every horizon. Accepted at EMBC 2026.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos established quantisation as the way to make a time series look like text so that a language-model objective applies, this paper measures what that conversion costs and finds it costs clinically relevant waveform detail at every horizon tested. Where PatchTST argued for patching mainly on efficiency and forecasting accuracy grounds, this paper shows the same choice governs whether a representation is stable across sessions for the same patient, which is a different and stricter test. HeAR and most physiological encoders work on windows of seconds because that is the unit a clinician reads; the retrieval result here says the individual-specific structure lives at the minute scale, so the window inherited from clinical practice is the wrong unit for pretraining. The contribution is a clean ablation rather than a new architecture, which is what makes it usable as a design prior.

## Why Leo should care
This is a direct answer to a question that recurs in wearable pretraining: how long a window, and tokens or continuous patches. The answer here is minutes rather than seconds, and continuous rather than quantised, with patient-level retrieval as the metric that separates them. The imported method is masked-autoencoder pretraining from vision, applied here at varying context — that transfer is already well travelled and should be treated as closed off. What remains open is whether the same context finding holds for signals with slower physiology than ECG, such as PPG-derived heart rate variability or accelerometry.
