# PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?

**Arxiv:** 2608.27345 | **Date:** 2026-08-29 | **Area:** world-model-rl

## What it does
The paper argues that a video generator used as a world model should reproduce the distribution of valid outcomes under a given initial state and action, not merely one plausible outcome, and formalises that requirement as probabilistic alignment. PAWBench supplies 50 scenarios in which a physical process can unfold in more than one legitimate way, and PAWEval converts repeated rollouts into an empirical distribution over outcomes. Across eleven current systems, none consistently matches the reference probabilities while still covering the range of valid behaviours. The authors then test whether prompting, initial noise sampling or training can reshape the predictive distribution.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [GX7WR7KA] DDPM (Ho 2020, graphify id `ho2020_ddpm`, community 3) — made the generator an explicit sampler from a learned distribution, which is the property this benchmark holds it to.
- [YJ9TK993] DiT (Peebles 2023, graphify id `peebles2023_dit`, community 3) — scaled that sampler with a Transformer backbone, the family under test here.
- [P92FLHJ2] World Model Eval (Vafa 2024) — argued that a model can score well on next-step prediction while holding an incoherent internal model of the world.

## Delta from parents
Where Vafa's evaluation work showed that sequence accuracy does not certify a coherent world model, this paper makes the same point at the level of the output distribution rather than the internal state, and supplies a measurable criterion for it. Where DDPM and DiT are trained and reported as samplers, they are in practice judged one sample at a time, so the sampling property that the training objective actually optimises is never checked; PAWBench checks it. The cross-area bridge in the library between `zhu2020_cyclegan` and `ho2020_ddpm` marked the move from deterministic image translation to distributional generation. PAWBench pushes that bridge further, into asking whether the distribution the model learned is the right one, which is a question the original move made possible but did not answer.

## Why Leo should care
This is the video-generation form of the failure documented yesterday in time-series foundation models: a model that is individually plausible on every sample and wrong about the spread. Forecast collapse showed flat predictions from per-series calibration; PAWBench shows collapsed or misplaced outcome distributions from per-sample evaluation. The shared diagnosis is that the evaluation unit does not match the quantity the downstream decision needs. For bio-sensing that matters because most clinically useful predictions are distributional rather than point-valued, and the outcome-level protocol here is a template worth copying.
