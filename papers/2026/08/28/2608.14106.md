# Forecast Collapse in Time-Series Foundation Models

**Arxiv:** 2608.14106 | **Date:** 2026-08-28 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
Documents a failure mode the authors call forecast collapse: when time-series foundation models forecast hourly returns for 1,000 US equities, the predictions go nearly flat and rank the series poorly, as measured by cross-sectional correlation. The same models do not collapse when forecasting trading volume on the identical panel, which isolates target predictability rather than architecture as the driver. The study covers time-series foundation models, twelve deep-learning forecasters and 97 public benchmark configurations, and separates two causes: low predictability caps the amplitude of any calibrated point forecast, and per-series training objectives never identify cross-series structure. The proposed fix, CalibRank, mixes calibration and ranking in one objective. On the Finance1K panel it nearly triples cross-sectional correlation while holding forecast amplitude close to the target, and it improves correlation on every model tested. Optimising cross-sectional correlation alone, by contrast, inflates amplitude by more than an order of magnitude.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos and Moirai established that a single pretrained model can forecast unseen series without fitting them, and where the Zhang survey catalogued that progress using per-series error metrics, this paper shows that the metric itself hides a failure. A model can be well calibrated on every series individually and still be useless for any decision that requires comparing series to each other, because squared-error training on low-predictability targets drives the conditional mean toward a constant. The contribution is diagnostic before it is methodological: the collapse is tied to target predictability, not to model family, so scaling the pretraining corpus does not remove it. CalibRank then changes the objective rather than the architecture, which means the finding transfers to any panel forecasting setup that ranks entities.

## Why Leo should care
Every wearable and bio-sensing study that pools many subjects is a panel problem, and the decisions built on those forecasts are almost always cross-subject rather than within-subject: who is deteriorating, who to alert, who to enrol. Heart rate variability, step counts and sleep-stage transitions are low-predictability targets in exactly the sense this paper defines, so the collapse mechanism should apply directly. The imported method here is a ranking objective from information retrieval, brought into time-series foundation model training; that import is now demonstrated for finance panels but not for physiological panels, which leaves the cross-subject version open. The immediate practical step is smaller: report cross-sectional correlation alongside per-series error in any multi-subject forecasting evaluation, because per-series metrics alone cannot detect this failure.
