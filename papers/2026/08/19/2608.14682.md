# RouteTS: Frequency-Time Routing for Time Series Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2608.14682 | **Date:** 2026-08-19 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
RouteTS partitions the amplitude spectrum of an input window and sends each part to the domain where it is best represented. Dominant high-amplitude frequencies stay in the frequency domain and are forecast by a complex-valued linear predictor, which preserves phase and avoids periodic misalignment over long horizons. Residual spectral energy is inverted back to the time domain and modelled by a lightweight MLP, so transient spikes are never smoothed by a Fourier basis. The routing decision comes from the data rather than a learned gate, keeping the model small.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [TVHWGWAV] FEDformer (2022) — established frequency-domain forecasting with a random subset of Fourier modes for tractable cost.
- [4QRHV2JG] DLinear (2023) — showed a single linear layer in the time domain beats most transformers on long-horizon forecasting.
- [HLTA8MDK] TimesNet (2023) — made periodicity explicit by reshaping the series into 2D blocks indexed by discovered periods.

## Delta from parents
Where FEDformer moved forecasting into the frequency domain wholesale and controlled cost by sampling Fourier modes at random, RouteTS keeps the frequency domain only for components where it is the correct basis and returns everything else to time, because a random or fixed mode subset cannot distinguish a component that is low-amplitude because it is unimportant from one that is low-amplitude because its energy is a spread-out transient. Where DLinear demonstrated that shallow time-domain models suffice, RouteTS retains that lesson in both branches but argues the single linear layer was being asked to fit periodic and transient structure simultaneously, and splits the two jobs. Where TimesNet extracted periodicity through a 2D reshape, RouteTS reads the same information off the amplitude spectrum directly, which is why it remains cheap.

## Why Leo should care
The routing mechanism is separable from forecasting. Wearable and bio-signal streams have exactly the structure RouteTS targets: strong circadian or cardiac periodicity plus sparse transient events that carry the clinical signal, and the standard objection to frequency-domain bio-signal models is that they smooth those transients away. Amplitude routing is cheap to attach as a front end to an existing encoder. On the porting question, RouteTS is not an import from CV or NLP — amplitude-based spectral routing is native signal processing — so this idea is still upstream of the time-series area rather than already absorbed into it.
