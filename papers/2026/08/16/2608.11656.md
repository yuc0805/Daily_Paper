# Continuous-Latent Predictive Modeling with Semantic Alignment for EEG-Language Foundation Models (BLPM)

**Arxiv:** 2608.11656 | **Date:** 2026-08-16 | **Area:** time-series (also llm-health, self-supervised)

## What it does
BLPM is an EEG foundation model that drops both of the two standard pretraining objectives for neural signals. Instead of reconstructing masked raw EEG or predicting discrete tokens, a Continuous EEG Latent Predictive (CELP) encoder is trained to predict latent targets, so the training signal lives in representation space rather than in the waveform. A second module, Multi-Query Semantic Decomposition (MQSD), pulls task-relevant components out of those representations and places them in a shared latent space with text embeddings, which is what makes the encoder usable as a front end for a language model. The authors report consistent generalisation across several EEG decoding benchmarks and frame continuous latent semantic prediction as the pretraining paradigm for EEG-language models.

- [ELYUE3NF] Sensor2Text (Chen 2024, graphify `chen2024_sensor2text`, community 4) — established that a sensor encoder can be aligned to natural language and queried conversationally, for wearable activity streams.
- [6INGKIJV] How Mask Matters: Masked Autoencoders Theory (Zhang 2023, graphify `zhang2023_mae`, community 1) — the theoretical account of what masked reconstruction does and does not recover, which is exactly the objective BLPM argues against for EEG.
- [HDPXBLAP] SensorLM (2025, time-series) — closest non-graphify neighbour: sensor-language pretraining at scale, but on wearable rather than neural signals.

## Delta from parents
Where Zhang 2023 explained why masked reconstruction learns whatever structure the mask forces it to learn, and Gu 2025 observed empirically that this leaves biosignal encoders optimising pixel-level fidelity instead of clinical semantics, BLPM replaces the objective outright: the prediction target is a latent representation of the signal, not the signal. That is the joint-embedding predictive move, imported from vision, applied to EEG. Where Sensor2Text aligned a sensor encoder to text after the encoder had already been trained on its own objective, BLPM makes the alignment part of the representation itself through MQSD, decomposing the EEG representation into multiple semantic queries rather than producing one pooled vector to match against a caption. The reason this matters technically is heterogeneity: EEG decoding tasks differ in channel count, montage, and label space, and a single pooled embedding forces all of them through one bottleneck, whereas multi-query decomposition lets different tasks read different components of the same representation. The cost is that latent-target prediction has no reconstruction check, so representation collapse is a live risk and the paper does not report an ablation that rules it out.

## Why Leo should care
This sits directly on the bio-sensing side of Leo's primary area and it closes a transfer he may have been considering. The CV method being imported is joint-embedding predictive architecture, the I-JEPA and V-JEPA line, and the target modality is EEG. After this paper, "apply JEPA-style latent prediction to neural signals" is no longer an open port; what remains open is the same move for the peripheral wearable modalities in graphify community 5, namely PPG, GSR, and IMU, where the label scarcity argument is stronger and the semantic vocabulary is thinner. The MQSD idea is the transferable part: multi-query decomposition against a text space is not EEG-specific and would apply to any sensing modality where one recording supports several unrelated downstream labels, which is the normal case in wearable health.
