# TailBooster: A Dual-Layer Generative Framework for Extreme Value Augmentation with Operational Validity Enforcement

**Arxiv:** 2608.11951 | **Date:** 2026-08-16 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
TailBooster generates synthetic records for rare extreme events in air transport, such as severe arrival delays and abnormal air times, where the historical record is too thin to train on. A statistical layer isolates extremes by interquartile range and feeds only that tail-concentrated subset to a dedicated Tabular Variational Autoencoder, so the generator is not competing with the bulk of the distribution. A second, learned layer applies autoencoder-based cleaning that discards synthetic records violating an operational envelope inferred from historical data, for example a short air time paired with a long flight distance. Evaluated on US flight records across diversity, statistical similarity, fidelity, operational validity, and utility, training six regression algorithms on the framework's records cut mean absolute error by 47 to 49 percent on extreme air time and 29 to 57 percent on extreme arrival delay relative to conventional synthetic data.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [RILRU5H6] TimeGrad (2021) — established deep generative modelling of time-series values with a probabilistic objective, the line whose tail under-representation this paper measures.
- [GG7GSYUJ] Generative Adversarial Networks (Goodfellow 2014) — the generative family that conventional tabular synthesis, the paper's baseline, draws from.
- [DZ2UYXUL] l-DAE (2024) — latent denoising autoencoding, the mechanism the second layer reuses as a validity filter rather than as a generator.

## Delta from parents
Where TimeGrad and the conventional tabular generators fit the whole distribution and are therefore dominated by the bulk, TailBooster splits the problem: a statistical extremes filter first, then a generator trained only on the tail, which converts an under-representation problem into a smaller and better-posed density estimation problem. Where l-DAE used autoencoder denoising to improve generation, this paper turns the same machinery around and uses reconstruction error as a rejection criterion, discarding samples the learned envelope calls infeasible. The reason both steps are needed is that a tail-focused generator will happily produce records that are statistically extreme and physically impossible, and neither the statistical layer nor the generator can catch that on its own.

## Why Leo should care
This is a time-series paper, but the mechanism is domain-general and directly relevant to bio-sensing, where the events worth predicting are rare by construction: arrhythmia episodes, falls, seizure onset, hypoglycaemic events. The CV or tabular method being imported is VAE-based synthesis plus autoencoder anomaly detection, and after this paper that combination is largely ported for tabular extreme-event augmentation. What is not ported is the same two-layer design for continuous multivariate physiological waveforms, where the "operational envelope" would be physiological plausibility rather than an operational constraint, and where the validity check would need to run on a window rather than a row. The evaluation is on flight records only, so transfer to physiological signals is a hypothesis, not a result.
