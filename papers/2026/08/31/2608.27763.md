# Fast Weight Attention for Continual Learning

**Arxiv:** 2608.27763 | **Date:** 2026-08-31 | **Area:** mamba

## What it does
Treats the state update of a recurrent fast-weight memory or a selective state-space model as an online learning rule, then asks what that rule is actually regressing on. Under read-after-write autoregressive semantics the paper argues the correctly aligned training pair at step t is the prefix-aligned pair (phi(k_{t-1}), v_t), not the same-step pair (phi(k_t), v_t) used by most existing linear-attention and SSM designs. From this it derives normalized first-order update families: Falcon-1 (a scalar normalized least-mean-squares update), Falcon-2 (a per-column extension), Falcon-3 (a sliding-window mini-batch update), plus inner-product variants Falcon-1A/2A/3A. It supplies recurrent, masked-parallel and chunk-parallel forms with positive-decay renormalization for numerical stability. Reported outcome is competitive language modeling with improved length extrapolation on variable-digit addition.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [PHB9VRVM] Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani 2017, graphify `vaswani2017_transformer`, Community 2, 12 edges) — the anchor node for Community 2; established softmax attention over a growing key-value cache as the default sequence operator.
- [JPKDWV2Q] Mamba-2 (Dao 2024) — established the structured state-space duality showing that selective SSMs and a restricted form of linear attention are the same computation, which is the equivalence this paper reinterprets as an online learning rule.
- [XNI34DQX] Mamba (Gu 2024) — introduced input-dependent selective gating for the fixed-size recurrent state, the concrete update rule this paper reframes and then replaces.

## Delta from parents
Where Vaswani stored every past token in a growing key-value cache and let softmax attention retrieve from it, and where Mamba compressed that history into a fixed-size state governed by a learned selective gate, this paper asks what objective that compression is implicitly optimizing and finds that the standard same-step association optimizes the wrong one under autoregressive semantics. Where Mamba-2 proved SSMs and linear attention are dual as matrix computations, this paper takes the dual view one level further and treats the shared update as a step of online regression, which makes the design space explicit rather than architectural: temporal alignment, plasticity, forgetting and bounded rehearsal become four separable knobs instead of one entangled gate. The Falcon family is then the set of normalized updates obtained by picking a loss and a batch size for that online problem, with normalization playing the role that step-size control plays in adaptive filtering. The claimed benefit is length extrapolation rather than in-distribution perplexity, which is consistent with the diagnosis: a misaligned target hurts most when the sequence runs longer than anything seen in training.

## Why Leo should care
Leo's library carries fourteen Mamba-family papers and nearly all of them apply an existing SSM block to a new modality — HARMamba to wearable accelerometry, Audio Mamba to spectrograms, ClinicalMamba to notes, Bi-Mamba+ to forecasting. None of them touch the update rule itself. This paper changes the rule, which means every one of those application papers has an untested variant sitting behind it. The transfer that matters for Leo is that biosignal streams are the setting where the diagnosis should bite hardest: PPG and EDA are long, non-stationary and amplitude-drifting, so a normalized least-mean-squares state update with explicit step-size control is a better match to the signal than a fixed learned decay gate. No CV or NLP method is imported here; the import runs the other way, from adaptive filtering into sequence models, which is a direction the wearable-sensing literature has not used.
