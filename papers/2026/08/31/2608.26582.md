# J-Zero: Unified Challenger--Solver--Judge Co-Evolution from Zero Data

**Arxiv:** 2608.26582 | **Date:** 2026-08-31 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Extends self-evolving language model training to domains where answers cannot be checked automatically. A Challenger generates progressively harder tasks and a Solver learns to answer them, the standard adversarial pair. The addition is a Judge that co-adapts alongside them, trained on preference pairs whose ordering is known from how each response was produced rather than from the Judge's own scores: the Solver's answer is taken as better than the Challenger's, and a decomposed-then-recombined answer is taken as better than a one-shot answer. Reported gains are 4.2 points on average in verifiable domains and 8.0 in unverifiable ones, with improvement continuing through at least ten iterations where baselines degrade after two.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [Z5IWHZAE] DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI 2025, graphify `deepseek2025_r1`, Community 0) — showed reasoning can be trained by reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards without supervised reasoning traces.
- [HBLPTRMY] Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023, graphify `wei2023_cot`, Community 0, 7 edges) — the anchor node for reasoning; established decomposition into intermediate steps as the mechanism that improves answers.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 obtained reasoning gains by reinforcement learning against a verifier, and therefore stopped wherever a verifier does not exist, this paper replaces the verifier with a Judge and then confronts the obvious failure: a frozen Judge caps self-evolution, and a Judge trained on its own scores drifts. The fix is to source the Judge's supervision from the generation procedure instead of from any model's opinion, so the preference ordering is known before either response is read. Where Chain-of-Thought used decomposition to improve a single answer at inference time, J-Zero repurposes decomposition as a supervision signal, treating "decomposed then recombined" as a construction that reliably produces the better member of a preference pair. That is the actual trick, and it converts a prompting technique into a source of free labels. The ten-iteration stability claim is the number to check, since two-iteration collapse is what the baselines here are known for.

## Why Leo should care
The listed cross-area bridge between DeepSeek-R1 and Chain-of-Thought in Leo's library already connects reasoning-by-prompting to reasoning-by-reinforcement-learning. J-Zero pushes that bridge further, into the case where no reward is available at all. For Leo's health work the unverifiable setting is the normal one: there is rarely a checkable ground truth for whether a physiological explanation or a counseling response is good, which is the gap CounselBench in his library measures rather than fixes. The procedure-derived preference pairs are the portable idea, and the question worth asking is whether an equivalent construction exists for physiological reasoning, where a claim grounded in a retrieved signal segment could stand in for the decomposed answer.
