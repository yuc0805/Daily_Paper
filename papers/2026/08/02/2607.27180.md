# HumanCLAW: Can Vision-Language Models Act Through a Body?

**Arxiv:** 2607.27180 | **Date:** 2026-08-02 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
An evaluation framework that separates a vision-language model's action decisions from low-level motor control. At each step a harnessed off-the-shelf VLM issues an atomic skill command, translated into a sub-second chunk of continuous full-body motion with real physics (gravity, collisions), so balance and motor errors are factored out and only the model's moment-to-moment action choices are measured. The benchmark HumanCLAW-Bench has 1,218 long-horizon egocentric find-navigate-interact episodes across 41 indoor scenes. Nine state-of-the-art VLMs are tested; none solves it, and the best reaches only 16.8% success. The gap is embodied self-awareness, not perception.

## Delta from parents
Where Flamingo benchmarks a VLM on passive image and video question answering, HumanCLAW puts the same class of model in a control loop and measures whether its choices move a physical body toward a goal, because passive perception scores say nothing about whether the model can act on what it sees. Where the Agent AI survey described the multimodal-interaction agenda in prose, HumanCLAW supplies a physics-grounded benchmark and a specific negative result: current VLMs lose track of their own body.

## Why Leo should care
Secondary-area watch item (multimodal). The framing — decouple decision quality from execution, then measure only decision quality — is a clean evaluation pattern reusable in any agent setting where success mixes a good choice with a working actuator. The 16.8% best-model result marks how far current VLMs are from acting through a body.
