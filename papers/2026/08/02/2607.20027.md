# Zero-Shot Heart Rate Variability Forecasting from Consumer Wearables Using Time Series Foundation Models

**Arxiv:** 2607.20027 | **Date:** 2026-08-02 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
Tests three time-series foundation models (TimesFM, Chronos, MOIRAI) on short-term heart-rate-variability forecasting from consumer-wearable data, with no fine-tuning, against classical baselines. Introduces a variability-preserving imputation method (linear interpolation plus locally adaptive stochastic noise) to handle the gaps left by artifact removal. Evaluated on 49 healthy participants with forecast horizons up to 2 hours.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos and MOIRAI established general time-series foundation models evaluated mostly on clean, broad benchmarks, this paper runs them unchanged on fragmented, artifact-rich consumer-wearable HRV and shows they still beat classical baselines (MASE 0.81 to 0.87), because the variability-preserving imputation keeps the input inside the distribution the pretrained models expect. Where TS-Agent treats time series as a reasoning problem for an LLM, this paper stays with pure forecasting and asks only whether the pretrained forecaster generalizes to a new signal type. The contribution is empirical (a zero-shot baseline on real wearable HRV) plus the imputation trick, not a new architecture.

## Why Leo should care
This is a small, clean, directly relevant result in the primary area: it quantifies how far generic time-series foundation models get on messy wearable physiological signals with no adaptation, and it isolates data fragmentation rather than model capacity as the engineering problem. Imported method: none new — it reuses existing time-series foundation models, so "generic TSFM, zero-shot, on a wearable physiological signal" is now a filled cell in the Community 4 map and is low-hanging fruit going forward. The reusable piece is the variability-preserving imputation for any wearable pipeline that must survive artifact removal.
