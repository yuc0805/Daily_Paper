# Knowing When to Quit: Diagnosing and Training LLMs to Abort Futile Reasoning

**Arxiv:** 2607.29211 | **Date:** 2026-08-14 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
On tasks beyond their capability, reasoning models keep generating long derivations that look valid and are not. The paper names this futile reasoning, characterizes it across difficulty levels, and reports two systematic patterns: capability overreach, meaning models attempt what they cannot do, and miscalibration between measured capability and actual behavior. The dominant failure mode is specious reasoning, where output is superficially well-formed but contains subtle errors, and its share grows with task difficulty. The proposed fix is CaRL, capability-aligned reinforcement learning, which shapes rewards to make refusal preferable to futile reasoning and adds hindsight refusal augmentation that turns observed failures into refusal supervision. The reported result is a large drop in futile reasoning with task performance preserved across difficulties. Accepted to ACL 2026 Findings.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 used outcome reward to make chains longer, this paper uses reward shaping to make them stop, because outcome-only reward gives no signal distinguishing "worked hard and failed" from "correctly declined". Where Chain-of-Thought treated the reasoning trace as always beneficial, this paper measures the regime where the trace is actively harmful: it burns compute and it produces plausible wrong answers that mislead the reader. This is a graphify cross-area bridge case. DeepSeek-R1 already bridged reasoning-via-RL and prompting-era chain-of-thought in the library, and this paper pushes that bridge into calibration territory, connecting reasoning to the abstention and hallucination line of work.

## Why Leo should care
The abstract reports the effect qualitatively rather than with headline numbers, so the size of the reduction has to come from the paper itself. The idea is portable to any setting where a model is asked to interpret a signal it may not be able to interpret, which describes most clinical and physiological question answering, and where a confident wrong reading costs more than a refusal. Worth reading the reward shaping section specifically, since the hindsight augmentation trick is cheap to reimplement.
