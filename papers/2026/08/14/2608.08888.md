# Full-bandwidth transformer

**Arxiv:** 2608.08888 | **Date:** 2026-08-14 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
An autoregressive transformer normally passes only one thing from decoding step t to step t+1: the sampled token. The top-layer hidden state, which holds everything the model computed but did not verbalize, is thrown away. The full-bandwidth transformer widens this channel by fusing the previous top-layer hidden state with the sampled token embedding through a gated linear unit and feeding the result back as the next input. The architecture, KV cache, and language-modeling objective are otherwise unchanged. Training uses a scheduled multi-pass objective that introduces latent feedback late in pretraining and mixes in a small fraction of deeper feedback passes for stability, so parallel teacher forcing is preserved.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [3TLRP8U5] PonderLM (2026) — established that a model can be trained by self-supervision to run extra forward passes inside a single token step, feeding back a distribution-weighted sum of token embeddings instead of a sampled token.
- [EMXEJYHV] Latent Reasoning Survey (2025) — framed the general argument that natural-language chain-of-thought is an expressive bottleneck and that multi-step inference can run in continuous hidden state.
- [PHB9VRVM] Attention Is All You Need (2017) — the base architecture whose horizontal (across-token) and vertical (across-depth) compute axes this paper takes as its starting frame.

## Delta from parents
Where PonderLM spends extra forward passes *within* one token step and feeds back a weighted embedding mixture, this paper feeds back the *top-layer hidden state* *across* token steps, because the goal is not extra thinking time at one position but a persistent latent channel that carries non-verbalized computation forward with a renewed depth budget. Where the Latent Reasoning Survey argued that latent computation should replace verbal chains, this paper keeps the verbal chain intact and adds latent state alongside it, so the language-modeling objective, the KV cache, and standard decoding all survive unchanged. The practical consequence is that the change is a drop-in modification rather than a new training paradigm: 1B-parameter models trained to 400B tokens match or approach standard transformers trained on roughly 1.5 times more tokens, with negligible per-token decoding overhead, and produce shorter reasoning traces at equal or better accuracy.

## Why Leo should care
This is a data-efficiency result dressed as an architecture result. The claim is that under a fixed high-quality data budget you can buy capability by spending training compute on a wider recurrent channel instead of on more tokens. For time-series work the transfer is direct and, as far as I can tell, unported: autoregressive forecasters discard the same top-layer state between steps, and forecasting is precisely a setting where the useful information at step t+1 is continuous rather than discrete, so a latent feedback channel should lose less than a sampled-token channel does. The authors are candid in the paper discussion that they lack scaling-law evidence, so treat the 1.5x figure as a single-scale observation rather than a trend.
