# Forecast Collapse in Time-Series Foundation Models

**Arxiv:** 2608.14106 | **Date:** 2026-08-17 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
The paper documents a failure mode it calls forecast collapse: when time-series foundation models forecast hourly returns for 1,000 US equities, the predictions become nearly flat and rank the series poorly, measured by cross-sectional correlation. The same models do not collapse when the target is trading volume under identical settings. The authors test the effect across several TSFMs, twelve deep-learning forecasters, and 97 public benchmark configurations, and trace it to two causes: low target predictability caps the amplitude of a calibrated point forecast, and per-series training objectives never identify cross-series structure. They propose CalibRank, an objective that mixes calibration and ranking terms.

## Delta from parents
Where Chronos and Moirai established that one pretrained model can forecast many series and reported gains on per-series error, this paper shows that per-series error is the wrong lens when the downstream decision depends on the ordering of series. Where PatchTST argued that channel independence improves accuracy and stability, this paper shows the same independence removes the signal needed to rank series against each other, because nothing in the loss ties one series to another at a given timestamp. The paper measures the tradeoff directly: optimising squared error flattens predictions, optimising cross-sectional correlation improves ranking but inflates forecast amplitude by more than an order of magnitude. CalibRank sits between the two, nearly tripling cross-sectional correlation on the Finance1K dataset while keeping amplitude near the target, and improving correlation on every model tested.

## Why Leo should care
The finding is about evaluation, not finance. Any setting where the useful output is a comparison across units rather than a per-unit number inherits the same blind spot, and multi-subject physiological forecasting is such a setting: ranking which subjects are trending toward a bad state matters more than each subject's absolute predicted heart rate. Bio-signals also have the low-predictability property that drives collapse, so a wearable TSFM scoring well on per-series MAE may be producing near-constant forecasts that are useless for triage. This is a CV/NLP-independent, time-series-native result — no imported method — so it opens rather than closes an opportunity: the cross-sectional evaluation protocol has not been carried into wearable or clinical benchmarks yet.
