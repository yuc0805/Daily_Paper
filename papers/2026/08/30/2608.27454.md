# WikiSkill: Compiling Agent Experience into Persistent Knowledge for Skill Evolution

**Arxiv:** 2608.27454 | **Date:** 2026-08-30 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
Separates three things that prior skill-discovery systems mix together: raw execution experience, accumulated knowledge, and executable skills. Experience is continuously consolidated into a persistent wiki, and skill updates read from the wiki rather than from raw optimization histories. Across several benchmarks and models it beats state-of-the-art skill-evolution methods and the no-skill baseline in most model-benchmark settings, and ablations confirm the persistent knowledge layer is what produces the gain. Two secondary results: skill evolution complements model scaling, with small models plus evolved skills sometimes beating substantially larger models without them, and skills transfer across model families, with externally evolved skills sometimes beating self-evolved ones. From Google Research and Virginia Tech.

## Delta from parents
Where Huang's survey catalogued memory mechanisms without running the comparison, WikiSkill runs the ablation and reports that a consolidated knowledge layer is the component that matters. Where Chain-of-Experience kept a flat, append-only log of prior attempts and re-prompted on it inside one task, WikiSkill compiles that log into structured knowledge that outlives the task and can be handed to a different model, because a flat trace does not survive transfer. Where ToolkenGPT put capability into learned tool embeddings tied to one model's weights, WikiSkill keeps capability in text that any model can read, which is what makes the cross-family transfer result possible.
