# CardioState-JEPA: Delay-Aware Cross-Modal Learning of a Shared Cardiac Representation

**Arxiv:** 2608.12944 | **Date:** 2026-08-30 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
Trains one shared Transformer encoder across ECG, PPG and PCG by predicting masked latent cardiac states rather than reconstructing waveforms, so the three sensors supervise each other instead of each getting its own model. A learned delay aligner matches signals at the corresponding point in the cardiac cycle to absorb the offsets between electrical, mechanical and hemodynamic events. Training is staged: within-modality structure from abundant unimodal data first, then scarce paired data used only to align modalities in latent cardiac time. As a frozen encoder across 25 downstream tasks it improves average PPG classification by 8.2 AUROC points, PCG murmur detection by 18.8, and ECG classification by 15.5 over the best self-supervised signal baseline.

## Delta from parents
Where Gu's survey catalogued biosignal foundation models and found the field split one encoder per modality, this paper treats that split as removable and shows the modalities can mutually supervise a single encoder. Where HeAR masked and reconstructed health audio in input space for one modality, CardioState-JEPA predicts in latent space across three, because reconstruction targets would force the encoder to retain sensor-specific waveform detail that does not transfer between electrical, optical and acoustic sensing. Where Zhang's analysis argued that masking helps because it moves the target onto semantic structure, the delay aligner is the biosignal statement of the same argument: the semantics are cardiac-cycle phase, so alignment is on physiological time rather than sample index. The staged curriculum is the practical concession — synchronized three-sensor recordings are scarce, so paired data is spent only on alignment and not on representation learning.

## Why Leo should care
This is the strongest version so far of the claim that multi-sensor physiological data should train one model rather than several, and it lands in his primary area. Two components are directly reusable outside cardiac work: the delay aligner generalizes to any wearable stack where accelerometer, PPG and skin temperature respond to the same event at different lags, and the unimodal-then-paired curriculum is a recipe for the common case of plenty of single-sensor data and very little synchronized data. On porting: JEPA-style latent prediction has now arrived in cardiac biosignals, so that import from vision is closed. The delay-aware variant applied outside cardiac timing is not.
