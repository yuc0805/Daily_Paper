# Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement

**Arxiv:** 2608.31046 | **Date:** 2026-09-01 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper audits on-policy distillation (OPD), where a teacher model scores token by token the trajectories a student generated, and asks whether the teacher's supervision is what produces the student's gains. Measuring supervision quality during training, the authors find substantial noise in the teacher's scores, and that the noise gets worse as the teacher gets larger. Removing the noisy supervision entirely leaves student performance essentially unchanged, and replacing the teacher's per-token advantages with a single fixed negative advantage matches full OPD. From this they build On-Policy Self-Adaptation (OPSA), which uses entropy-adaptive negative advantages and no teacher at all, improving Avg@32 on AIME24 by 35.41 points over base Qwen3-1.7B (a 263 percent relative gain) and beating OPD by 16.77 points.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 accepted sparse outcome rewards as the price of avoiding a teacher, and where on-policy distillation was proposed as the dense alternative that buys token-level signal from a stronger model, this paper shows the dense signal is largely uninformative and that the useful part is a policy-internal quantity. The teacher scores off-policy trajectories, so its per-token judgements are unreliable in a way that grows with teacher capacity, and the student turns out not to depend on them. What actually drives improvement is suppression of low log-probability tokens, which the student can identify from its own entropy. So the parent line assumed the distinction was sparse-versus-dense supervision, and this paper reframes it as teacher-versus-self: the density helped, the teacher did not.

## Why Leo should care
The result is a negative control that most distillation papers do not run, and the experimental design is portable. If a method claims a teacher provides information, the check is whether replacing the teacher's signal with a constant or with a self-computed statistic reproduces the result. That test applies directly to knowledge distillation in time-series and bio-sensing, where large physiological foundation models are routinely distilled into small on-device students and the assumed value of the teacher is rarely isolated. The entropy-adaptive advantage itself is a candidate transfer: a wearable model's own predictive entropy over signal patches is available without any teacher forward pass, which matters when the deployment constraint is compute on the device.
