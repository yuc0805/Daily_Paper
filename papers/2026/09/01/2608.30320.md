# On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture: Evaluation, Efficiency, and Training Stability

**Arxiv:** 2608.30320 | **Date:** 2026-09-01 | **Area:** mamba

## What it does
This is an architecture report for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 125B total parameters, 6B activated per token, and a further 51B parameters of n-gram embedding tables kept in host memory rather than on the accelerator. Token mixing alternates Gated DeltaNet layers with global attention at a ratio of three to one, and at continued-pretraining time the full-attention layers are swapped for a sparse attention variant that scores context at micro-block granularity through a compressed indexer. The residual stream is widened to four branches read through an elementwise gate. Against the 397B-A17B predecessor the model leads on eight of fourteen pre-training benchmarks and trails on the rest by at most 2.6 points, at one third the activated parameters, one third the training tokens, and roughly one ninth the training FLOPs.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [PHB9VRVM] Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani 2017) — anchor node for llm, 12 edges; the full-attention layer this architecture keeps at one in four.
- [JPKDWV2Q] Mamba-2 (Dao 2024) — the state-space line whose linear-time recurrence Gated DeltaNet extends with a delta rule.
- [XNI34DQX] Mamba (Gu 2024) — established selective state-space models as the attention substitute for long sequences.

## Delta from parents
The Mamba line argued that a selective recurrence could replace attention outright, and the architecture literature since has mostly been about how much attention must be kept. This report answers with a measured ratio rather than an argument: one full-attention layer in four, with the rest linear-recurrent, and it then replaces even those with block-sparse attention after pretraining. The second departure from the parents is that capacity is moved off the backbone entirely into n-gram embedding tables prefetched from host memory, which is a different axis from both the attention-versus-recurrence question and the mixture-of-experts question. The paper also reports something the parent papers did not evaluate: the interaction between architecture choice and optimal hyperparameters, finding that this design together with the Muon optimizer shifts the optimal learning rate and batch size upward and makes batch-size warmup unnecessary.

## Why Leo should care
The honest finding here is that loss and downstream accuracy come apart, shown concretely by enlarging the n-gram vocabulary: loss falls monotonically while downstream accuracy saturates. Anyone selecting architecture variants on validation loss alone should note that. The hybrid ratio is also the number worth carrying into time-series work, since long physiological sequences are the setting where the recurrence-versus-attention tradeoff bites hardest, and HARMamba and Bi-Mamba+ in the library both take the all-recurrence position without measuring how much attention would have been worth keeping.
