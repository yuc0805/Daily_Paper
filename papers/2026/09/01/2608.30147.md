# CAST: Critique-Aware Supervision for Training Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Calling Agents

**Arxiv:** 2608.30147 | **Date:** 2026-09-01 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
In long-horizon interactive settings, a single wrong tool call can be irreversible, and the failure may show up only across repeated trials rather than in any single run. CAST converts sparse end-of-task outcomes into action-level supervision by analysing agent trajectories and synthesising structured rationales that explain why a given action was valid or invalid under partial observability. Those rationales train a critique model, and the critique model then generates training data for the policy. Fine-tuning Qwen3-family models on dynamic tool-calling benchmarks, CAST exceeds GPT-OSS-120B by more than 10 percent pass^4 on Retail tasks and gains a further 9 percent on Telehealth, which is held out of domain.

## Delta from parents
LATS catches a bad action by searching alternatives and scoring them at inference, which costs compute at every deployment and leaves the base policy unchanged. CAST moves that judgement into training: the critique is learned once from synthesised rationales and then used to reshape the policy, so the deployed agent is cheaper and the improvement transfers to a domain it was not trained on. Where ToolkenGPT made the tool call a token the model could emit, this work supplies the missing part of that setup, which is a per-token-level credit signal for whether emitting it was right, given that the only ground truth available is whether the whole episode succeeded. The reported metric matters too: pass^4 measures success across four independent trials rather than one, so it scores reliability rather than best-case capability.

## Why Leo should care
This paper extends ToolkenGPT, which the graphify prior already lists as a cross-area bridge to PyVision, so the tool-using-agent bridge in the library now runs further toward training-time reliability rather than inference-time scaffolding. The out-of-domain result is on Telehealth, which is the closest an agent-reliability paper has come to the health setting Leo works in, and the pass^k framing is the right evaluation for any clinical decision-support agent where an occasional catastrophic action is the binding constraint rather than average accuracy.
