# Dream-RSI: Recursive Self-Improvement through Evolving Worlds

**Arxiv:** 2609.14858 | **Date:** 2026-09-15 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
Adds a lightweight orchestration layer above an unchanged coding agent so that the exploration strategy becomes explicit and programmable. Accumulated discovery history is reused as a replay simulator over the realized search space, and the system evaluates candidate exploration policies inside that simulator instead of running expensive online rollouts. The improved policy is redeployed online, which expands the simulator pool and closes the loop. Evaluated on algorithm engineering, mathematical optimization, and GPU kernel engineering, where it matches or improves discovery quality at lower discovery cost in several settings.

## Delta from parents
Where LATS pays full online cost for every node it expands, this paper treats the tree it has already built as a dataset and evaluates new exploration policies against it offline, because the expensive part of agentic discovery is the evaluation, not the proposal. That is the offline reinforcement learning move from Levine 2020, applied one level up: the object being improved is the search policy over a meta-space, not an action policy over an environment. Voloshin's results are the reason to be cautious about it, since off-policy estimates degrade exactly when the evaluated policy drifts away from the logged behaviour, and a replay simulator built from past discovery trees covers only the realized search space. The paper's own framing is honest about this, calling the simulator a replay over what was actually visited.
