## Tier A — deep read (~20 min)

### Declarative Attention: Language Models Can Control Their Own Attention
Ho, Ahmad, Koh, Yun, Schuster, dos Santos (KAIST AI) — arXiv:2609.02737 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02737

**Problem.** A language model spends most of its attention mass on a small fraction of the context, but it still reads the entire KV cache at every decoding step in order to find the few tokens that matter. On a one-million-token conversation, every generated token forces a full scan. The standard fix is to pre-select candidate tokens with a cheap proxy scorer, but that scorer itself runs over all N positions, so the per-step cost stays O(N). The authors ask whether the scoring can be removed entirely on the grounds that the model already knows where it needs to look.

**Method.** Declarative Attention is a protocol, not a new architecture. During its chain of thought the model emits declarations that partition generation into three modes: `<global>` attends to the full context, `<focus>` attends to one named region, and `<local>` attends only to recent output. The inference engine parses these declarations the same way it parses tool calls, and skips the corresponding parts of the KV cache read. The model is not retrained; the declarations are elicited zero-shot from off-the-shelf checkpoints.

**Result.** Across 15 long-context tasks, Declarative Attention reduces total attended tokens during decoding by 52.0 percent on Gemma-4-31B and 31.1 percent on Qwen-3.6-27B. Accuracy falls by 1.27 and 2.75 percentage points respectively. The accuracy gap narrows as model scale grows, which is the direction one wants: the mechanism gets cheaper to use as the models it runs on get larger.

**Limitations.** Everything reported is zero-shot on two model families, so it is unclear how much of the gain comes from the protocol versus from those particular checkpoints already having good positional self-knowledge. The declarations are generated by the same model whose attention they control, so a model that misjudges relevance will silently skip the region containing the answer, and the paper does not report a per-task worst case or a fallback trigger. The savings are measured in attended tokens, not wall-clock latency, and the two do not convert one-to-one on real serving stacks. Training-based versions are left to future work.

**Why it matters to Leo.** This is the clearest recent example of a model's own reasoning trace being used as a control signal for its own compute, rather than as an output to be read. For long physiological streams the same structure applies directly: a week of wearable data is exactly the setting where the relevant window is small, known in advance, and expensive to scan. See the Gap Watch section below.

**How this builds on what you know:** The direct parents are Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani 2017, `PHB9VRVM`, graphify `vaswani2017_transformer`, Community 2 — Transformer and SSM Architectures), Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023, `HBLPTRMY`, graphify `wei2023_cot`, Community 0 — LLM Agents and Reasoning), and DeepSeek-V3 (2024, `2JCKA7GI`, graphify `deepseek2024_v3`, Community 0). Where Vaswani established that every query attends to every key and left the cost as a fixed architectural property, this paper makes the attention span a decision the model takes at run time, because the cost is no longer acceptable at million-token context. Where Wei used the chain of thought as a place to write intermediate answers, this paper uses it as a place to write control instructions the serving engine executes, which turns the reasoning trace from output into a scheduling signal. Where DeepSeek-V3 cut KV cache cost by compressing the representation of every key, this paper leaves the representation alone and skips reading most of it.

This paper sits on the same seam as your two existing bridges into architecture work: it extends `vaswani2017_transformer`, the highest-degree node in Community 2, using a mechanism from `wei2023_cot` in Community 0. Those two communities are joined in your library only through the `deepseek2025_r1` to `wei2023_cot` bridge, which crosses reasoning-via-RL and reasoning-via-prompting inside Community 0. Declarative Attention makes a genuinely new crossing — reasoning trace as architectural control — and pushes the bridge in the direction of inference-time compute allocation rather than answer quality.

## Tier B — TLDR (~10 min total)

### 1. Repo-To-Skill / DisCo: Distilling GitHub Repositories Into AI4AI Skills
Chen, Hu, Qian, Liu, Wei et al. (BAAI) — arXiv:2609.02749 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02749

The authors name a missing layer in autonomous research agents, which they call operational knowledge: the know-how that separates knowing a method from making it work. This knowledge exists in repositories and papers but in a form written for human readers and far too large to load into a context window during a task. DisCo distills it in two modes, one task-agnostic pass over widely used repositories and one task-oriented pass that produces what a specific job needs, and the task-agnostic pass over 1,000 repositories yields the AREX-Skill Library of more than 5,000 verified skills across 20 areas and 178 capability families. Holding the GPT-5.5 backbone, the research harness, and the execution budget fixed, the skill-equipped agent scores 134.3 percent higher on MLE-bench, 34.4 percent higher on PaperBench, 9.2 percent higher on FrontierCS, and 14.0 percent higher on PassNet than the same agent without skills.

**How this builds on what you know:** The parents are ToolkenGPT (Hao 2024, `6RDHVVA2`, graphify `hao2024_toolkengpt`, Community 0), Rethinking Memory Mechanisms of Foundation Agents (Huang 2026, `BDY3HUCV`, graphify `huang2026_memory`, Community 0), and the Agent AI Survey (Durante 2024, `Z9WZPMNU`, graphify `durante2024_agentai`, Community 0). Where ToolkenGPT compressed a tool's interface into a learned token so the model could call it, DisCo compresses a repository's operating procedure into a text artifact the model reads, because the bottleneck it targets is not calling the tool but configuring it correctly. Where the Memory Mechanisms survey framed agent memory as accumulated episodic trace, this paper treats memory as a curated and verified library built offline and shared across tasks, which removes the dependency on the agent having already seen a similar problem.

This paper extends `hao2024_toolkengpt`, which in your library already bridges to `zhao2025_pyvision` under the tool-using-agents relation. The new work pushes that bridge away from tool invocation and toward tool operation — the skill is not a callable but a procedure, so the crossing now covers how a tool is used, not just that it is used.

### 2. SolarWM: Open Data and Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Video World Models
Huang, Fang, Qian, Kong, Zhao et al. (CUHK-Shenzhen) — arXiv:2609.02886 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886

Training video world models across heterogeneous sources is hard because datasets differ in temporal scale, camera geometry, visual quality, motion statistics, and captioning style, while video generators differ in representation and objective, so naive mixing produces inconsistent supervision and results that cannot be compared. SolarWM separates the two problems with a reconfigurable data engine and a backbone-native adaptation framework. The engine converts 1.43 million canonical clips from 10 datasets into one frame-aligned contract covering observations, metric camera geometry, captions, quality metadata, selection decisions, and provenance, and it decouples source processing from mixture construction so the mixture becomes an experimental variable rather than a preprocessing accident. Four models from 5B to 33B parameters are instantiated on Wan2.2, LTX-2.5, and MiniMax-H3 under a shared three-stage recipe, and the resulting causal models support real-time rollouts from minutes to hours after training on only 5-second sequences, with data, weights, and pipeline released.

**How this builds on what you know:** The parents are World Models (Ha and Schmidhuber 2018, `938DAXZT`), Navigation World Models (Bar 2024, `JQ7JD8AR`), and DINO-WM (Zhou 2025, `W44RSJJI`), all in your world-model-rl area. Where Ha and Schmidhuber learned a compact latent dynamics model in one environment and treated the data as given, this paper treats the data contract itself as the object of study, because the current failure is not learning dynamics but comparing dynamics models trained on incommensurable corpora. Where DINO-WM fixed the representation to a pretrained visual encoder in order to make world models comparable across tasks, SolarWM fixes the data and lets the representation vary across four backbones, which is the complementary control.

### 3. EarlyEval: Cheaper Agent Evaluation via Early Outcome Prediction
Shi, Sun, Dong, Wan, Lo, Gu (SJTU, SMU) — arXiv:2609.02783 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02783

One pass of a frontier model over an agentic benchmark costs hundreds to thousands of dollars, and that price is paid again on every development cycle. Prior work on benchmark distillation reduces how many tasks are run but leaves the cost of each retained task untouched. EarlyEval attacks the within-task axis on the observation that an agent's final outcome is usually evident from its intermediate behavior well before the run completes: two LightGBM classifiers, one for success and one for failure, read behavioral, textual, and reference-solution features and halt the run when either crosses a calibrated confidence threshold. On SWE-bench Verified, TerminalBench, and Toolathlon this removes 13 to 26 percent of agent steps and up to 44.1 percent of input tokens and 29.4 percent of output tokens at 89 to 97 percent prediction accuracy, moving per-agent resolve rates by only one to two percentage points.

**How this builds on what you know:** The closest parents are Evaluating World Models (Vafa 2024, `P92FLHJ2`) and the Agent AI Survey (Durante 2024, `Z9WZPMNU`, graphify `durante2024_agentai`, Community 0). Where Vafa asked whether an evaluation measures the capability it claims to measure, this paper takes the measurement as correct and asks how much of the run can be discarded before the estimate degrades, which is a sampling question rather than a validity question. Where the Agent AI survey catalogued agent capabilities without costing their measurement, this paper makes evaluation cost a first-class quantity with a stated accuracy-versus-savings trade-off. Note the structural similarity to today's Tier A paper: both spend a small predictor to avoid a large amount of work, one over context positions and one over trajectory steps.

## Tier D — Time-series / bio-sensing Gap Watch

No time-series or bio-sensing paper landed in today's candidate pool, so this section reports unported opportunities from the day's top CV and NLP work rather than classifying new arrivals.

**Unported opportunity 1 — Declarative Attention for long physiological streams (arXiv:2609.02737).** Transfer hypothesis: a model reading a month of wearable data could declare the window it needs — "the three nights before the reported event" — and skip the rest of the KV cache, giving retrieval over long sensor histories at a cost that does not grow with history length. This is not covered by Community 4 (Time Series and LLM Integration): TS-Agent (`I2CIT4I7`) reaches the series through operator calls and ChatTS and Sensor2Text (`ELYUE3NF`) reach it through a trained alignment bridge, and none of the three changes what the model reads inside its own context. The declaration protocol is a third integration point, and the physiological setting is unusually favourable because the relevant window is often nameable in advance from the clinical question.

**Unported opportunity 2 — Repository-distilled skills for sensor preprocessing (arXiv:2609.02749).** Transfer hypothesis: the operational knowledge gap DisCo names is larger in bio-sensing than in the ML benchmarks it was evaluated on, because filter cutoffs, artifact rejection thresholds, resampling conventions, and normalization choices are the difference between a working pipeline and a broken one, and they live in repository code rather than in method sections. An AREX-style distillation over the standard wearable and physiological toolboxes would produce exactly the artifact a time-series research agent currently lacks. Community 5 (Wearable Sensing and Behavior) contains no analogue; SensorLM (`HDPXBLAP`) and MindScape (`nepal2024_mindscape`) both assume the preprocessing is already correct.
