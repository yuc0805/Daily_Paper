# EarlyEval: Cheaper Agent Evaluation via Early Outcome Prediction

**Arxiv:** 2609.02783 | **Date:** 2026-09-03 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
Reduces the cost of evaluating LLM agents by halting runs whose outcome is already predictable. Two LightGBM classifiers, one for success and one for failure, read behavioral, textual and reference-solution features at each step and stop the run when either crosses a calibrated confidence threshold, at negligible per-step overhead. On SWE-bench Verified, TerminalBench and Toolathlon this removes 13 to 26 percent of agent steps and up to 44.1 percent of input tokens and 29.4 percent of output tokens at 89 to 97 percent prediction accuracy, perturbing per-agent resolve rates by one to two percentage points on average.

## Delta from parents
Where Vafa questioned the validity of the measurement, this paper accepts the measurement as correct and asks how much of each run can be discarded before the estimate degrades, which turns an evaluation-design question into a sequential-sampling question with an explicit accuracy-versus-savings trade-off. Where the Agent AI survey enumerated agent capabilities without costing their measurement, this paper makes evaluation cost a first-class quantity and reports the exchange rate. The structural move is the same one used by today's Tier A paper on the context axis: spend a cheap predictor to avoid a large amount of work, here over trajectory steps rather than over context positions.

## Why Leo should care
Practical rather than conceptual: any agent-style pipeline Leo builds over time-series operators inherits the same evaluation cost curve, and a calibrated early-stopping classifier over intermediate behavior is cheap to add. Worth noting that the features are behavioral and textual, so the method does not depend on the task being code.
