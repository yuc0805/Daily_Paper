# TempCloze: Can Video-LLMs Identify the Missing Middle?

**Arxiv:** 2609.01515 | **Date:** 2026-09-13 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
A video cloze benchmark: given the beginning and ending clips, a model picks the true missing middle from four candidates. The 1,521 filtered videos come from seven sources and are mostly long-take and egocentric. Distractors are drawn from the same source along three axes — Semantic (what should happen), Alignment (when it should occur), Progression (how it unfolds) — with shared scenes and objects so appearance cannot decide the answer. Across 10 proprietary and 21 open-source video language models, Alignment is the bottleneck.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [RYGRHCG3] Video Action Survey (graphify `zhu2020_video_action`, Community 9 "Video Action Recognition") — the temporal-modelling evaluation tradition this benchmark departs from.
- [SC8KWYVK] Flamingo (Alayrac 2022, multi-modal area) — interleaved visual-language conditioning, the architecture class under test.
- [4N5WXKPI] BLIP-2 (Li 2023, multi-modal area) — the frozen-encoder-plus-language-model recipe most evaluated models inherit.

## Delta from parents
Where the Video Action Survey line evaluated temporal understanding with closed label sets and no language mediation, TempCloze keeps the language interface but removes the linguistic shortcut, because the shortcut and not the temporal reasoning is what recent benchmark scores were measuring. Where Flamingo and BLIP-2 are scored on question answering whose options carry wording cues and answer correlations, TempCloze makes the candidates themselves video, so option text cannot leak the answer. The decomposition is the useful part: separating what, when, and how shows models handle semantic plausibility and local progression but fail on temporal placement, which is a sharper finding than an aggregate score drop.

## Why Leo should care
The cloze-with-same-source-distractors construction ports to physiological time series with almost no modification: mask a window, generate candidate middles from the same subject and session, and score whether a model places it correctly in time. Graphify Community 4 (Time Series and LLM Integration) holds ChatTS, TS-Agent, Sensor2Text, and the LLM-for-time-series survey, and all of them are evaluated through language-mediated question answering, which is exactly the format this paper shows is shortcut-prone. The Alignment finding also predicts what would break: temporal placement, which is the capability a sleep-staging or activity-transition model actually needs. This import is open.
