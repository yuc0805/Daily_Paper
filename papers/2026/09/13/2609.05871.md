# Where Does the Sound Go? Tracing Acoustic Information Loss in Audio-Conditioned LLMs

**Arxiv:** 2609.05871 | **Date:** 2026-09-13 | **Area:** audio

## What it does
Audio-conditioned language models underuse prosody, emotion, and non-speech sound. This paper asks at which stage that information disappears, probing after the encoder, after the projector, inside the language model, and at the language-model head. It also replaces the ASR-trained Whisper frontend with reconstruction codecs (EnCodec, DAC-VAE, WavTokenizer) inside a shared pipeline to test the common assumption that the frontend is the culprit. Accepted to EMNLP 2026 Findings.

## Delta from parents
Where LTU built the encoder-projector-language-model pipeline and measured what it can answer, this paper instruments the same pipeline and measures what it still contains, because a capability failure and a representation failure call for different fixes. Where HeAR showed that acoustic detail survives encoding, this work shows that surviving encoding is not the binding constraint: the discriminative audio information is still present in the last hidden layer, so the loss is at the readout, not the frontend. Where CLAP and the codec family are usually compared on what they preserve in principle, the paper compares them inside one fixed downstream pipeline and finds that swapping Whisper for reconstruction codecs does not close the gap. The reported fix is narrow and telling: fine-tuning only the letter rows of the output head recovers most of the multiple-choice accuracy gap.

## Why Leo should care
This is the diagnostic protocol Leo's area has not run. Time-series and biosignal work currently debates tokenizer design — patching, quantization, text rendering — on the assumption that the encoder throws information away. The paper gives a cheap layerwise test for that assumption, and in the audio case the assumption was wrong. Running the same probe on ChatTS or Sensor2Text would say whether the field is optimizing the wrong stage. Note that the result is on multiple-choice readout, so it is specific to how the answer is produced, and generalizing it to open-ended generation needs its own check.
