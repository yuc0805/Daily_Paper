# X-AuT: Progressive Audio-Encoder Compression for Speech LLMs with Cross-Scale Distillation

**Arxiv:** 2609.11412 | **Date:** 2026-09-11 | **Area:** audio

## What it does
X-AuT removes layers from the audio encoder of a speech language model without the deletion and premature end-of-sequence errors that whole-block pruning normally causes. Layer combinations are chosen by short behavioral probes rather than by weight-magnitude heuristics, and the pruned encoder is repaired by representation alignment, cross-scale distillation from a larger teacher, scheduled student-policy supervision, and LoRA finetuning while the language backbone stays frozen. Compressing Qwen3-ASR-0.6B from 18 to 16 encoder layers reduced macro-average error from 5.61 to 5.27 percent across ten Chinese-English benchmarks; the 14-layer model reached 5.75 percent with 20.7 percent fewer audio-tower parameters, against 6.73 percent for direct rather than progressive pruning.

## Delta from parents
Where Audio Mamba attacked encoder cost by replacing attention with a state-space model and retraining from scratch, X-AuT keeps the trained transformer encoder and removes depth from it, because a pretrained encoder attached to a frozen language model is an asset that a from-scratch architecture swap throws away. The interesting number is not the parameter saving but that 18 to 16 layers improved accuracy, which says the original encoder depth was not on the right side of the capacity-noise trade-off for this decoder. Where HeAR established that a general health-acoustic encoder transfers across tasks and left inference cost alone, this supplies the compression recipe that such an encoder would need on a device. The progressive-versus-direct result, 5.75 against 6.73 percent at the same final depth, is the methodological point: the path through intermediate depths carries information that the endpoint does not.

## Why Leo should care
The frozen-backbone, prune-the-encoder, distill-across-scales pattern is directly reusable for any signal encoder feeding a language model, which is the shape of most wearable-plus-LLM systems. The behavioral-probe selection step matters more than the pruning: choosing which layers to drop by measuring downstream behavior rather than weight statistics is a method that has not been applied to physiological encoders, where the equivalent probe would test whether a pruned encoder still separates clinically distinct signal patterns. Note also the caveat in the abstract, which reports single-run results and says the effect varies across benchmarks; treat the 0.34-point improvement as suggestive rather than established.
