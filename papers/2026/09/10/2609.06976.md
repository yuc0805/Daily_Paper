# HealthLoopQA: A Context-Aware Question Answering Benchmark for Interpreting Wearable Monitoring Data in Diabetes Care

**Arxiv:** 2609.06976 | **Date:** 2026-09-10 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
Evaluates language model reasoning over continuous diabetes monitoring data using a simulated hybrid closed-loop insulin delivery testbed rather than a collected dataset. The testbed generates physiological and therapeutic traces under varied patient activity schedules and injects 17 fault scenarios covering device failures and security compromises, so the benchmark can ask questions that no observational record would contain. Question templates cover process mining, anomaly detection and predictive reasoning, and are graded by reasoning depth from descriptive statistics through causal and inferential reasoning. Each item carries both a numerical answer and a textual rationale, so quantitative accuracy and reasoning fidelity are scored separately.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [2BGP9QJL] Verifiable Physiological Reasoning (Wang 2026) — established that a physiological answer should be checked against an explicit derivation, not accepted from the model's output alone.
- [7RHTW5L3] Health-LLM (Kim 2024) — the short-horizon wearable evaluation setup this paper argues is too narrow.
- [I2CIT4I7] TS-Agent (Liu 2025) — the tool-using, evidence-logging pattern that this benchmark's process-mining and anomaly-detection questions are designed to reward.

## Delta from parents
Where Health-LLM scored short-horizon classification and statistical summary over wearable features, this paper asks about long-horizon patterns, therapeutic context and system failures, because a deployed closed-loop device fails in ways that a fixed observational dataset never records. Where Wang 2026 required a verifiable derivation for a single physiological claim, this paper carries that requirement into a benchmark by attaching a rationale to every item, so a model that reaches the right number by the wrong route is separable from one that does not. Where TS-Agent demonstrated that an agent with operators outperforms direct reading on time-series reasoning, this benchmark supplies the fault-injection setting where that advantage should be largest and can be measured. The design choice worth noting is simulation: it buys controlled faults and ground-truth causal structure at the price of realism, which is the exact opposite trade from WearableQA on the same day.

## Why Leo should care
Read this next to WearableQA rather than on its own, because the pair defines the two ends of a design space Leo will have to choose between. WearableQA keeps real distributions and cannot supply ground-truth causes; HealthLoopQA supplies ground-truth causes and faults and cannot supply real distributions. A method evaluated on only one of them is easy to dismiss. The fault-scenario axis is also the part of the design that transfers outside diabetes: any closed-loop or continuously-monitored bio-sensing setting has device failures that look like physiological events, and no existing benchmark in Leo's area scores whether a model can tell those apart. On the ported question, controlled fault injection is a standard systems-evaluation method now imported into health time-series QA, and that import is closed off for diabetes but open for every other continuous-monitoring modality.
