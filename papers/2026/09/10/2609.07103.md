# Revisiting Complete Reasoning Traces for Post-Training

**Arxiv:** 2609.07103 | **Date:** 2026-09-10 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Asks whether supervised fine-tuning on complete reasoning trajectories is actually what makes post-training work, and reports that it is largely not. A pilot study finds that full trajectories give only limited benefit over partial ones, and that partial trajectories remain effective under heavy truncation. Attention-based analysis and controlled token-removal studies then show that intermediate tokens contribute little to final reasoning quality. The reading the authors offer is that a model given the endpoints of a trajectory can infer coherent intermediate steps from its own internal knowledge, so much of the middle of a long chain is redundant supervision rather than necessary supervision.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [HBLPTRMY] Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023) — established that intermediate reasoning steps improve final answers, which is the premise this paper tests as a training signal rather than a prompting device.
- [Z5IWHZAE] DeepSeek-R1 (2025) — the reference point for long-trajectory post-training, where reasoning length grows as a byproduct of reward optimization.
- [FFWLYL3J] CODI (Shen 2025) — already argued that the reasoning chain can be compressed into latent form without losing the answer.

## Delta from parents
Where Wei 2023 showed that producing intermediate steps at inference time helps, this paper separates that from the claim that consuming intermediate steps at training time helps, and finds the second claim much weaker than the field has assumed. Where DeepSeek-R1 established long chains as the shape of a strong reasoner, this paper argues the length is partly incidental to what the model learns, because truncated supervision reaches comparable quality. Where CODI compressed the chain by moving it into a latent space and still had to represent it, this paper reports that a good deal of the chain can simply be deleted from the training data, which is a stronger and cheaper claim if it holds. The evidence is attention analysis plus controlled token removal, which is a correlational instrument attached to a causal intervention; the intervention is the part that carries weight.

## Why Leo should care
This is a cost argument before it is a science argument. If partial trajectories under heavy truncation match full ones, the data budget for reasoning post-training drops by a large factor, and long-trace collection stops being the bottleneck for anyone working outside a frontier lab. For Leo the transferable question is whether the same holds when the intermediate steps are numerical operator calls rather than natural-language sentences: in a time-series agent the middle of the trace is where the evidence is gathered, so a model cannot infer it from internal knowledge the way it can infer an arithmetic step. That contrast is a testable claim and a small paper. The result is also a caution about reading trace length as evidence of reasoning quality in any benchmark Leo builds against.
