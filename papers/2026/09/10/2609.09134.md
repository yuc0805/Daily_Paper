# Co-Evolving Harnesses and Models: On-Policy Correction Helps Weaker Models Catch Up Where Imitation Fails

**Arxiv:** 2609.09134 | **Date:** 2026-09-10 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
Studies what happens when an agent harness, meaning the system prompt, tool set, execution hooks and context management around a model, is evolved for a weak model and the weak model is then fine-tuned on a stronger expert's trajectories under that same harness. Across seven enterprise agent tasks the combination regresses on all seven, by 4 to 30 points for Qwen3-Coder and Gemma 4, even though the identical fine-tuning helps when the harness has not been evolved. The diagnosis is a fit problem: imitation transfers knowledge and raises scaffold usage but installs the expert's planning strategy in a model that cannot execute it, and that strategy no longer matches a harness shaped around the weak model's native planning. The fix is on-policy expert correction, automated by a meta-level agent that locates the failing turn in the weak model's own rollout and has the expert rewrite only that turn, which keeps the planning style and recovers the gains from both sides.

## Delta from parents
Where ADaPT matched decomposition depth to executor competence inside a single run, this paper finds the same mismatch appearing across a training boundary, because supervised fine-tuning silently overwrites the executor's planning style while the harness stays fixed. Where LATS and the scaffold line treated harness improvement and weight improvement as independent additive gains, this paper shows they interact negatively once the harness has been evolved against the weak model, which is a result the additive assumption cannot produce. Where NeoHorse-1 logged full harness context and trained on it to good effect, this paper isolates the case that breaks: the trajectories there came from the model's own serving traffic, whereas here they come from a stronger expert, and the difference between on-policy and off-policy provenance is what flips the sign. The proposed correction is minimal-edit rather than full-trajectory, which is the same on-policy logic that appeared in the reverse-distillation work covered yesterday, arriving from the agent side instead of the RL side.

## Why Leo should care
The transferable finding is that a scaffold tuned to a model becomes a liability once that model's behavior is retrained, and this generalizes past agents to any pipeline where a hand-tuned preprocessing or prompting layer sits in front of a model that later gets fine-tuned. It is also a clean negative result with a stated mechanism and a fix, which is the shape of paper worth imitating. The convergence is worth noting on its own: two papers in two days, one from RL post-training and one from agent scaffolding, both concluding that supervision has to be delivered on the student's own rollouts rather than the teacher's.
