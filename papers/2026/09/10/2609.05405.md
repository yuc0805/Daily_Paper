# WearableQA: A Benchmark for Health Reasoning over Real-World Wearable Data

**Arxiv:** 2609.05405 | **Date:** 2026-09-10 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
Builds a question-answering benchmark on top of the actual longitudinal records of 200 real people, each carrying up to 500 days of daily wearable measurements alongside blood biomarkers and demographics, and asks whether a language model can reason over that record. The result is 4,084 ten-option multiple-choice questions sorted into 16 types along two axes: data reasoning versus health reasoning, which separates arithmetic over the series from physiological interpretation, and single-signal versus cross-signal reasoning, which separates reading one stream from combining several. Questions are generated by a dual-grounding procedure that requires each item to be supported both by a published physiological finding and by a statistically validated pattern present in the population data, so the answer key is not a model's opinion. Fourteen proprietary and open-source models score between 19.6 and 72.9 percent against a 10 percent chance floor, and most land below 60 percent.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [JX3X3KH5] LLMs are Few-Shot Health Learners (Liu 2023) — established that a language model can be prompted to read raw physiological numbers and produce health-relevant answers without task-specific training.
- [7RHTW5L3] Health-LLM (Kim 2024) — the first systematic evaluation of language models on wearable-derived health prediction, built from curated features rather than raw records.
- [ICD9EG8Q] GLOBEM (Xu 2023) — supplied the multi-year, multi-cohort behavior dataset design that makes longitudinal wearable evaluation possible at all.

## Delta from parents
Where Liu 2023 showed that a language model can answer health questions from physiological numbers presented in a clean prompt, this paper keeps the noise, the missingness and the between-person variability that a real device produces, because the earlier setup measured whether the capability exists rather than whether it survives contact with a deployed sensor. Where Health-LLM evaluated on engineered features and short windows, WearableQA evaluates on up to 500 days of a single person's own record, which changes the failure mode from "can the model map a feature vector to a label" to "can the model locate the relevant stretch of a person's history and compute over it". Where GLOBEM contributed data and a prediction task, this paper contributes a question schema on top of comparable data, and the schema is the substantive part: separating data reasoning from health reasoning makes it possible to say whether a model failed at arithmetic or at physiology, which a single accuracy number cannot. The dual-grounding construction is the other change, since it forces every question to be defensible twice, once from literature and once from the population statistics, rather than accepting whatever an annotator model produced. The headline number is the spread, 19.6 to 72.9 percent, and the fact that most systems sit below 60 percent on a task that is answerable from the data in front of them.

## Why Leo should care
This lands directly on Leo's working area and is the closest thing yet to a shared yardstick for the claim that language models can reason over wearable records, which is the claim the whole time-series-plus-LLM line depends on. The 16-type breakdown is useful independent of the leaderboard, because it gives a ready-made ablation axis: a method that improves cross-signal health reasoning while leaving single-signal data reasoning flat is doing something specific, and that is a cleaner result than a single benchmark delta. It also settles a question about what to build against, since the alternative until now was to evaluate on GLOBEM-style prediction and argue about whether the number transferred. On the ported versus unported question, this imports LLM benchmark construction methodology, specifically the multiple-choice-with-distractors format and the literature-grounded item validation used in medical QA, into wearables. That import is now closed off. What is not closed is the model side: nobody has published a method that moves the cross-signal health reasoning number, and the benchmark exists to score one.
