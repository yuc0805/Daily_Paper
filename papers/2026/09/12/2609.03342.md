# Gradients Know What Outcomes Don't: Unlocking Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning with Gradient-Aligned Rewards

**Arxiv:** 2609.03342 | **Date:** 2026-09-12 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Constructs a dense reward for reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards without training a process reward model and without step-level annotation. Truncated backpropagation through the output projection layer produces a compact gradient vector for each rollout, and the reward is the cosine similarity between that vector and an anchor gradient built from an expert solution already present in the training corpus. The authors prove the cosine decomposes multiplicatively into a prediction-error factor and an activation-pattern factor. Overhead is under 9 percent wall-clock relative to the base loop.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 accepted a flat reward across every correct trajectory and absorbed the cost in long, noisy traces, this paper keeps the same verifiable setup but adds an ordering within the correct set, taken from the policy's own gradients, because the gradient direction already carries the trajectory-quality information that the outcome bit discards. Where Latent-GRPO changes what the policy optimizes over, this paper changes what the policy is paid for, so the two are composable rather than competing. Relative to the process-reward-model line surveyed in Latent Reasoning Survey, the cost structure is the substantive difference: no second model, no step labels, and under 9 percent extra compute. This also extends the graphify cross-area bridge `deepseek2025_r1` to `wei2023_cot`, which already crossed reasoning-via-reinforcement-learning and reasoning-via-prompting; the new work pushes that bridge into supervision derived from expert text already sitting in the corpus.

## Why Leo should care
This is the cheapest dense-reward construction published so far, and the cost structure is what makes it portable out of mathematics. Any domain with paired data and written expert interpretation can supply the anchor: an ECG trace with a cardiologist's reading, an accelerometer window with a scored activity rationale, a polysomnography record with a sleep report. Time-series reasoning models currently train against forecast error or a binary classification hit, so no method in graphify Community 4 derives a dense reward from annotator prose. That import is open, and the required annotation is clinical text that already exists.
