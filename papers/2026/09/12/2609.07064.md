# SpatialBlock: Enhancing Spatial Intelligence in LVLMs via Synthetic Block-Stacking Problem

**Arxiv:** 2609.07064 | **Date:** 2026-09-12 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
Trains large vision-language models on synthetic block play rather than on annotated real scenes. SpatialBlock-15k contains 15,000 block-stacking problems covering 3D-to-2D projection, viewpoint transformation, and structural combination, with controlled colour modulation added so the model learns to anchor reasoning on task-relevant blocks instead of on global appearance. Two training strategies are released, one predicting the answer directly and one reasoning before answering, and both transfer to real-scene spatial benchmarks despite the synthetic and compact training set.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [4N5WXKPI] BLIP-2 (Li 2023, multi-modal and computer-vision areas, graphify Community 3 "Vision-Language and Generative") — established the frozen-encoder plus query-transformer recipe most current vision-language models still follow.
- [PDAMP7VF] Machine Mental Imagery (Yang 2025, reasoning area; also [NSI6PVD7] in multi-modal) — argued these models need an internal visual scratchpad to reason about structure rather than appearance.
- [B7F2Q998] ViT (Dosovitskiy 2021, multi-modal and computer-vision areas) — the patch-token encoder whose lack of explicit geometric inductive bias is the gap this paper trains around.

## Delta from parents
Where BLIP-2 aligned an image encoder to a language model on captions and web pairs, and Machine Mental Imagery proposed changing the model's internal representation, this paper changes the data instead: a synthetic curriculum of controlled geometric transformations, on the argument that spatial skill is a learnable curriculum rather than an architectural property. Relative to ViT, no inductive bias is added to the encoder; the geometry is taught through supervision. The claim that carries beyond the benchmark is that 15,000 synthetic items beat annotation-heavy real-scene supervision, which inverts the usual assumption that spatial question answering needs dense geometric labels from external perception modules.

## Why Leo should care
The transferable object is the cheap synthetic curriculum, not the block domain. If a controlled 15,000-item synthetic set can install a geometric skill that survives the move to real scenes, the same argument applies to sensor modalities where real labels are the bottleneck: synthetically generated signal morphologies with known ground-truth transformations, used to install invariances that self-supervised pretraining on unlabelled traces does not reliably produce. Note this is an import candidate rather than an established result for signals.
