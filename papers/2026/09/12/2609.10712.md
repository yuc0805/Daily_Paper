# An Open Recipe for IMO Gold: Training Nemotron for Olympiad Mathematics

**Arxiv:** 2609.10712 | **Date:** 2026-09-12 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Post-trains two specialist checkpoints from Nemotron 3 Ultra, one by supervised fine-tuning and one by reinforcement learning, then builds a test-time pipeline in which three checkpoints generate, verify, and refine natural-language proofs while a separate high-compute stage selects each final submission. The system uses no formal prover, no external tools, and no internet access, and it scored 30 out of 42 points at IMO 2026, above the gold-medal threshold. The release includes both post-trained checkpoints, the training data, the training and inference code, the submitted solutions, and Nemotron-IMO-Bench, a set of 200 novel olympiad-level problems.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [Z5IWHZAE] DeepSeek-R1 (graphify `deepseek2025_r1`, Community 0) — showed that reinforcement learning on verifiable answers produces long reasoning traces without process supervision.
- [BZKDNHD6] SPRINT (Biju 2025, LLM area) — restructured reasoning trajectories into planning rounds plus parallel execution, cutting sequential tokens by up to 39 percent.
- [W7RPRTCH] Test-Time Training for Abstract Reasoning (Akyurek 2024, test-time-training area) — established that extra compute at inference can substitute for extra capacity on reasoning tasks.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 put the compute into training a single policy and SPRINT put it into reorganizing one trajectory, this paper puts it into an outer loop over several specialist checkpoints, because verification and refinement buy more at fixed compute than a better single generator does. Where Test-Time Training for Abstract Reasoning adapted the weights at inference, this pipeline leaves weights frozen and spends the budget on search breadth and on a selection stage instead. The ablation is the contribution worth extracting: the paper separates how much of the result came from checkpoint choice, how much from verification, and how much from refinement, which is the decomposition anyone budgeting test-time compute needs. SPRINT and this paper are composable, since SPRINT parallelizes within a trajectory while this searches across trajectories.

## Why Leo should care
The medal is not the useful part; the compute decomposition is. For a proposal that has to justify an inference budget, this is the current reference for how much verification and refinement are each worth at the frontier, measured on a task with no partial credit and no tool access. The released benchmark of 200 novel problems also matters because contamination arguments are getting harder to make with older olympiad sets.
