# RISE: Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation

**Arxiv:** 2609.05295 | **Date:** 2026-09-07 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
On-policy distillation gives dense per-token supervision for post-training, but its ceiling is the teacher: an external teacher brings a distribution mismatch, and self-distillation with privileged conditioning is limited by how much the model can use in context. RISE builds a synthetic teacher out of the model's own reinforcement learning trajectory. It takes the displacement between the current checkpoint and a trailing anchor checkpoint, in parameter space or in output logit space, and extrapolates along it, which converts a sparse outcome-induced parameter update into a dense token-level target with no external model and no privileged conditioning. Outcome rewards from RLVR ground the extrapolation toward correct reasoning while the extrapolated teacher refines token-level decisions, and because the teacher is regenerated each iteration as the student improves, distillation becomes a recursive loop rather than a one-shot compression. The method beats RLVR-only training and on-policy self-distillation across mathematical reasoning, multi-domain STEM, code generation and multi-turn agentic tasks.

## Delta from parents
This paper extends the `deepseek2025_r1` to `wei2023_cot` bridge already recorded in `_meta/area_index.json`, which pairs reasoning-via-RL against reasoning-via-prompting. Where DeepSeek-R1 turned a sparse outcome reward into a parameter update and stopped there, RISE reads that same update as a direction and steps further along it to manufacture a teacher, because the displacement between two checkpoints already encodes what the reward was pushing toward at every token. That converts one bit of outcome signal per rollout into supervision on every position, which is the density gap between RLVR and distillation. Against the on-policy distillation paper Leo read two days ago, the two agree on the diagnosis and differ on the fix: that paper concluded the bottleneck is the algorithm and not the data volume, and RISE replaces the teacher rather than the data. The new work pushes the bridge further toward treating optimizer trajectories, rather than models, as the source of supervision.

## Why Leo should care
Checkpoint extrapolation as a source of supervision is a mechanism, not a task-specific trick, and it needs only a training trajectory and a trailing anchor. The obvious transfer is to any setting where the reward is sparse and per-step targets are missing, which describes most sequence-labelling and forecasting objectives on physiological data where only an episode-level outcome is known. The cost is that it inherits whatever bias the RL trajectory has, so the failure mode to check is whether extrapolation amplifies a systematic error rather than the intended direction.
