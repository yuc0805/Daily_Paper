# Iris: Climbing to the Search Frontier

**Arxiv:** 2609.04304 | **Date:** 2026-09-07 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
Iris-mini and Iris-pro are search agents trained at 35B-A3B and 397B-A17B scales, released with the data pipeline and training recipe. Training questions are reverse-constructed from the hyperlink structure of a web corpus: multi-hop chains are authored over an entity graph distilled from a seed page and its out-links, every non-answer entity is rewritten as a descriptive reference so that no clue resolves by string matching, and only questions that a reference model fails closed-book but solves with the supporting evidence are admitted. Supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning against live search alternate in a loop the authors call SFT-RL climbing, with the hardest solved and most efficient rollouts of each RL round fed back into the next supervised pass. With inference-time context management enabled the two models reach 82.2/84.8/86.9/52.3 and 88.6/85.1/92.9/56.4 on BrowseComp, BrowseComp-ZH, DeepSearchQA and HLE, from a single ReAct agent with no sub-agents and no test-time verification.

## Delta from parents
Where LATS bought capability at inference time by expanding a search tree over candidate trajectories, Iris moves the same capability into the weights and then runs a single ReAct pass, because the training loop can afford the expensive exploration once while deployment cannot afford it per query. The second difference from DeepSeek-R1 is that the RL is run against live search rather than a verifier on a fixed answer set, which makes the environment nonstationary and forces the engineering the paper spends most of its length on: reward judge and observation summarizer served inside the training cluster, and over-long rollouts interrupted at the request level and resumed from a committed prefix. The measurement contribution is separate from both parents and more useful than the headline numbers, since the authors report every benchmark with and without inference-time context management and show the gap that management produces is larger than most reported differences between competing systems.

## Why Leo should care
The context-management ablation is the part worth taking. It says that a large share of published agent-benchmark differences is attributable to a component that papers usually leave unreported, which is a reason to treat single-number agent comparisons in reviews with suspicion. The reverse-construction recipe for training data is also portable: building questions backward from a known evidence graph, then filtering to those a model fails closed-book, is a general way to make a benchmark that resists memorisation, and the same construction would work over a sensor-record graph rather than a hyperlink graph.
