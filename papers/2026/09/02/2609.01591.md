# StudentSim: Training LLM-based Student Simulators

**Arxiv:** 2609.01591 | **Date:** 2026-09-02 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
An AI tutor can only be tuned to a learner if there is evidence about which guidance helps that learner, and collecting it from real people is slow. StudentSim builds a simulator per student from sparse per-student records through two stages, pooled training across all students followed by per-student specialization. The companion protocol, StudentSimEval, covers 60 students across chess, second-language English writing, and mathematics, and scores two separate abilities: behavioral fidelity, whether the simulator reproduces the student's own responses, and guidance responsiveness, whether it updates under a tutor's correction. On chess, StudentSim reaches fidelity 0.51 and responsiveness 0.91, against 0.23 and 0.72 for GPT-5.4 and 0.45 and 0.27 for Maia2. Used as the reward for tutor reinforcement learning, it produces a tutor that human experts rate above both a no-reinforcement baseline and a tutor trained against a GPT-5.4 reward.

## Delta from parents
Where Machine Theory of Mind learned a predictor of another agent's behaviour and stopped at prediction, this paper requires the learned model to also move correctly when the observed agent is instructed, which is a second and separately scored property. The decomposition matters because the two abilities trade off in opposite directions across existing methods: the fitted behavioural model, Maia2, scores 0.45 fidelity but 0.27 responsiveness, while the prompted language model scores 0.23 and 0.72. Neither prior class is deficient in general; each is deficient on the axis the other covers, and the two-stage pipeline exists to get both from the same sparse records. Where CounselBench evaluates a model against per-person data as a benchmark endpoint, StudentSim closes the loop and spends the fitted per-person model as a reward signal, so the simulator becomes training infrastructure rather than an evaluation target.

## Why Leo should care
The structure of the problem is the same one that per-subject physiological modelling faces: a handful of records per person, far too few to fit an individual model, and a population that is heterogeneous enough that a single pooled model is wrong for most individuals. Pooled training followed by per-individual specialization is the standard answer, but this paper contributes an evaluation design worth copying rather than a new fitting method: score the personalized model on fidelity to the individual and on responsiveness to an intervention separately, because a model can be good at one and useless at the other and a single aggregate metric hides it. For wearable health, the intervention analogue is a behavioural prompt or a treatment change, and no benchmark in the library scores responsiveness to it.
