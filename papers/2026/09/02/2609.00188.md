# ZimaBlue: Evolving Generalizable World Action Models through Scalable Video Pre-training

**Arxiv:** 2609.00188 | **Date:** 2026-09-02 | **Area:** world-model-rl

## What it does
Robot manipulation policies generalize in proportion to the breadth of physical experience they were trained on, and action-labelled robot trajectories are the expensive, narrow part of that supply. Egocentric human video is abundant and covers contact dynamics, tool use, and long-horizon behaviour, but carries no action labels. ZimaBlue converts it through a three-stage curriculum: causal embodied video pre-training on human and robot egocentric video, video-action mid-training that grounds the learned dynamics in heterogeneous robot trajectories under a unified action representation, and specialization to a target robot. To make a generative world model usable for control, an asynchronous slow-fast split runs a high-capacity world model for representation and a light branch for 30 Hz action prediction on a single RTX 4090. Real-robot zero-shot success rises from 36.1 percent when training on target-robot data alone to 77.8 percent at over 120,000 hours of embodied video.

## Delta from parents
Where Ha 2018 learned the world model from the agent's own interaction data, and where DINO-WM took the representation as given and put the learning into planning, this paper learns the model from video the agent never generated and never labelled, then attaches actions in a separate later stage. The order is the claim: dynamics first without actions, grounding second with a small amount of action data, specialization third. The scaling number is what makes the ordering credible, since success more than doubles as the action-free corpus grows while the action-labelled portion stays where it was, which is evidence the video is carrying dynamics and not merely visual variety. The slow-fast split addresses the standing objection to generative world models in control, that sampling is too slow to close a loop, by decoupling the rate at which representations are refreshed from the rate at which actions are emitted.

## Why Leo should care
The transferable structure is pre-training on a large unlabelled stream of a related modality and attaching the labelled target task in a short second stage, with an explicit measurement of how success scales with the unlabelled corpus rather than a single-point comparison. That scaling curve is the missing figure in most bio-sensing pre-training papers, SSL for HAR included, which report a final number at one corpus size. The slow-fast decoupling is also directly relevant to on-device physiological inference, where a heavy encoder cannot run at the sampling rate but a light head can, and the library has no paper that separates the two rates.
