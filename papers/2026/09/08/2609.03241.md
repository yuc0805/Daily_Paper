# FlowBalance: Verifier-Grounded Self-Improvement from On-Policy Reasoning Experience

**Arxiv:** 2609.03241 | **Date:** 2026-09-08 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
FlowBalance learns a normalised distribution over complete responses rather than a per-token objective, and combines two supervision sources that fail in opposite directions. A terminal verifier gives reliable but sparse signal; a frozen training-time copy of the same policy, given privileged context, gives dense token-level log-probability gains that are aggregated into one trajectory-level self-guidance score. On mathematical reasoning it improves average accuracy over FlowRL on both Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, trains faster and more stably, avoids the response-length collapse seen in direct on-policy self-distillation, and keeps higher correct-strategy diversity in a controlled AIME24 diagnostic.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 accepted sparse outcome reward and paid for it in sample efficiency, FlowBalance keeps that reward as the ground truth but adds a dense signal drawn from the model itself, so the two supervision sources are combined rather than substituted. Where Chain-of-Thought treated the reasoning trace as a prompting artefact, this paper treats the whole trajectory as the unit of the learned distribution, which is what allows the length-collapse failure mode to be diagnosed as a distributional effect instead of a decoding one. The self-guidance score is deliberately aggregated to trajectory level rather than applied per token, because the paper's diagnosis is that per-token privileged guidance is what narrows the reasoning-path set. Compared to Latent-GRPO, the change is not the optimiser but what the advantage is computed over.
