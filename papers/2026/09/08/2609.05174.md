# SMILE: Self-Explainable Multimodal Information Bottleneck for Medical Diagnosis

**Arxiv:** 2609.05174 | **Date:** 2026-09-08 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
SMILE formulates self-explainable multimodal medical diagnosis inside the information bottleneck framework, and optimises prediction accuracy and modality-specific explanation jointly rather than attaching an explanation after training. The model identifies which elements inside each modality carry the diagnostic signal, so the explanation is per-modality rather than a single saliency map over a fused representation. Optimisation uses a matrix-based Renyi alpha-order entropy functional, which makes the bottleneck term tractable without a variational bound, under the assumption that the encoders are expressive enough.

## Delta from parents
Where UNI produced a strong single-modality representation and left interpretation to whatever attribution method the user attaches, SMILE makes the selection of informative elements part of the training objective, because a post-hoc attribution over a fused multimodal representation cannot say which modality carried the decision. Where Concept Bottleneck Models placed an interpretable layer inside the network but required a predefined concept vocabulary, the information bottleneck formulation here needs no such vocabulary and instead compresses each modality against the label. Where SHAP is model-agnostic and therefore always available, it is also always approximate and computed after the fact; the trade this paper makes is to give up model-agnosticism in exchange for an explanation that the optimisation actually enforced. This is the first paper in the library that connects graphify Community 6, model interpretability, to Community 1, health and self-supervised models, which were previously separate.
