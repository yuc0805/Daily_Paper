# Unlocking Lossless Speedups in LLMs via Discrete Diffusion (Uno)

**Arxiv:** 2609.04010 | **Date:** 2026-09-08 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
Uno splits a language model into two parameter sets: autoregressive weights trained with ordinary next-token prediction, and lightweight diffusion weights trained in a short distillation phase to draw several tokens at once from that same autoregressive distribution. A sampler family called Psi-Spec then decodes multiple tokens per step while provably matching the base model distribution, so there is no quality loss and no separate draft model. The 8B Uno model reports up to 3x throughput over its autoregressive base at every batch size tested, and beats the 26B DiffusionGemma and the proprietary Mercury 2 on agentic tool use, coding and long-context reasoning.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [GX7WR7KA] DDPM (Ho 2020, graphify_id ho2020_ddpm, community 3) — established denoising diffusion as a way to sample from a data distribution by iterative parallel refinement rather than sequential factorisation.
- [PHB9VRVM] Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani 2017, graphify_id vaswani2017_transformer, community 2) — established the next-token-prediction transformer whose sequential decode is the bottleneck this paper removes.
- [BZKDNHD6] SPRINT (Biju 2025) — established that a reasoning model can be post-trained to identify and exploit parallelism inside its own trajectory.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM used diffusion to define the model distribution itself, this paper uses diffusion only as a proposal mechanism and keeps the autoregressive model as the distribution of record, because that is what preserves quality: diffusion language models to date have bought parallelism by accepting a different and weaker distribution. Where the transformer paper made next-token prediction the training objective and the decoding order at the same time, Uno separates them, training with next-token prediction but decoding out of order under a sampler that repairs the mismatch. Where SPRINT found parallelism at the level of reasoning steps and needed curated trajectory data to do it, Uno finds parallelism at the level of tokens and needs only a distillation pass over existing weights. The practical consequence is that the speedup applies to any open-weight autoregressive model without retraining it from scratch and without the draft-model machinery that speculative decoding requires.

## Why Leo should care
This is the first result in the library where diffusion crosses from the generative-vision community into the language-model community as an inference-time mechanism rather than a generative one, which is exactly the kind of crossing that later becomes portable to time series. TimeGrad already puts diffusion on forecasting in his library, but as the forecast distribution; the Uno decomposition suggests the opposite arrangement for long-horizon sensor forecasting, where an autoregressive backbone stays the distribution of record and a diffusion head emits a block of future steps in parallel. That transfer is not yet done by anyone and is listed under Gap Watch below.
