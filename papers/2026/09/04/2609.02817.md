# Cliff: Learning Process Rewards from the First Mistake

**Arxiv:** 2609.02817 | **Date:** 2026-09-04 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Cliff is a reward-shaping rule for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. An off-the-shelf LLM acting as a teacher reads each rollout and marks the position of the first reasoning error. That single index splits the rollout into a correct prefix and an incorrect suffix, and Cliff assigns positive token-level advantage to the prefix and negative advantage after the break. Across 12 scenarios it beats on-policy distillation by 15 percent and standard GRPO by 7 percent, and the gain holds with teachers of modest capability.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 gave one scalar per rollout and let the optimiser spread that credit over every token, Cliff spends one teacher call to locate the single point where the trace goes wrong and shapes the advantage around it, because the argument is that everything after the first error is conditioned on an invalid prefix and therefore carries almost no additional information. Where on-policy distillation needs the teacher and student to share a reasoning pattern so that token-level KL is meaningful, Cliff needs the teacher only to point at an index, which is why a weak teacher still works. Where the on-policy distillation audit in your library asked how much signal the teacher really supplies, Cliff answers by reducing the teacher's job to the smallest useful output.

## Why Leo should care
The mechanism is a cheap, general recipe for turning a weak verifier into dense supervision, and the assumption it rests on — that error is absorbing, so post-error tokens are uninformative — is a property of sequential decision processes rather than of language. It transfers to any agent whose trace is a sequence of steps that can each be checked against evidence, which includes time-series reasoning agents: the first operator call whose numeric claim is not supported by the evidence log is exactly a first-mistake index. No one has done this in the time-series or bio-sensing setting.
