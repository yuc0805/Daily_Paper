# BALMS: Benchmarking Agentic LLMs for Longitudinal Mental Health Sensing

**Arxiv:** 2608.27219 | **Date:** 2026-09-04 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
BALMS is a benchmark for LLM agents that must read weeks or months of wearable behavioural and physiological signal and predict a self-report wellbeing score, together with a rationale grounded in the evidence. It covers three real-world longitudinal datasets, two task families (closed-form score prediction, and rationale generation auto-graded by an LLM judge), three agentic paradigms, and five open and closed backbones. The headline result is negative: zero-shot agents rarely beat predicting the per-subject mean, and they beat it only with the strongest backbones or when the input is a compact set of semantically meaningful features. Chain-of-thought prompting helps reasoning-tuned backbones but does not deliver temporal grounding or numerical correctness. Accepted at EMNLP 2026 Main.

## Delta from parents
Where GLOBEM fixed the datasets and the generalisation protocol for supervised wellbeing prediction, BALMS keeps the datasets and swaps the predictor for an agent that must decide for itself what history to retrieve, so the measured quantity moves from model accuracy to agent policy quality. Where Few-Shot Health Learners showed that an LLM can read a short window of physiological features, BALMS asks whether the same reading holds over months, and finds that it does not: the mean baseline is hard to beat once the context is long. Where HEARTS scored single-shot reasoning over health series across many domains, BALMS narrows to mental health but adds the agentic loop and the rationale-grading task, so the failure it exposes is retrieval and grounding rather than perception. Where MindScape used the LLM to write back to the participant, BALMS points the same pipeline at a held-out numeric target and grades it.

The paper extends Few-Shot Health Learners, which already crosses Community 5 (wearable sensing) and Community 1 (health AI) in your library through its Sensor2Text bridge. The new work pushes that bridge further into evaluation: the crossing is no longer a demonstration that language models touch sensor data, it is a measurement of how badly they do so at long horizons.

## Why Leo should care
This is a negative result on exactly the setup that a lot of wearable-plus-LLM work assumes. Zero-shot agentic prompting over longitudinal sensor streams is now measured, published at a main conference, and shown to be near or below a mean baseline, which means the honest starting point for new work in this area is no longer "can an agent do this" but "what does the agent need in order to beat the mean". The paper names three of those requirements: selective history retrieval, temporal grounding of the evidence, and interpretable behavioural features rather than raw streams. The imported method is agentic evaluation from the LLM-agent literature, and it should be treated as ported and closed off for longitudinal mental health sensing. What is not closed off is the fix: no one has yet trained the retrieval policy, and no one has applied process-level reward shaping to the agent's intermediate evidence steps.
