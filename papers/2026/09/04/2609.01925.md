# CRISP: Cliff-awaRe Input-adaptive Sparse Prefilling with Structural-Mass-Motivated Routing

**Arxiv:** 2609.01925 | **Date:** 2026-09-04 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
CRISP attacks the quadratic prefill cost of long-context attention with two changes to dynamic sparse routing. First, it replaces Jensen-Shannon divergence routing with C_struct, a structural measure of attention mass at Vertical-Slash compatible positions that reproduces the same routing decisions while removing the pooled matrix multiply and the KL computation. Second, it formalises a post-softmax mass cliff and shows that cumulative coverage thresholds accumulate O(n) background noise at long context, then sets a sink-aware threshold at the noise floor instead. On InfiniteBench, RULER and LongBench across two model families it is the strongest sparse method, matches or exceeds dense attention on retrieval-heavy tasks, recovers up to 28.0 percentage points on retrieval over baselines, and reaches a 5.30x attention speedup at 512k tokens.

## Delta from parents
Where Vaswani made every query read every key, CRISP keeps the architecture and decides per input which heads can afford a sparse pattern, so the saving is a serving decision rather than a training one. Where Declarative Attention put the selection decision inside the model's own output stream and applied it at decode time, CRISP keeps the decision outside the model, reads it off the proxy attention map, and applies it at prefill, which is the phase Declarative Attention does not touch; the two act on complementary halves of the same cost and are in principle stackable. Where DeepSeek-V3 shrank the cost of reading each entry, CRISP declines to read most of them, and its specific contribution over earlier dynamic routers is diagnostic: it shows that a cumulative coverage threshold silently admits O(n) background mass, which is why long-context retrieval accuracy fell for reasons that had nothing to do with the routing rule.

## Why Leo should care
This is the second paper in two days to argue that the practical limit on long-context attention is a selection rule rather than an architecture, and the argument here is the sharper of the two because it isolates a specific artefact of the threshold. That matters for long physiological sequences, where minute-resolution multi-day streams put sequence length, not model capacity, on the critical path. The mass-cliff analysis is signal-processing reasoning applied to attention maps, so it should be readable directly.
