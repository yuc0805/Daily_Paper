# Latent Reasoning with Normalizing Flows (NF-CoT)

**Arxiv:** 2606.06447 | **Date:** 2026-06-04 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
NF-CoT replaces the explicit chain-of-thought token stream with compact continuous states generated by a normalizing flow head inside the LLM backbone. The flow head produces latent "thought" positions while the standard LM head produces text positions, all within the same causal stream. This preserves left-to-right decoding, KV-cache compatibility, and tractable likelihood estimation. On code-generation benchmarks, NF-CoT improves pass rates over explicit CoT and prior latent-reasoning methods while cutting intermediate-reasoning token cost.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 improved reasoning via reinforcement learning over the full token output, NF-CoT moves the reasoning computation into a continuous latent space and uses normalizing flows to keep the density tractable. Where Latent-GRPO applied group relative policy optimization to latent thoughts, NF-CoT provides exact log-likelihoods through the flow, removing the need for reward-model approximation and enabling direct maximum-likelihood or policy-gradient training in latent space. The key technical contribution is instantiating a TARFlow normalizing flow inside the transformer backbone so that latent positions and text positions coexist in one causal stream with shared KV cache.

## Why Leo should care
This paper sits at the intersection of two directions Leo tracks: latent/continuous reasoning (Community 0: LLM Agents & Reasoning) and efficient inference. The normalizing-flow formulation is a clean alternative to the "soft tokens" approach in papers like PonderLM and CoLaR. If the method generalizes beyond code generation to other structured-output tasks, it could change how test-time compute is allocated in reasoning models. For Leo's time-series work, the idea of replacing verbose CoT with compact latent states is directly relevant to TS-Agent-style systems where each reasoning step currently requires explicit text generation over numeric evidence.
