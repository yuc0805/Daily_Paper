# Bridging the Last Mile of Time Series Forecasting with LLM Agents

**Arxiv:** 2606.02497 | **Date:** 2026-06-01 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
This paper formulates the "last-mile forecasting" problem: after a foundation model produces a statistically plausible baseline forecast, an LLM agent revises it using weakly structured business context such as holiday effects, campaign plans, external events, historical analogs, and expert feedback. The system maintains a unified forecast workspace, invokes tools to retrieve contextual evidence, and converts reasoning trajectories into explicit forecast revision actions under structural safety constraints.

## Delta from parents
Where TS-Agent uses an LLM to reason over raw series via tool calls, this paper shifts the target from understanding/reasoning to forecast correction. The key difference is that the agent does not replace the forecaster; it wraps around a pre-existing foundation model forecast and applies structured revisions. Where the LLMs-for-TS survey noted that zero-shot foundation models often fail on domain-specific distribution shifts, this paper provides a concrete mechanism — tool-augmented contextual revision — to close that gap at deployment time.

## Why Leo should care
This is directly in Leo's primary area (time-series + LLM integration, Community 4). The "last-mile" framing is practically important: foundation models for time series are getting good at the statistical baseline, but the gap between a good baseline and a usable forecast involves domain knowledge injection. This paper shows one way to do that with agents. For bio-sensing applications, the analogous problem is adjusting a foundation model prediction using patient context, medication schedules, or activity logs — the same architecture applies.
