# Uncertainty-Aware Foundation Models for Clinical Data

**Arxiv:** 2604.04175 | **Date:** 2026-06-25 | **Area:** llm-health

## What it does
The paper builds a clinical foundation model that represents each patient as a distribution over plausible latent states rather than a single point embedding. It enforces agreement across partial views of the same patient, so the model captures what can be inferred and marks the rest as epistemic uncertainty. The objective mixes reconstruction, contrastive alignment, and a distributional regularizer inside a multimodal encoder. It reports better prediction, better robustness under missing data, and better-calibrated uncertainty than point-embedding baselines.

## Delta from parents
Where the biosignals survey describes clinical foundation models that all produce a single deterministic embedding, and MAE-style reconstruction supplies the pretraining objective, this paper keeps the reconstruction-plus-contrastive recipe but changes the output object from a point to a set-valued distribution, because sparse, irregular, modality-dependent clinical data make a single deterministic embedding overconfident. The new ingredient relative to Leo's library is treating "what was not observed" as a modeling target with explicit epistemic uncertainty rather than a nuisance to average over.

## Why Leo should care
This is in Leo's secondary focus (LLM-health) and squarely useful for physiological data, which is always partial. On the porting axis: distributional, set-valued self-supervised representations are now applied to multimodal clinical data, closing that route.
