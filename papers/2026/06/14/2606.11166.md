# Flaws in the LLM Automation Narrative

**Arxiv:** 2606.11166 | **Date:** 2026-06-14 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
The paper questions the claim that frontier LLMs match human experts on knowledge-work tasks. The authors point out that most benchmarks report average accuracy on standardized datasets, often overlap with training data, and rarely measure how variable the model is or how large its errors are. They build a benchmark that asks for computer code to complete a real data-analysis task and compare a frontier LLM against human expert submissions, measuring spread and error size, not just the mean.

## Delta from parents
Where CounselBench built a domain benchmark and scored models on it, this paper turns the lens on benchmarking itself, arguing that mean accuracy hides the variance and error magnitude that matter in high-stakes use. The finding is that human experts do better on average and vary less, which contradicts the strong "expert-level" reading of headline benchmark numbers.

## Why Leo should care
The methodological point applies directly to Leo's own area. Reported wins for health and physiological models are usually single averaged scores; this paper is a reminder to report variance and worst-case error, which matter more for clinical signals than a mean does.
