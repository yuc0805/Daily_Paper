# Fine-tuning Multi-modal LLMs with ART: Art-based Reinforcement Training

**Arxiv:** 2606.11854 | **Date:** 2026-06-14 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
ART adapts a frozen multimodal LLM by optimizing only its raw visual input, not its weights or its computational graph. Gradients are backpropagated into a plain pixel array, so any fine-tuning objective can be used, and the optimized image ends up looking like a task-relevant piece of computational artwork. Because nothing in the precompiled model is changed, the approach stays compatible with high-throughput serving engines, which standard PEFT methods like LoRA and soft prompting are not.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- No direct parents in Leo's library. Closest neighbors are SigLIP (Zhai 2023, Vision-Language and Generative community) and PyVision (Zhao 2025, LLM Agents community), both vision-language models that ART could in principle wrap.

## Delta from parents
Where LoRA inserts trainable weights and soft prompting inserts trainable tokens, both of which edit the model's computational graph, ART moves the trainable parameters out of the model entirely and into the pixels of the input image. The technique combines adversarial reprogramming (optimizing the input to repurpose a frozen model) with parameter-efficient fine-tuning and reinforcement-style objectives, so the only thing that changes after deployment is the picture the model is shown.

## Why Leo should care
The input-space adaptation idea maps cleanly onto frozen time-series or bio-sensing foundation models: one could optimize a learned perturbation on the raw sensor channels to steer a frozen wearable model toward a new task, with no weight updates and no serving-graph changes. This pixel-space-to-signal-space transfer has not been reported for physiological foundation models.
