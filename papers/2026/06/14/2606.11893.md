# Beyond representational alignment with brain-guided language models for robust reasoning

**Arxiv:** 2606.11893 | **Date:** 2026-06-14 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper studies whether large language model internal representations line up with human brain activity recorded during deductive reasoning, and whether the brain signals can be used to make the models reason better. Using a task-specific fMRI dataset, the authors measure neural predictivity (a "brain score") and then build a brain-guided framework that steers model representations along directions defined by the joint structure of model and brain activity. The steering is applied both at inference and during fine-tuning. Across ten models from 1.5B to 72B parameters, the method raises deductive-reasoning accuracy by up to 13 percentage points, with gains that transfer across reasoning types.

## Delta from parents
Where Chain-of-Thought improves reasoning by changing the text the model produces, this paper leaves the prompt alone and changes the model's internal representation using an outside signal, human fMRI activity. Where DeepSeek-R1 improves reasoning with reinforcement learning over the model's own outputs, this paper adds a signal that the model could never get from text alone, and reports that the two sources of improvement do not overlap. The reported brain score is about 76% of explainable variance at the aggregate level but only about 27% within specific reasoning types, so the alignment is partial, and the contribution is to turn that partial correlation into a usable training direction.

## Why Leo should care
This paper sits on the bridge between Leo's two working areas. It treats a biological signal (fMRI) as a foundation-model supervision source, which is the same move as using wearable physiology, only the target is reasoning rather than health. The transferable idea is representation steering from an auxiliary biological signal: the same recipe could align a wearable physiological foundation model with a downstream clinical target at inference, without retraining. That transfer has not been done for time-series or bio-sensing, so it is an open opportunity rather than closed ground.
