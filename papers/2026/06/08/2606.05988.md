# Compress-Distill: Reasoning Trace Compression for Efficient Knowledge Distillation

**Arxiv:** 2606.05988 | **Date:** 2026-06-04 | **Area:** llm

## What it does
The paper studies whether compressing chain-of-thought reasoning traces before distilling them into smaller student models can save cost without losing accuracy. Two large teacher models (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B and gpt-oss-120B) generate about 283k correct traces each, which are then compressed to 8.6-21.0% of their original character length using instruction-tuned compressor models. Compressed traces reduce training tokens to 12-30% of raw, speed up training by 2.0-7.6x, and shorten inference outputs by 3-19x. However, raw traces retain the highest accuracy at every scale — compression offers an accuracy-efficiency trade-off (up to 96% of raw accuracy with 18x higher per-token efficiency) rather than a free win.

## Delta from parents
Where CoT prompting and DeepSeek-R1 focused on producing longer, more detailed reasoning traces to improve accuracy, this paper addresses the downstream cost: those traces are expensive to distill. The compression step sits between the teacher's generation and the student's training. Where DeepSeek-R1's distillation used raw traces directly, Compress-Distill shows that model-based compression outperforms naive truncation for smaller students, suggesting the compressor learns to keep structurally important reasoning steps.

## Why Leo should care
If Leo distills reasoning capabilities from large models into smaller ones for deployment, this paper quantifies the cost-accuracy trade-off cleanly. The finding that compression beats truncation (but not raw traces) at small scale is practically useful for choosing a distillation strategy under compute constraints.
