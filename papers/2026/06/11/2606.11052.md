# Attention Amnesia in Hybrid LLMs: When CoT Fine-Tuning Breaks Long-Range Recall, and How to Fix It

**Arxiv:** 2606.11052 | **Date:** 2026-06-09 | **Area:** mamba

## What it does
Chain-of-thought supervised fine-tuning reliably improves reasoning, but in hybrid linear-attention models it destroys long-context retrieval: HypeNet-9B falls from 67.2% to 9.4% on NIAH-S2 at 256K context after CoT-SFT, and the damage grows with retrieval difficulty and context length. The authors trace the cause to query-key projections (W_Q, W_K), whose gradients become biased toward short-range patterns during CoT-SFT. The fix, QK-Restore, is training-free: revert only W_Q and W_K to the pre-SFT checkpoint and keep all other post-SFT parameters; a Procrustes variant balances routing preservation against reasoning adaptation. On HypeNet-5B this lifts S3@256K from 65.4% to 76.4% while preserving reasoning performance.

## Delta from parents
Where Wei 2023 showed that step-by-step supervision improves reasoning, this paper shows the same supervision quietly breaks the memory pathway of linear-attention hybrids, because reasoning adaptation and long-range routing share the same W_Q/W_K parameters. Where the SSM/linear-attention line (community 2) established that hybrids can match transformer recall at lower cost, this paper shows that recall is fragile under standard post-training and can be restored surgically without retraining. In graph terms the paper adds a new edge between community 0 (reasoning) and community 2 (architectures).

## Why Leo should care
If Leo fine-tunes any hybrid or SSM-based sensor model (the HARMamba line) on a narrow downstream task, the same mechanism predicts silent loss of long-range temporal recall, and the same cheap diagnostic applies: compare pre- and post-SFT input projections, then selectively revert. The finding is also a caution for CoT-style supervision of time-series reasoning models.
