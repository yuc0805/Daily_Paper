# Rethinking the Divergence Regularization in LLM RL (DRPO)

**Arxiv:** 2606.09821 | **Date:** 2026-06-08 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
LLM RL post-training is usually off-policy because of training-inference mismatch and policy staleness, so trust-region control decides whether training is stable. PPO and GRPO approximate the trust region with ratio clipping, which is a poor proxy for distributional shift on long-tailed vocabularies. DPPO replaced clipping with a hard divergence-based mask, but a hard mask discards the gradient of any token that crosses the boundary. DRPO replaces the hard mask with a smooth advantage-weighted quadratic regularizer on policy shift, keeping the same trust-region geometry while turning discarded gradients into bounded corrective signals. Experiments across model scales, architectures, and precision settings show more stable and more efficient RL training.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that large-scale RL produces reasoning ability, DRPO addresses the optimizer that makes such training reliable: it argues the importance ratio is the wrong measurement of policy shift on long-tailed vocabularies and that even a divergence-based hard mask wastes signal at the boundary. Where Latent-GRPO modified what the policy reasons over, DRPO modifies how far the policy may move per update. The graphify bridge deepseek2025_r1 to wei2023_cot (reasoning-via-RL vs prompting) already crossed the prompting and RL sides of community 0; DRPO pushes the bridge further into RL optimization territory — the open question is no longer whether RL beats prompting but which trust-region geometry makes RL dependable.

## Why Leo should care
Every RL-trained reasoning or agent model Leo tracks sits on top of this objective-level machinery, and instability is the most common practical failure when reproducing R1-style training. DRPO is objective-level rather than architecture-level, so it is portable to RL over tool-using time-series agents.
