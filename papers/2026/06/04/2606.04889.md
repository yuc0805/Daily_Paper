# GRAIL: Gradient-Reweighted Advantages for RL with Verifiable Rewards

**Arxiv:** 2606.04889 | **Date:** 2026-06-03 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
Proposes a token-wise advantage reweighting scheme for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Instead of assigning uniform credit across all tokens in a response, GRAIL uses gradient-activation saliency to identify which tokens most influenced the correct or incorrect outcome, then reweights their advantage signals accordingly. It outperforms GRPO by 3.60% accuracy and 3.05% Pass@3 without requiring a process reward model.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 introduced GRPO with uniform advantage across all tokens in a response, GRAIL recognizes that not all tokens contribute equally to a correct answer. It computes gradient-activation saliency — essentially asking "how much did generating this specific token change the likelihood of the final verified outcome?" — and uses that signal to reweight advantages at the token level. This is a strictly finer-grained credit assignment than GRPO without introducing the overhead of training a separate process reward model, which methods like PRM800K require.

## Why Leo should care
The token-wise saliency mechanism has a clear transfer opportunity to time-series foundation model fine-tuning. In clinical prediction tasks, different time steps in a physiological signal contribute unequally to downstream predictions — a brief arrhythmia event matters far more than minutes of normal sinus rhythm. Applying GRAIL-style gradient-activation saliency to identify high-salience time steps during fine-tuning could substantially improve sample efficiency for clinical TS models.
