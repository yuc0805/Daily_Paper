# Language Models Need Sleep

**Arxiv:** 2605.26099 | **Date:** 2025-05-25 | **Area:** mamba, llm

## What it does
Introduces a sleep-like consolidation mechanism for language models that periodically converts recent context into persistent fast weights stored in SSM (State Space Model) blocks, then clears the KV cache. During sleep, the model performs N offline recurrent passes and updates SSM fast weights through a learned local rule. Tested on cellular automata, multi-hop graph retrieval, and math reasoning, the method shows that increasing sleep duration N consistently improves performance, with the largest gains appearing on tasks requiring deeper reasoning chains.

## Delta from parents
Where Vaswani et al. established attention over explicit KV caches and the Mamba line of work (HARMamba, Swin-UMamba) introduced SSM recurrence as a parallel or replacement pathway, this paper does something fundamentally different: it uses offline "sleep" passes to distill attention-derived context into SSM fast weights through a learned local update rule. The key insight is that attention and recurrence need not operate simultaneously — instead, attention gathers information during waking inference, and recurrence consolidates it during sleep. This sidesteps the quadratic cost of long-context attention without sacrificing the representational richness that attention provides during active processing.
