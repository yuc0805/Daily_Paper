# When Is Compositional Reasoning Learnable from Verifiable Rewards?

**Arxiv:** 2602.07992 | **Date:** 2026-06-22 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper studies, in theory, which compositional reasoning problems a model can learn under reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where feedback comes only from a check on the final answer. It defines the "task-advantage ratio", a joint property of the problem and the base model, and proves that problems where correct intermediate steps clearly raise the chance of a verified answer are efficiently learnable, while problems without that structural advantage can drive RLVR to a suboptimal composition. In some cases the base model's quality alone determines whether the advantage exists.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated empirically that outcome-only RL can extend reasoning, this paper supplies the missing theory for when that happens, because it isolates the structural property (the task-advantage ratio) a problem must have for outcome-level feedback to reinforce correct intermediate steps. Relative to Chain-of-Thought, which introduced intermediate reasoning as a prompting trick, this work asks when such intermediate structure is recoverable from rewards alone. It extends the reasoning-via-RL versus prompting bridge already in Leo's library (DeepSeek-R1 to Chain-of-Thought) by giving a formal condition that separates the two regimes.

## Why Leo should care
RLVR is the dominant training signal behind recent reasoning models. The task-advantage ratio is a transfer target: it could be redefined for time-series reasoning agents, where intermediate operator calls play the role of intermediate reasoning steps, to predict when outcome-only rewards can train an agent to choose the right analysis operators. No wearable or forecasting paper has framed tool selection as an RLVR learnability question, so this is an open opportunity.
