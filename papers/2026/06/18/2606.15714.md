# Beyond English: Uncovering the Multilingual Gap in Vision-Language-Action Models

**Arxiv:** 2606.15714 | **Date:** 2026-06-18 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models turn an image and a written instruction into robot actions, but are almost always trained and tested in English. This paper builds the first systematic multilingual test for VLAs by translating two robot benchmarks (LIBERO, SimplerEnv) into Chinese, French, Russian, and Arabic, plus a code-switching setting. It measures task success against English and proposes Multilingual Principal Component Alignment (MPCA) to reduce the gap.

## Delta from parents
Where DALL-E 2 and the SigLIP line build a single shared image-text representation space, this paper shows that such a space is fragile under downstream alignment. After a VLA is fine-tuned to produce actions, the non-English part of the representation drifts away from the English manifold, and relative task success drops by about 28 to 47 points for most models (65 to 76 for weaker action heads). The drop persists even with a multilingual backbone, which locates the problem in action alignment rather than the base model. MPCA projects hidden states onto a shared principal-component subspace and re-aligns them, recovering part of the gap without hurting English.

## Why Leo should care
The reusable pattern is diagnosis plus fix: a foundation model loses a capability after task-specific alignment, and a representation-space correction restores it. This is not a time-series or bio-sensing paper, but MPCA is a candidate to port to wearable sensing, where the device or cohort axis plays the role of the language axis (see Gap Watch). The imported method here is principal-component subspace alignment for cross-domain representation drift, which is not yet applied to wearable signals.
