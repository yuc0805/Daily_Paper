# GURU: Revisiting RL for LLM Reasoning from A Cross-Domain Perspective

**Arxiv:** 2506.14965 | **Date:** 2026-06-05 | **Area:** reasoning, rl

## What it does
Constructs a 92,000-example corpus spanning six domains (Math, Code, Science, Logic, Simulation, Tabular) with ground-truth verifiers and uses it to systematically study cross-domain transfer in RL-based LLM reasoning. Trains GURU-7B and GURU-32B using GRPO-style optimization and measures transfer matrices across all domain combinations. The central finding is a dichotomy: domains well-represented during pretraining (Math, Code, Science) benefit from cross-domain RL transfer, while underrepresented domains (Logic, Simulation, Tabular) require explicit in-domain training. GURU-7B/32B achieve state-of-the-art among open RL-trained models, outperforming baselines by 7.9% and 6.7% on a 17-task evaluation suite.

## Delta from parents
DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that GRPO produces strong reasoning, but trained and evaluated primarily on math and code. Chain-of-thought prompting showed that multi-step reasoning transfers across tasks within those same domains. GURU asks the next question: does RL-trained reasoning generalize to domains that are structurally different from math and code? The answer is nuanced. For domains with substantial pretraining exposure, cross-domain RL works — the RL phase surfaces and refines capabilities that already exist in the base model. For underrepresented domains, cross-domain RL fails because there is no latent capability to surface. This reframes RL not as a universal reasoning amplifier but as a domain-conditional skill activator whose effectiveness depends on the pretraining foundation.

## Why Leo should care
This paper directly informs the question of whether RL-based reasoning can be applied to time-series and biosignal analysis. These domains are almost certainly underrepresented in standard pretraining corpora, which — per GURU's findings — means they will not benefit from cross-domain RL transfer. Any attempt to build reasoning agents for sensor data (extending TS-Agent or similar systems) will need dedicated in-domain RL corpora with verifiable rewards. This is a concrete gap: no such corpus exists for time-series reasoning. The 92K-example Guru corpus also provides a template for how to construct one.
