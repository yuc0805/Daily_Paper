# Biosignal Fingerprinting: A Cross-Modal PPG-ECG Foundation Model

**Arxiv:** 2605.09579 | **Date:** 2026-05-14 | **Area:** llm-health, time-series

## What it does
Trains a Multi-modal Masked Autoencoder (M2AE) on 3.4 million paired ECG and PPG signal segments using cross-modal contrastive learning combined with masked reconstruction. Achieves AUROC 0.974 for 5-class cardiovascular disease classification and 0.877 for hypertension detection. The defining feature is single-modality inference: after pretraining on paired data, the model deploys with PPG alone, allowing consumer wearables to use representations informed by hospital-grade ECG data.

## Delta from parents
Zhang et al. applied masked autoencoders to single-modality time-series data. Yuan et al. demonstrated self-supervised learning for wearable sensors but within a single modality. Gu et al. surveyed the biosignal foundation model landscape and identified cross-modal pretraining as an open challenge. This paper closes that gap by training a masked autoencoder on paired ECG-PPG data with an explicit cross-modal contrastive loss. The contrastive pressure forces the encoder to learn modality-invariant features — representations that capture cardiovascular state regardless of whether the input is ECG or PPG. This is what enables the single-sensor deployment: the PPG encoder has been trained to produce features that are aligned with the ECG encoder's feature space, inheriting the richer diagnostic signal of ECG even when only PPG is available at test time.

## Why Leo should care
This is the most directly relevant paper to Leo's wearable sensing work (Community 5). It demonstrates a concrete pipeline for transferring hospital-grade signal quality to consumer wearables via foundation model pretraining. The 3.4M paired-segment dataset and the cross-modal contrastive framework provide a reusable template for other sensor pairings (e.g., accelerometer-force plate for gait analysis, wrist PPG-chest ECG for different body locations). The AUROC numbers are strong enough to be clinically meaningful.
