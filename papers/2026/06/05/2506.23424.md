# PETSA: Parameter-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation for Time Series Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2506.23424 | **Date:** 2026-06-05 | **Area:** time-series, test-time-training

## What it does
Introduces a parameter-efficient test-time adaptation method for time-series foundation models. When the test distribution shifts from training, PETSA updates only small calibration modules — low-rank adapters plus dynamic gating mechanisms — rather than the full model. It uses a specialized three-part loss: a robust reconstruction term that tolerates outliers, a frequency-domain term that preserves spectral structure, and a patch-wise structural term that maintains local temporal patterns. The method achieves competitive forecasting accuracy with far fewer updated parameters than full-model test-time adaptation baselines.

## Delta from parents
PatchTST introduced patching for time-series but assumes a fixed data distribution between training and deployment. TTT for Abstract Reasoning showed that test-time adaptation works on reasoning tasks but did not address time-series or the parameter efficiency problem. Sundial and similar foundation models are designed for general-purpose forecasting but degrade on out-of-distribution signals. PETSA bridges these three lines of work: it applies test-time adaptation (from TTT) to time-series foundation models (Sundial, PatchTST) while keeping the adaptation parameter-efficient through low-rank modules. The frequency-domain loss term is a novel contribution that addresses the specific failure mode of patching-based models — spectral shift between train and test distributions that patch-level losses alone cannot detect.

## Why Leo should care
This is the first paper in Leo's reading that applies parameter-efficient test-time adaptation specifically to time-series foundation models. For wearable health monitoring, distribution shift is the default condition: a model trained on population-level data will encounter individual physiological patterns, device-specific artifacts, and activity-dependent signal characteristics at deployment. PETSA provides a lightweight mechanism to handle this shift without retraining the full model, which is essential for on-device deployment where compute is limited.
