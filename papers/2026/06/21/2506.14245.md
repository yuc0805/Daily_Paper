# Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning in Base LLMs

**Arxiv:** 2506.14245 | **Date:** 2026-06-21 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
The paper studies reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for language model reasoning, building on the GRPO algorithm used by DeepSeek-R1. It argues that rewarding only the final answer can still credit wrong reasoning that lands on the right number, and introduces a metric, CoT-Pass@K, that counts a sample as a success only when both the final answer and the intermediate steps are correct. Using this metric, RLVR is shown to extend the reasoning boundary for math and coding rather than only sharpening answer selection.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 trained with verifiable rewards and measured success by the final answer, this paper points out that final-answer reward does not distinguish correct reasoning from lucky guesses, and proposes CoT-Pass@K to measure step correctness directly. Where Chain-of-Thought treated the reasoning trace as a prompting device, this work treats trace correctness as the quantity the reward implicitly shapes. This is the cross-area bridge already present in Leo's library (deepseek2025_r1 to wei2023_cot, reasoning-via-RL versus prompting); the new paper pushes that bridge toward measuring and crediting the trace itself, not only the answer.
