# Adaptive Patching Is Harder Than It Looks for Time-Series Forecasting

**Arxiv:** 2606.04074 | **Date:** 2026-06-21 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
The paper asks when content-adaptive patching (placing finer patches where a series looks locally complex) actually beats a well-tuned uniform patch size. Through a controlled study on three architectures, it replaces each adaptive mechanism with a uniform patch-size sweep while holding backbone, data, and training fixed. On standard long-horizon benchmarks the validation-selected uniform baseline matches the adaptive versions, with per-setting effects near zero and no consistent direction once aggregated by dataset.

## Delta from parents
Where PatchTST fixed a single patch length and showed patching helps long-horizon forecasting, this paper tests the next assumption that adapting the patch length to local signal complexity should help further. It shows that assumption is fragile: a scalar measure of local complexity cannot by itself produce a non-uniform optimum without a coupling constraint across time steps, so "looks informative" and "finer patching lowers the loss" are different statements. The contribution is a negative result plus a theory of when adaptivity can help, rather than a new architecture.

## Why Leo should care
This is a useful caution for anyone building time series tokenizers, including for wearable signals where adaptive patching is tempting around events such as heartbeats or steps. The method involved (dynamic patch routing) is from the time-series literature itself, so nothing is being ported from CV or NLP here; the value is the warning that the uniform baseline must be tuned before claiming an adaptive win.
