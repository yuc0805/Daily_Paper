# Neuro-JEPA: Learning a Sparse Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Multimodal Neuroimaging

**Arxiv:** 2606.14957 | **Date:** 2026-06-27 | **Area:** self-supervised

## What it does
Neuro-JEPA pretrains a foundation model on multimodal neuroimaging by predicting in a learned latent space rather than reconstructing the raw signal, and it enforces sparsity in that latent so the representation stays compact. The joint-embedding predictive setup avoids spending capacity on voxel-level detail that does not help downstream tasks. The result is one model that produces transferable representations across imaging modalities.

## Delta from parents
Where MAE theory explained masked reconstruction and SSL for HAR scaled it on 700K person-days of sensor data, Neuro-JEPA swaps reconstruction for latent prediction, because reconstructing raw neuroimaging wastes capacity on noise. The sparsity constraint on the latent is the new piece relative to both. It sits near Foundation Models for Biosignals as a biosignal effort, but on the latent-predictive rather than reconstructive branch.

## Why Leo should care
The joint-embedding predictive method (JEPA), a vision self-supervised idea, is now ported to neuroimaging, closing that transfer for brain signals. If Leo works on other physiological modalities, the open question is whether latent prediction plus sparsity beats masked reconstruction there too.
