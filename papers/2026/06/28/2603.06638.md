# HEARTS: Benchmarking LLM Reasoning on Health Time Series

**Arxiv:** 2603.06638 | **Date:** 2026-06-28 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
HEARTS is a benchmark that tests how well large language models reason over health time series. It collects 16 real-world datasets across 12 health domains and 20 signal types (ECG, EEG, PPG, EMG, audio, eye tracking, CGM, and others), with sampling rates from daily aggregates to 48 kHz and sequence lengths from tens of points to over a million. It defines 110 tasks grouped into four capabilities: perception, inference, generation, and deduction. The authors evaluate 14 current LLMs on more than 20,000 test samples.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [I2CIT4I7] TS-Agent (Liu 2025) — an LLM that reasons over raw numeric series by calling analysis operators and logging evidence, rather than reading the series as text.
- [JX3X3KH5] LLMs are Few-Shot Health Learners (Liu 2023) — showed that LLMs can handle simple health-signal tasks from a few examples, which set the expectation HEARTS now tests at scale.
- [VSCNJG5J] ChatTS (Xie 2025) — aligns time series with an LLM through training so the model can answer questions about a series.
- [N2JLZBY3] LLMs for Time Series: A Survey (Zhang 2024) — maps the methods HEARTS uses as baselines.

## Delta from parents
Where TS-Agent (Liu 2025) and ChatTS (Xie 2025) each propose one method to make an LLM reason over series and report wins on the benchmarks they chose, HEARTS does the opposite: it builds a single graded test set across many modalities and task types and asks whether any current LLM actually reasons. The answer is that LLMs fall well short of specialized models, lean on simple heuristics, and get worse as the number of temporal reasoning steps grows. Where Liu 2023 suggested LLMs are promising few-shot health learners, HEARTS shows that promise does not extend to multi-step reasoning, and that general reasoning scores barely predict health-time-series scores. This matters because TS-Agent and ChatTS both sit in your Time Series + LLM Integration community (graphify community 4); HEARTS gives that community a shared yardstick instead of method-specific claims.
