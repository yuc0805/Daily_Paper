# FEEL: Quantifying Heterogeneity in Physiological Signals for Generalizable Emotion Recognition

**Arxiv:** 2604.05926 | **Date:** 2026-06-28 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
FEEL is a large benchmarking study of emotion recognition from electrodermal activity (EDA) and photoplethysmography (PPG) across 19 public datasets. It evaluates 16 models spanning classical machine learning, deep learning, and self-supervised pretraining, grouped into four modeling families, and runs both within-dataset and cross-dataset tests to measure how well models hold up across experiment settings, devices, and labeling schemes.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [2XWEG7AF] Foundation Models for Biosignals: A Survey (Gu 2025) — maps the pretraining methods FEEL tests for biosignals.
- [RTMH75VW] SSL for HAR (Yuan 2024) — the self-supervised pretraining recipe FEEL evaluates as one model family.
- [3LA8GNCU] HeAR (Baur 2024) — a health acoustic representation model, an example of the signal foundation-model family FEEL benchmarks.

## Delta from parents
Where the Gu 2025 survey describes biosignal foundation models in the abstract and HeAR (Baur 2024) and SSL for HAR (Yuan 2024) each report results on their own data, FEEL measures all four model families on the same 19 datasets with explicit cross-dataset transfer. Its finding is concrete: fine-tuned contrastive signal-language models score highest on arousal and valence, but handcrafted-feature models stay competitive and beat raw-signal models in noisy, low-resource settings, and models trained on real-life data transfer well to lab settings (F1 = 0.79). This tempers the assumption that larger self-supervised pretraining always wins on physiological signals.

## Why Leo should care
