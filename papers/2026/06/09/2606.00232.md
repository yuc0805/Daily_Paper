# TIGER: Traceable Inference with Graph-Based Evidence Routing for Mitigating Hallucinations in Multimodal Generation

**Arxiv:** 2606.00232 | **Date:** 2026-06-01 | **Area:** hallucination, multi-modal

## What it does
TIGER is an inference-time repair framework for multimodal generation. It independently extracts an observation graph from the input and a claim graph from the output, matches claims against observations, ranks claims by factual risk, and repairs the highest-risk claims under a fixed compute budget. This makes hallucination repair explicit, rankable, and localized.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)

## Delta from parents
Where Permutation Self-Consistency detects hallucinations by checking whether outputs are consistent under input permutations, TIGER goes from detection to active repair by constructing explicit evidence graphs. The graph-based approach means each claim can be independently verified and prioritized, avoiding the problem where joint feedback methods are biased by the hallucinations they are trying to fix. The deterministic risk-ranking also makes the repair process auditable.

## Why Leo should care
The graph-based evidence routing approach could transfer to clinical report generation from biosignals, where hallucinated claims about patient status are dangerous. The explicit claim-observation matching is more auditable than black-box consistency checks.
