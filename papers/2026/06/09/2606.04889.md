# GRAIL: Gradient-Reweighted Advantages for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

**Arxiv:** 2606.04889 | **Date:** 2026-06-03 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
GRAIL introduces token-wise advantage reweighting for RLVR training of LLMs. Instead of broadcasting one sequence-level advantage to all tokens (as GRPO does), it uses gradient-activation saliency to assign higher weight to tokens whose hidden-state activations are most sensitive to the final answer. Evaluated on five models across math reasoning benchmarks, it achieves +3.60% accuracy and +3.05% Pass@3 over GRPO without requiring any process reward model.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 applied GRPO with uniform advantage broadcasting across all tokens in a response, GRAIL adds an intrinsic token-level credit assignment signal derived from gradient-activation saliency. This means the RL update focuses on the tokens that actually drive the answer rather than treating filler and reasoning tokens equally. Where Chain-of-Thought showed that intermediate steps matter, GRAIL makes the training procedure explicitly aware of which steps matter most — without the cost of training a separate process reward model. The key technical insight is that a single extra backward pass through the model yields a saliency map that serves as a cheap proxy for step-level reward.

## Why Leo should care
This is directly relevant to reasoning and RL-based training, both of which Leo tracks as secondary areas. The gradient-saliency approach is architecture-agnostic and could transfer to any setting with verifiable rewards, including time-series forecasting where output correctness is numerically checkable. If applied to TS foundation model fine-tuning, saliency-weighted GRPO could focus training signal on the most informative temporal positions — an unported opportunity.
