# Agentic Chain-of-Thought Steering for Efficient and Controllable LLM Reasoning (ACTS)

**Arxiv:** 2606.03965 | **Date:** 2026-06-02 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
ACTS formulates chain-of-thought reasoning control as a Markov decision process where a small controller agent steers a frozen reasoning LLM during inference. At each step the controller observes the reasoning trace and remaining token budget, then issues a steering action (strategy label + steering phrase) that initiates the next reasoning step. The controller is trained from synthetic steering trajectories with multi-budget augmentation, then refined with RL using budget-conditioned reward shaping.

## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [Z5IWHZAE] DeepSeek-R1 — established that pure RL (without supervised demonstrations) can produce chain-of-thought reasoning with emergent self-verification and backtracking
- [HBLPTRMY] Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023) — showed that prompting LLMs to produce step-by-step reasoning traces improves accuracy on multi-step tasks

## Delta from parents
Where Wei (2023) introduced chain-of-thought as a static prompting strategy and DeepSeek-R1 trained the reasoner itself via RL to produce long traces, ACTS leaves the reasoner frozen and instead trains a separate controller to dynamically choose what reasoning strategy to apply at each step. This decouples the question of what to think about from how long to think, enabling budget-aware inference that existing approaches cannot provide. The MDP formulation means the controller can learn when to explore alternatives, when to verify, and when to conclude — all without modifying the base reasoner's weights.

## Why Leo should care
This paper is relevant to anyone building systems that need controllable inference cost. The controller-reasoner separation is also architecturally interesting: it is analogous to metacognitive control in cognitive science, and the MDP formulation opens the door to applying standard RL tooling (reward shaping, curriculum) to reasoning efficiency. For time-series applications, a budget-aware reasoning controller could be useful for on-device inference where compute is limited.
