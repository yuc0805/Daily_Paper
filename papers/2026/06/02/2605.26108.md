# Reinforcing Few-step Generators via Reward-Tilted Distribution Matching

**Arxiv:** 2605.26108 | **Date:** 2026-06-02 | **Area:** generative-cv

## What it does
RTDMD aligns few-step image generators with human preferences in two stages. It shows that minimizing KL divergence to a reward-tilted teacher splits into a distribution-matching term and a reward-maximization term. Stage one (Ambient-Consistent Distribution Matching Distillation) does subinterval-wise matching with a consistency regularizer so the fake score model tracks the shifting generator under few updates. Stage two jointly optimizes both terms with a hybrid policy gradient: a GRPO-style estimator for stochastic intermediate steps plus direct reward backprop through the deterministic final step, with step-subset GRPO to cut variance. With 4 inference steps it sets new state of the art on SD3, SD3.5, and FLUX.2; the distilled FLUX.2 4B beats the 9B teacher at 50 steps on most rewards.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM relied on many denoising steps and DiT scaled the backbone, this paper distills to four steps and then applies reward RL to recover preference alignment lost by the speed-up. The contribution is the clean split of a reward-tilted KL objective into matching and reward terms, plus a hybrid gradient that handles the mix of stochastic and deterministic steps in a short sampler.

## Why Leo should care
This is a watch-area method, not a direct fit. The reward-tilted KL decomposition is a general recipe for combining distribution matching with reward maximization, which could apply to any distilled generator, including generative models of physiological signals where few-step sampling matters for on-device use.
