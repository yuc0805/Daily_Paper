# Next-Token Prediction Learns Generalisable Representations of Sleep Physiology

**Arxiv:** 2606.09605 | **Date:** 2026-06-13 | **Area:** self-supervised

## What it does
The paper introduces Hypnos, a large autoregressive RQ-Transformer trained by multi-modal next-token prediction on physiological sensor streams. Each of eight modalities (including EEG, ECG, and respiratory signals) is turned into a stream of discrete tokens by residual vector quantization, and the model is trained to predict the next token across more than 20,000 overnight polysomnography recordings. The learned representations transfer to downstream health tasks, and the model generalizes beyond sleep to daytime physiology, where it surpasses a dedicated ECG foundation model at detecting atrial fibrillation.

## Delta from parents
Where the biosignal survey (Gu 2025) catalogued masked reconstruction and contrastive learning as the two main self-supervised recipes for physiological data, this paper argues both are a poor match: masked reconstruction assumes a signal that is largely predictable from context, which clashes with the stochastic nature of physiological recordings, and contrastive methods need positive-pair definitions that are not well understood for these signals. Where masked-autoencoder theory (Zhang 2023) studied reconstruction in pixel space, Hypnos instead discretizes each modality with residual vector quantization and applies next-token prediction, the same objective that drives language models built on the Vaswani 2017 Transformer. The result is that a single autoregressive recipe, borrowed from language modeling, produces representations that transfer across modalities and even across the sleep/wake boundary.

## Why Leo should care
This paper sits in Leo's primary working area. It imports two methods from outside time-series and bio-sensing: autoregressive next-token prediction from natural-language modeling, and residual vector quantization from neural audio and image codecs. The combination "tokenize a physiological signal with RVQ, then train an autoregressive model on the tokens" is now demonstrated for multi-modal physiology, so that specific transfer is closing as low-hanging fruit. The open question it raises is whether next-token prediction beats masked reconstruction broadly for bio-signals or only on the sleep-heavy data used here.
