# Where Should Knowledge Enter? A Layered Framework for Knowledge Infusion in Multimodal Iterative Generative Models

**Arxiv:** 2606.06356 | **Date:** 2026-06-13 | **Area:** multi-modal

## What it does
The paper studies where external knowledge should be injected into iterative generative models such as diffusion when generation must respect structured, domain-specific, or safety-critical constraints. It identifies four places knowledge can act: the input/output boundary, the transition function, the intermediate state, and the model parameters, mapping these to four intervention layers (surface, trajectory, latent, and parametric infusion). In a controlled safety-alignment experiment with two diffusion backbones, each added layer addressed failure classes the earlier layers could not reach, cutting knowledge-violating outputs by 70.97% relative to plain generation.

## Delta from parents
Where DDPM (Ho 2020) defined a fixed denoising transition with no place for external constraints, this paper treats that transition and its intermediate states as injection points and asks which one to use for a given kind of knowledge. Where DiT (Peebles 2023) scaled the diffusion backbone without changing how knowledge enters, this paper holds the backbone fixed and varies the infusion layer instead. The contribution is a map from "type of knowledge constraint" to "where in the generative process to enforce it," with evidence that the layers are complementary rather than redundant.

## Why Leo should care
The layered-infusion idea is a candidate transfer into bio-signal generation. No work in Leo's library yet applies trajectory or latent knowledge infusion to diffusion-based physiological signal synthesis or imputation, where clinical constraints (for example bounds on heart-rate variability or QT interval) would be the injected knowledge. That is an unported opportunity rather than a closed one.
