# Personalized Deep Learning for Short-Term Forecasting of Impending Atrial Fibrillation from Continuous Wearable ECG Signals

**Arxiv:** 2606.10900 | **Date:** 2026-06-13 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
The paper tests whether personalizing a global ECG model by fine-tuning on one patient's own signals improves short-term forecasting of impending atrial fibrillation. A global model trained on the ICENTIA11K dataset is compared against personalized models fine-tuned across three cohorts (ICENTIA11K, IRIDIA-AF, MobiCARE), using 60-second ECG segments to forecast over a five-minute horizon. Personalized models reach AUROCs of 0.711 versus 0.614 on ICENTIA11K and 0.686 versus 0.585 on MobiCARE, and the gain grows with the amount of patient-specific fine-tuning data. Feature attributions point to clinically meaningful precursors such as premature atrial complexes and short supraventricular tachycardias.

## Delta from parents
Where biosignal foundation models (Gu 2025) aim for one global model that works across patients, this paper shows that the global model is held back by inter-patient ECG variability and that per-patient fine-tuning recovers a large part of the lost accuracy. Where few-shot health learning (Liu 2023) adapted a general model to health tasks with small labeled sets, this paper applies the same idea at the level of a single patient's ECG and quantifies how the benefit scales with adaptation data. The contribution is not a new architecture but evidence that test-time personalization, rather than a bigger global model, is what closes the gap for impending-AF forecasting.

## Why Leo should care
This is bio-sensing time series, Leo's primary area. The method imported here is straightforward fine-tuning for personalization, a form of test-time adaptation that is already common in CV and NLP; applying it to wearable ECG is now well demonstrated, so the transfer is largely closed. The useful residual question is how to personalize without per-patient labels, since the gains here depend on having patient-specific fine-tuning data.
