# TS-Haystack: A Multi-Scale Retrieval Benchmark for Time Series Language Models

**Arxiv:** 2602.14200 | **Date:** 2026-06-30 | **Area:** time-series

## What it does
TS-Haystack is a benchmark that tests whether time-series language models can find a specific pattern inside a long signal. It poses retrieval at several scales, from a short local motif to a long-range trend, so a model cannot pass by attending only to a small window. The goal is to measure long-context retrieval for time series the way "needle in a haystack" tests measure it for text. ## Direct parents (in Leo's library)
- [VSCNJG5J] ChatTS — built a time-series-aware LLM that answers questions about a series.
- [I2CIT4I7] TS-Agent — used an LLM that calls analysis operators over a raw series and keeps an evidence log.
- [N2JLZBY3] LLMs for Time Series: A Survey — mapped the landscape of LLM-for-time-series methods and their evaluation gaps.

## Delta from parents
Where ChatTS and TS-Agent show that an LLM can reason about a series, TS-Haystack asks a sharper question: can it locate the right part of a long series at all. The benchmark targets the failure mode that the survey (N2JLZBY3) flagged, namely that existing evaluations reward pattern recognition on short clips and do not test long-context retrieval. The contribution is measurement rather than a new model.

## Why Leo should care
For wearable and physiological streams, which are long and mostly uninformative with rare events, retrieval at scale is the property that matters. A benchmark that isolates this skill tells Leo which time-series LLMs would actually find a brief arrhythmia or a single fall inside hours of recording.
