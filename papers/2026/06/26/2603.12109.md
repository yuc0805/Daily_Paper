# On Information Self-Locking in Reinforcement Learning for Active Reasoning of LLM agents

**Arxiv:** 2603.12109 | **Date:** 2026-06-26 | **Area:** agent

## What it does
The paper identifies "information self-locking": LLM agents trained with outcome-reward reinforcement learning stop asking informative questions and fail to use evidence they already hold in active-reasoning settings. It decomposes active reasoning into Action Selection (which queries shape the observation stream) and Belief Tracking (how belief updates from evidence), and shows that weak versions of both starve exploration during training, while poor exploration prevents either capability from improving, a feedback loop that pins the agent in a low-information state.

## Delta from parents
Where DeepSeek-R1 showed outcome-reward RL yields strong reasoning on closed tasks, this paper shows the same recipe breaks once the agent must actively collect information, because outcome rewards do not credit the act of asking a good question. Where LATS adds explicit search to drive exploration, this paper explains why a plain RL agent fails to explore on its own, and names the two capabilities that have to improve together.
