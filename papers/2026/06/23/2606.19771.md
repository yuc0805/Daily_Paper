# Beyond Entropy: Learning from Token-Level Distributional Deviations for LLM Reasoning

**Arxiv:** 2606.19771 | **Date:** 2026-06-23 | **Area:** reasoning

## What it does
This paper introduces the Independent Combinatorial Tokens (ICT) framework for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards. Instead of treating every token update equally or maximizing a single scalar entropy term, ICT measures the Jensen-Shannon divergence between token logit distributions and updates only the roughly 10 percent of tokens that sit at distinctive branching points. On Qwen2.5 models (0.5B, 1.5B, 7B) it reports an average pass@4 gain of 4.58 percent, with a maximum of 14.9 percent, over GRPO and entropy-control baselines across seven math, commonsense, and Olympiad benchmarks.

## Delta from parents
DeepSeek-R1 and standard GRPO update all tokens in a rollout under one outcome reward, which the paper argues causes either entropy collapse (premature convergence) or entropy explosion (incoherent exploration). Where those methods control exploration with a single scalar uncertainty term, ICT shifts to the distributional shape of each token's logits and updates only the tokens whose distributions deviate most, which it proves regulates both Shannon and second-order Renyi entropy at once. The change is therefore in where the gradient is applied, not in the reward: it is a targeted credit-assignment rule that stabilizes training without a separate entropy schedule.

## Why Leo should care
Training stability of RLVR is the main practical barrier to applying it outside math and code. A token-selection rule that needs no hand-tuned entropy coefficient lowers that barrier for new domains, including time-series reasoning, where reward is sparse and exploration is hard to tune.
