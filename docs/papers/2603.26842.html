VAN-AD: Visual Masked Autoencoder with Normalizing Flow for Time Series Anomaly Detection

Authors et al. · arXiv 2026-03 · arXiv:2603.26842 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

VAN-AD detects anomalies in multivariate time series by rendering the series as an image-like input, masking it with a checkerboard pattern, and reconstructing it with a masked autoencoder. Reconstruction error signals an anomaly. A statistical-space mapping pushes the reconstructed sequence back into the distribution of the original to widen the gap on abnormal windows, and a normalizing-flow module models the probability density of the current window so the anomaly score is a calibrated likelihood rather than a raw error.

Why

Reconstruction-based anomaly detectors flag points by reconstruction error, but a raw error is not a probability and is hard to threshold consistently across signals. Prior masked-autoencoder anomaly methods inherit this calibration problem. VAN-AD wants both the representational strength of masked reconstruction and a principled, density-based anomaly score.

How

A forward module reshapes the time series for the masked autoencoder; a reconstruction module masks and rebuilds it; a statistical mapping amplifies reconstruction discrepancies; and a normalizing-flow module assigns a likelihood to each window. The anomaly score combines the amplified reconstruction signal with the flow likelihood, so windows that are both hard to reconstruct and low-likelihood under the learned density are flagged.

Solves vs predecessors

MAE learns image representations by reconstructing masked patches but produces no calibrated anomaly score on its own. VAN-AD fixes the specific failure mode "reconstruction error is not a probability" by adding a normalizing-flow density head and a statistical-space step that widens the normal-versus-abnormal gap, turning a representation learner into a usable anomaly detector for time series.

Method vs predecessors

Compared with MAE, VAN-AD keeps masked reconstruction but moves the application from image pretraining to time-series anomaly scoring and pairs it with a density model. Compared with the biosignal foundation-model work in your library (Foundation Models for Biosignals), which uses the same masked-reconstruction family for representation learning, VAN-AD targets detection and adds an explicit likelihood model rather than a downstream classifier.
