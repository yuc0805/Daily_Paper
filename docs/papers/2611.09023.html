Constrained Nested-Logit Pricing: An Exact Reformulation and Global Optimization Method

Hoang Giang Pham, Tien Mai · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.09023

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Sellers must price substitutable products while meeting operational requirements such as price limits and sales targets. Nested logit captures stronger substitution among similar products, but these requirements can undermine the structure used to solve unconstrained pricing problems. We develop an exact reformulation and a global optimization method for a fixed assortment with price bounds and weighted price caps. The key novelty is to concentrate the difficult interactions at the nest level while retaining convex optimization over individual products, without discretizing prices or approximating demand. This structure makes the number of nests, rather than products, govern the exponent in a conditional worst-case complexity bound. The method returns feasible prices together with a bound on how much profit could still be gained, allowing decision makers to assess solution quality even when computation stops early. Extensions cover purchase-probability bounds and price-order rules, with explicit accounts of their computational cost. In synthetic experiments, the method certifies solutions for assortments of up to 500 products with few nests. Projecting unconstrained prices onto the feasible set loses up to 32.60% of profit relative to direct constrained optimization, highlighting the value of jointly optimizing substitution and feasibility.
