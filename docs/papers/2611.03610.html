A GPU Engine for Sparse Matrix-Vector Products on $k^2$-Tree-Compressed Graphs

Francesco Tosoni, Gabriele Mencagli · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.03610

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

The $k^2$-tree is a compact representation of Boolean matrices: it stores the recursive hierarchy of their non-empty submatrices, with neither values nor coordinates, and serves rows and columns alike. Its traversal, however, locates the children of each node by rank queries, a chain of dependent irregular loads that leaves a GPU waiting on memory. We implement sparse matrix-vector multiplication (SpMV) on $k^2$-trees for the GPU. Since the traversal does not depend on the vector, the positions of the children are static: we descend the tree one level at a time and obtain them from a parallel prefix sum or from a sampled directory of about 0.1 bits per node. Descending in tiles bounds the temporary memory by the depth of the tree, so the device memory of a product is known before it runs. The same traversal computes right and left products, for one vector or a batch, over the plus-times, Boolean and tropical semirings. We evaluate the engine on an NVIDIA GB10 and an H100 with fourteen Wikidata, Software Heritage, web and social graphs of up to 1.2 billion edges, against cuSPARSE, a grammar-compressed GPU engine and a Boolean CSR. Our results show that the tree is 2.3-27x smaller than the CSR of cuSPARSE and that, with its last internal level cached, a product takes 0.7-2.4x the time of cuSPARSE on the right and 0.27-2.6x on the left. On a 1.2-billion-edge graph, a product needs 1.1 GB on the device, 9x less than cuSPARSE. The engine is memory bounded: the $k^2$-tree is the format of choice when device memory, not time, is the constraint.
