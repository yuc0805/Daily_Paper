Look Before You Leap: Re-Architecting Disk-Resident Vector Search with TriVS

Yizou Chen, Baotong Lu, Peiqi Yin, Zhisheng Hu et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.29948

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Disk-resident approximate nearest-neighbor search (ANNS) scales vector search beyond DRAM, yet state-of-the-art systems remain I/O-bound, even with fast SSDs and sophisticated I/O execution. To understand this bottleneck, we conduct an I/O-centric analysis that breaks each query down into an approach phase that locates a query-near region and a converge phase that resolves the final top-k. This analysis reveals complementary I/O tradeoffs: cluster-based search circumvents approach-phase I/O through in-memory routing but reads coarse-grained, full clusters during convergence, whereas graph-based search employs fine-grained, vector-level I/O throughout, enabling efficient convergence at the cost of inevitable I/O during the approach phase. To combine the complementary I/O benefits of the two granularities, we present TriVS, a new paradigm that re-architects disk-resident vector search as a trilogy: approach coarsely, distill progressively, and converge exactly. Its key innovations come in two parts. First, it decouples the approach and converge phases from a single search granularity, employing cluster-based search to avoid approach-phase I/O and leveraging vector-level I/O for efficient convergence. Second, to bridge the two granularities, it introduces a new in-memory distill phase between the original two, progressively reducing routed clusters to a vector-level shortlist using compact quantized vectors before disk access. Moreover, coarser clustering shrinks the centroid-routing index, allowing TriVS to reallocate existing memory to distillation rather than expand its memory budget. Our evaluation on five datasets spanning diverse vector types and scales shows that, at Recall@10=0.9, TriVS achieves on average 3.42X PipeANN's throughput, 51.2% lower latency, and 63.7% less disk I/O, while using less memory and storage than PipeANN.
