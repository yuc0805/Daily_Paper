SARAF: Stationarity-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Time Series Forecasting

Zhou et al. · arXiv 2026-06-02 · arXiv:2606.04135 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

SARAF is a retrieval-augmented framework for time-series forecasting that adaptively balances relevance and diversity in the retrieved historical segments. It modulates diversification strength based on dataset-level stationarity, addressing the failure mode where similarity-only retrieval is brittle under non-stationary conditions.

Why

Real-world time series exhibit non-stationarity and regime shifts that challenge fully parametric forecasters. Retrieval-augmented approaches promise to condition forecasts on relevant historical evidence, but a highly similar past segment does not guarantee a similar future when the generating process changes. Existing retrieval methods select purely by similarity, leading to redundant retrievals that miss regime diversity.

How

SARAF first forms a candidate pool via temporal similarity with time-aligned enhancement. It then applies a diversity-aware selection strategy to cover heterogeneous historical regimes, with the diversification strength automatically modulated by dataset-level stationarity. Finally, a stationarity-aware aggregation step fuses the retrieved futures. On eight real-world datasets, SARAF improves average accuracy and robustness over strong baselines, with particularly clear benefits under non-stationary settings.

Builds on

  • iTransformerInverted transformer for time series; SARAF adds retrieval augmentation on top of parametric forecasters like iTransformer.
  • PatchTSTPatch-based time-series transformer; SARAF complements patch representations with retrieved historical evidence.

Solves vs predecessors

Prior retrieval-augmented time-series methods (TS-RAG, RATF) retrieve segments purely by similarity. Under non-stationarity, similar history does not imply similar future, so these methods retrieve redundant segments that reinforce a single regime while missing regime transitions. SARAF's stationarity-aware modulation adaptively increases retrieval diversity when the data is more non-stationary, covering regime heterogeneity that similarity-only retrieval misses.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to iTransformer and PatchTST, which rely entirely on parametric forecasting from a single input window, SARAF adds an external evidence channel: retrieved historical segments condition the forecast. Compared to prior retrieval-augmented TS methods, the key difference is the diversity-aware selection with stationarity-based modulation — a mechanism that has no equivalent in existing TS retrieval frameworks.
