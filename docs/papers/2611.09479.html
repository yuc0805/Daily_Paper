Resolving non-neutrality across scales in a hyperdiverse tropical forest

Pietro Zgaga, Pablo Villegas, Tancredi Caruso, Tommaso Gili · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.09479

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Different ecological processes operate at different spatial and temporal scales. Macroscopic regularities can therefore be captured by descriptions that ignore microscopic details, even when those details are mechanistically relevant to ecological processes. Here, we develop a framework inspired by statistical mechanics to track interspecific spatial correlations under spatial coarse-graining. To do so we combine random-matrix filtering with a spatially explicit neutral model. Applying this approach to eight censuses of the Barro Colorado Island tropical forest, we observe a continuous crossover from an interacting to an effectively neutral organization. Below a few tens of metres (20--35~m), the empirical correlation spectrum differs from neutral expectations, revealing scale-dependent species-specific structure. Part of this structure can be attributed to environmental heterogeneity. However, residual spatial associations provide candidate signatures of biotic interactions or other unmeasured ecological, non-neutral processes. With increasing spatial coarse-graining, empirical and neutral correlation spectra progressively converge, and above the crossover scale the neutral model provides an accurate description of the measured collective organization. Neutrality thus emerges as an effective description of the collective organization of the forest above a characteristic scale, without implying that species are equivalent at the individual level. Our framework provides an operational way to locate that scale.
