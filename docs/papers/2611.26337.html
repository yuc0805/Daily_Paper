How Should Candidates Be Weighted under Open-List Proportional Representation? Harmonic Weights and Dirichlet Order Statistics

Daria Boratyn, Dariusz Stolicki · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.26337

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Candidates in open-list proportional representation systems are not equally prominent, and measurements that involve aggregation over candidates therefore require some form of weighting. We address the question of how those weights should be chosen. Since weights based on realized preference votes are unavailable in advance and endogenous to the electoral outcome, we seek a priori weights that depend on list position alone, and derive them as expected ordered vote shares under a probabilistic model of the division of the list vote among candidates. The natural starting point -- the uniform distribution on the simplex -- is unrealistic, as it prescribes the same degree of concentration of the vote for every list, and under it the leading candidates become more alike as lists lengthen. The other members of the symmetric Dirichlet family require a concentration parameter, which is unknown and which there is no reason to expect to be constant across countries, parties, and elections. Accordingly, we treat that parameter as random. We prove that under a scale-invariant prior restricted to lists with at least two effective candidates, the expected share of the $k$-th candidate decays as $1/k$, and we show that over the leading positions of a long list, up to one hundred of them, the resulting weights are within 1.4 to 3.4 percent, in total variation, of the harmonic weights $(k H_n)^{-1}$, where $H_n$ is the $n$-th harmonic number. No fixed value of the parameter yields similar weights. Moreover, under the uniform distribution itself, harmonic weights are exactly the normalized expected margins between consecutive candidates.
