Evaluating Feedback-Driven Black-Box Processes via Stochastic Modeling and Simulation

Mengfan Xu, Zirui Liu, Juan Zhai, Shiqing Ma et al. · 2026 · arXiv · arXiv:2611.18477

Why recommended

Very recent in your research areas.

Abstract

We study feedback-driven black-box processes across software engineering and machine learning. These processes are inherently sequential: policies generate inputs, observe outputs/feedback from systems, and use the feedback to guide the design of subsequent inputs. While many policies are developed under structural assumptions on the underlying systems, many real-world systems operate without such knowledge, necessitating black-box policies that lack a unified evaluation framework. We introduce a stochastic framework that models and evaluates such policies using Markov chains and simulation, thereby connecting empirical practice with theory. We instantiate the framework in mutation-based fuzzing (widely used for discovering software bugs via sequential input mutation), by a novel Markov chain model of fuzzing whose transitions are governed by the mutation strategy and whose feedback is given by program execution. We develop a rigorous evaluation paradigm with new performance measures, sound estimators, and evaluation procedures with provable guarantees. Guided by the theory, we evaluate 9 fuzzers on 18 widely used fuzzing benchmarks and conduct case studies in 4 non-fuzzing domains. These results show that the framework provides a principled basis for evaluation while connecting to practice, with implications beyond fuzzing.
