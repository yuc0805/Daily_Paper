Information-Theoretic Threshold Designated-Verifier Digital Signatures for Resource-Constrained Devices

Andrew Wright, Aydin Abadi, Ittoop Vergheese Puthoor · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.18355

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Digital signatures are synonymous with data integrity and authenticity. Among applications such as e-commerce, software distribution, and instruction processing they play a vital role in the secure communication of data. Within this context and in preparation for quantum adversarial tactics we present three new information-theoretic threshold designated verifier digital signature schemes and prove them secure against \textit{any} adversary under the standard security criterion. The $n$-of-$n$ multi-signature, $t$-of-$n$ threshold with dealer, and $t$-of-$n$ threshold without dealer designated verifier signature schemes can be implemented with simple mathematical constructs and we demonstrate the efficiency of these with evaluation of signature computation ($\lessapprox$ 80\,ms) and verification times ($\lessapprox$ 150\,ms) on the resource constrained platform of Raspberry Pi 4. We find our scheme also has equivalent or improved (2$\times$) performance than a comparable state-of-the-art post-quantum threshold digital signature scheme on both resource constrained devices and the more capable consumer-grade 2025 M5 MacBook Pro.
