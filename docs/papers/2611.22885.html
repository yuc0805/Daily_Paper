The Whole Warps the Parts: Attentional Certainty Modulates Ensemble-Induced Bias in Visualizations

Jennifer E. Corbett, Brian W. Anthony · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.22885

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Data visualizations often require viewers to extract both ensemble statistics and individual values from collections of graphical marks. Yet individual items embedded within an ensemble can be systematically biased by that context. Prior studies have reported both repulsion away from and attraction toward ensemble statistics, but these effects have been observed across different conditions. We test whether task or attentional certainty explains these opposing forms of ensemble-induced bias. In Study 1, observers briefly viewed ensembles of differently sized circles and then judged the size of an individual circle embedded within the ensemble. Using a common stimulus generation framework, we tested both identification and discrimination when the target was specified before or after the ensemble, and an additional no-cue identification condition without a specified target. Under the shared cued conditions, effects were predominantly repulsive or near zero in both identification and discrimination. In contrast, eliminating target-specific information reversed ensemble-induced bias from repulsion to attraction. Identification precision also decreased systematically from pre-cue to post-cue to no-cue conditions. Study 2 replicated this cue-dependent pattern in graphical displays of startup valuations and precinct vote contributions. Our findings isolate attentional certainty as a key determinant of whether ensemble context repels or attracts individual-item representations, extending the design question beyond precision to how attention directing choices determine which bias the viewer will be operating under.
