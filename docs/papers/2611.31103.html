Self-similar Inequality Dynamics in Biased Competition for a Conserved Resource

Giacomo Bartolucci, Jonathan Bauermann · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.31103

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

In many complex systems the redistribution of a conserved resource can generate strong inequality. Important examples are cities exchanging inhabitants, farms competing for farmland, or agents for wealth in saturated markets. Here we model such systems as agents competing for a fixed total resource, and show that coupling agent extinction to a bias favoring richer agents dynamically amplifies this inequality over time. Drawing on an analogy with emulsions, where droplets compete for a conserved quantity, we introduce a dynamical critical resource size that determines an agent's fate. Crucially, this threshold grows in time, continuously raising the bar for survival and further reinforcing inequality. Increasing the bias strength, we find a transition from a continuous resource concentration towards a winner-takes-all outcome. Furthermore, the case of continuous resource concentration is characterized by a self-similar dynamics accompanied by a power-law or exponential increase in inequality, quantified by the Gini coefficient. We derive these results analytically in a mean-field model and compare them to agent-based simulations, showing excellent agreement.
