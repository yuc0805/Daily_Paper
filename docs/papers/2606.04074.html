Adaptive Patching Is Harder Than It Looks for Time-Series Forecasting

Authors per arXiv listing · arXiv 2026 · paper · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

The paper tests when content-adaptive patching, which places finer patches where a series looks locally complex, beats a tuned uniform patch size. It runs a controlled study on three architectures, swapping each adaptive mechanism for a uniform patch-size sweep while holding the backbone, data, and training fixed. On standard long-horizon benchmarks the validation-selected uniform baseline matches the adaptive versions, with effects near zero and no consistent direction once aggregated by dataset.

Why

Adaptive or dynamic patching has been proposed as an improvement over fixed patches, on the intuition that complex regions deserve finer resolution. But "looks informative" and "finer patching lowers the loss" are different claims, and prior work did not isolate the adaptive mechanism from confounds such as larger models or longer training.

How

For each architecture the adaptive routing is removed and replaced with a single fixed patch length selected on validation, keeping everything else constant. The EntroPE comparison contrasts entropy-guided segmentation with a matched fixed-length alternative inside one architecture, and the TimeMosaic comparison removes the per-region routing decision. The paper adds a theoretical account: adaptivity helps only when the routing signal aligns with regions where extra resolution truly reduces loss, and that headroom collapses under modest misalignment.

Solves vs predecessors

PatchTST fixed a single patch length and showed patching helps; later adaptive methods assumed varying the length by local complexity should help more. This paper shows that the adaptive step does not give a reliable gain once the uniform baseline is properly tuned, correcting an over-claim rather than the original PatchTST result.

Method vs predecessors

Where adaptive methods such as EntroPE, TimeMosaic, and HDMixer add a routing module that chooses patch boundaries or lengths, this work strips the routing out and compares against a uniform sweep under identical conditions, then explains with theory why a scalar local-complexity signal cannot by itself produce a non-uniform optimum without a coupling constraint across time steps.
