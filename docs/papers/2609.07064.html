SpatialBlock: Enhancing Spatial Intelligence in LVLMs via Synthetic Block-Stacking Problem

Ryu, Kim, Yang (KAIST AI) · arXiv 2026 · link · arXiv: 2609.07064 · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

SpatialBlock-15k is a fully synthetic set of 15,000 block-stacking problems covering 3D-to-2D projection, viewpoint transformation, and structural combination, extended with controlled colour modulation so that models learn to anchor their reasoning on task-relevant blocks under visually cluttered conditions. Two training strategies are released, a direct model that predicts the answer immediately and a reasoning model that produces a trace first. Both outperform baselines and transfer to real-scene spatial benchmarks despite the synthetic and compact nature of the training data.

Why

Large vision-language models describe what a scene contains but reconstruct the 3D structure behind a 2D image poorly, and that gap does not close with more captioning data. The standard remedy, real-scene spatial question answering, requires dense geometric annotation that is expensive, slow, and noisy because the labels are produced by external perception modules whose own errors propagate into the supervision. The motivation here is to obtain the skill without the annotation, by taking the route along which human spatial cognition develops: structured block manipulation, where the ground truth is known by construction because the scene was generated rather than measured.

How

Scenes are generated programmatically, so every projection, viewpoint change, and structural combination has exact ground truth and no perception module is in the loop. The three problem families are chosen to isolate distinct skills rather than to cover a domain: projecting a known 3D arrangement to a 2D view, transforming between viewpoints, and composing sub-structures. Controlled colour modulation is added as a visual cue that must be used to identify the relevant blocks, which pushes the model toward anchor-based reasoning rather than global scene matching. Training proceeds either as direct answer prediction or as reasoning-then-answer, and both variants are evaluated on real-scene spatial benchmarks they were never trained on.

Solves vs predecessors

BLIP-2-style alignment on captions and web image-text pairs produces models that name objects and relations in language but do not recover metric or topological structure, so they fail on viewpoint change and on occluded stacking. Machine Mental Imagery diagnosed the same failure and proposed to fix it inside the model, which requires architectural change and retraining. This paper shows the failure can be reduced from the data side alone, at 15,000 items and with no geometric annotation, and that the fix survives the transfer to real scenes. That inverts the working assumption that real-scene spatial supervision is necessary, and it removes the dependence on external perception modules whose noise was limiting the prior datasets.

Method vs predecessors

Where BLIP-2 changes the interface between a vision encoder and a language model, and Machine Mental Imagery changes the internal representation used during reasoning, this changes only the training distribution. No inductive bias is added to the encoder, so relative to ViT the geometry is taught rather than built in. The controlled colour cue is the one designed mechanism, and it targets attention allocation rather than representation: the model is forced to select which blocks matter before reasoning about them, which is the step a global appearance match skips.
