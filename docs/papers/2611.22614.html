An Affordable Vibrotactile Surface for Tactile Figure Recognition: Exploration Strategies of Blind and Sighted Users

Uday Nath Gunda, Arun Raman, Parthasarathy PD · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.22614

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Refreshable tactile displays can make graphics accessible to blind and low-vision people, but high-resolution actuator arrays remain expensive. We study whether a low-cost vibrotactile surface can convey simple figures, and how they are read. Nine participants (four blind; five sighted, tested blindfolded then open-eyed) identified seven figures by touch, with video and timing analysis of their exploration. Blind and blindfolded-sighted readers achieved comparable accuracy, but blind participants were about three times faster, using a continuous, rhythmic "gliding" strategy-reading through motion, whereas sighted participants searched in halting, pause-heavy passes; among the sighted, more pausing accompanied errors. Recognition failures were concentrated in specific figures (a diagonal was misread even with vision), locating the bottleneck in representation rather than resolution. We contribute an empirical characterization of tactile reading strategies and design implications for low-cost refreshable tactile displays and affordable tactile representation.
