RouteTS: Frequency-Time Routing for Time Series Forecasting

Anonymous et al. · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2608.14682 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

RouteTS partitions the amplitude spectrum of the input window and delegates each part to the computational domain where it is best represented. Dominant high-amplitude frequencies remain in the frequency domain and are forecast by a complex-valued linear predictor, which preserves phase and therefore does not accumulate periodic misalignment over long horizons. Residual spectral energy, where transient and local structure lives, is inverted back to the time domain and modelled by a lightweight MLP. The two branch outputs are recombined into the final forecast.

Why

Real time series carry global periodic structure and localised non-stationary variation at the same time, and existing forecasters process both inside one domain. Time-domain models drift out of phase with the periodic component over long horizons. Frequency-domain models suffer the complementary failure: the Fourier basis spreads a transient spike across many coefficients, so reconstructing the spike requires exactly the coefficients the model has already smoothed. Neither failure is fixed by adding capacity, because it originates in the representation rather than the model. The paper's claim is that the optimal computational domain is a property of the data, not of the model.

How

The amplitude spectrum of the input is split by amplitude threshold. High-amplitude components go to a complex-valued linear predictor operating in the frequency domain, preserving both magnitude and phase of the dominant periodicities. The residual spectrum is inverse-transformed and handled by a small MLP in the time domain. Routing is computed from the data rather than learned as a gate, which keeps the parameter count low; both branches are deliberately shallow. RouteTS reports the best average MSE on six of eight benchmarks. The two informative endpoints are COVID-19, the most non-stationary dataset, where it is best on both MSE and MAE because residual energy is handled purely in the time domain, and Solar, a strongly periodic dataset, where it is also best on both metrics, showing the dual-branch split costs nothing where a single domain would have sufficed.

Builds on

  • FEDformer (2022)Established frequency-domain forecasting with a random subset of Fourier modes for tractable cost.
  • DLinear (2023)Showed a single linear layer in the time domain beats most transformers on long-horizon forecasting.
  • TimesNet (2023)Made periodicity explicit by reshaping the series into 2D blocks indexed by discovered periods.

Solves vs predecessors

FEDformer moved forecasting into the frequency domain wholesale and controlled cost by sampling Fourier modes at random. That design cannot distinguish a component that is low-amplitude because it is unimportant from one that is low-amplitude because its energy is a spread-out transient, so transient events are dropped along with noise. RouteTS fixes that specific failure mode by keeping the frequency domain only for components where it is the correct basis and returning the residual to the time domain intact. DLinear demonstrated that shallow time-domain models suffice for long-horizon forecasting, but RouteTS argues the single linear layer was being asked to fit periodic and transient structure at once, and the COVID-19 and Solar results are the evidence that separating the two jobs helps at both extremes rather than trading one for the other.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to FEDformer, the mode-selection step changes from random sampling to an amplitude-based partition, and the frequency branch uses a complex-valued linear predictor that preserves phase rather than an attention block over sampled modes. Compared to DLinear, the architecture keeps the shallow-linear philosophy but instantiates it twice, once per domain, with a data-driven split between them. Compared to TimesNet, which recovers periodicity through a 2D reshape indexed by discovered periods, RouteTS reads the same information directly off the amplitude spectrum and avoids the reshape, which is the main source of its lower computational cost. None of the three predecessors appear in the graphify seed subset, whose time-series coverage (Community 4) is weighted toward LLM integration rather than the classical forecasting lineage.
