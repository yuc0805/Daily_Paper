Optimal Autoregressive Chain-of-Thought Learning via Bagging

Awni Altabaa, John Lafferty, Omar Montasser · 2026 · arXiv · arXiv:2611.21597

Why recommended

Very recent in your research areas.

Abstract

We study how to learn an autoregressive next-token generator $f\colonΣ^\star\toΣ$ from a class $\mathcal{F}$, given chain-of-thought (CoT) demonstrations of length $T$ generated by $f$. We present a simple algorithm based on bagging, which outputs a majority vote of generators that are trained to be CoT-consistent on bootstrap samples of the training data. For binary token sets, it achieves the optimal sample complexity of $O((d_{\mathrm{VC}}+\log(1/δ))/ε)$, independent of the CoT length $T$, where $d_{\mathrm{VC}}$ is the VC dimension of $\mathcal{F}$. For finite token sets $Σ$, the VC dimension is replaced by a multiclass analogue, the Graph dimension $d_{\mathrm{G}}$, and the sample complexity is optimal up to a $\log|Σ|$ factor. The number of generators in the majority-vote ensemble is $O(\log(T/δ))$, and we show that the ensemble can be distilled into a single generator in $\mathcal{F}$ at the expense of $n\log(nT)$ unlabeled prompts.
