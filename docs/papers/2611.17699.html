Spatial scaling of heterogeneity transfer from boron concentration to absorbed dose in boron neutron capture therapy

Roni Muslim, Bidhari Pidhatika, Sutanto Sutanto, Ramacos Fardela et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.17699

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

We investigate how the amplitude and spatial correlation length of boron-concentration fluctuations affect the boron-induced absorbed-dose distribution in boron neutron capture therapy (BNCT). The spatial energy-deposition response of alpha particles and lithium nuclei is obtained using Geant4 and convolved with three-dimensional correlated lognormal boron fields under a uniform neutron field. Approximately $90\%$ of the reaction-product energy is deposited within a radius of $6.00~μ\mathrm{m}$, with a characteristic deposition length of $\ell_B\approx2.05~μ\mathrm{m}$. Dose heterogeneity increases with both the fluctuation amplitude $H$ and correlation length $ξ$, while the transfer factor $S=CV_D/H$ exhibits an approximate scaling collapse as a function of $ξ/\ell_B$. Analytical Gaussian and lognormal--Gaussian models provide reference predictions for this behavior. At short correlation lengths, the Geant4 response yields a higher transfer factor than a Gaussian response with the same characteristic length, highlighting the influence of the energy-deposition shape on dose smoothing. For $D_{95}$, defined as the dose received by at least $95\%$ of the modeled volume, the relative difference between homogeneous and heterogeneous boron-dose models reaches $(86.62\pm1.07)\%$ at $H=1.2$ and a target ratio $ξ/\ell_B=6$. We show that mean boron concentration alone is insufficient to characterize spatial nonuniformity of the boron-dose component.
