Neuro-JEPA: Learning a Sparse Latent Predictive Foundation Model for Multimodal Neuroimaging

Neuro-JEPA authors · arXiv 2606.14957 2026 · link · Appears in: Self-supervised

What

Neuro-JEPA pretrains a foundation model on multimodal neuroimaging by predicting in a learned latent space rather than reconstructing the raw signal, and it enforces sparsity in that latent so the representation stays compact. The joint-embedding predictive setup avoids spending capacity on voxel-level detail that does not help downstream tasks. The result is one model that produces transferable representations across imaging modalities.

Why

Masked-reconstruction pretraining forces a model to predict raw signal, which on neuroimaging spends capacity on noise and fine detail that downstream tasks do not need. A latent-predictive objective should keep the useful structure while dropping the rest, but it had not been combined with a sparsity constraint for multimodal brain data. The paper builds and tests that combination.

How

The model uses a joint-embedding predictive architecture: an online encoder and a target encoder produce latent representations, and the model predicts target latents from context rather than reconstructing inputs. A sparsity constraint on the latent keeps the representation compact. Pretraining runs across multiple neuroimaging modalities so the encoder transfers.

Solves vs predecessors

Where MAE-style methods reconstruct masked input, Neuro-JEPA predicts in latent space, which fixes the waste of modeling raw neuroimaging noise. The sparsity constraint addresses a second failure of dense latents being hard to transfer, by keeping the representation compact.

Method vs predecessors

The technical shift is from reconstruction to joint-embedding latent prediction, plus an added sparsity penalty that neither MAE theory nor the HAR self-supervised work used. It sits on the latent-predictive branch of biosignal foundation models rather than the reconstructive branch surveyed by Gu 2025.
