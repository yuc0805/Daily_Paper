Towards a General Intelligence and Interface for Wearable Health Data

Narayanswamy, Xu, Heydari et al. · arXiv 2026-05-21 · arXiv:2605.22759 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series, LLM-Health

What

A foundation model pretrained on more than one trillion minutes of unlabeled wearable sensor signals from five million participants. It is evaluated on 35 health prediction tasks spanning cardiovascular, metabolic, sleep, and mental health, as well as lifestyle and demographic factors. A "classroom" of LLM agents autonomously searches the space of downstream predictive heads built on frozen embeddings, and a Personal Health Agent integrates these predictors with clinician validation.

Why

Converting low-level wearable sensor data into representations that characterize higher-level health states is difficult because of high phenotypic diversity and variation in individual baselines. Collecting outcome-labeled wearable data at scale is expensive and retrospective annotation is practically infeasible. Existing biosignal foundation models, as surveyed by Gu 2025, are either modality-specific or trained on small cohorts, leaving the scaling question unanswered.

How

Joint scaling of model capacity and pretraining data volume on unlabeled sensor signals from 5M participants. The frozen embeddings serve as input to downstream predictive heads. Instead of manual head design, a classroom of LLM agents searches the architecture space — performance improves with LLM capacity. A Personal Health Agent wraps these predictors to produce contextually aware responses, validated by 1,860 clinician ratings.

Solves vs predecessors

The Biosignals survey (Gu 2025) catalogued models trained on at most tens of thousands of participants and a handful of tasks. The primary failure mode was limited transfer: a model trained for one health outcome (e.g., sleep staging) would not generalize to another (e.g., cardiovascular risk) without retraining. This paper solves that by pretraining at population scale, producing embeddings that transfer across 35 diverse health tasks via frozen representations.

Method vs predecessors

GLOBEM used traditional ML pipelines with handcrafted features for cross-dataset transfer. Sensor2Text used student-teacher distillation to connect sensors to language. This paper skips both feature engineering and distillation: it pretrains directly on raw sensor signals at scale, then uses LLM agents (not human engineers) to design downstream predictive heads. The architectural search via LLM agents is a structurally different approach from prior manual or NAS-based head design.
