Towards a General Intelligence and Interface for Wearable Health Data

Narayanswamy, Xu, Heydari, McDuff et al. · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM-Health

What

A foundation model for wearable health, pretrained on more than one trillion minutes of unlabeled sensor signal from about five million participants. On top of the frozen representation, a classroom of LLM agents searches the space of downstream predictive heads, and the best heads feed a Personal Health Agent that answers health questions in natural language. The model is evaluated on 35 health prediction tasks spanning cardiovascular, metabolic, sleep, and mental health, plus lifestyle and demographic targets.

Why

Turning low-level wearable streams into personal health states is hard because individuals differ in baseline health, physiology, and lifestyle, so the same raw signal means different things across people. Paired labels (signal plus a confirmed outcome) are expensive and cannot be added retrospectively, so labeled data stays scarce. Prior wearable models were trained at smaller scale and used hand-built interfaces between signal and language, which limited both accuracy and the range of questions the system could answer.

How

The authors pretrain with self-supervision on the trillion-minute corpus and measure how performance moves as model size and data volume scale together. They keep the representation frozen and use LLM agents to automatically select downstream heads (which embedding, which architecture, which target) rather than designing one head per task. The selected predictors are wrapped in a Personal Health Agent, whose responses were rated by clinicians across 1,860 ratings for relevance, context awareness, and safety.

Solves vs predecessors

The main failure mode of the predecessors was scale and rigidity. Yuan 2024 demonstrated self-supervision at 700K person-days but could not show how far it scales; this paper provides scaling-law evidence at one trillion minutes and five million people. Liu 2023 relied on prompting with summary numbers, which loses signal detail; training heads on a learned representation keeps more of the signal and supports few-shot transfer to new tasks. Sensor2Text needed a hand-built captioning path; the agentic head search removes that manual step.

Method vs predecessors

Where earlier work fixed one downstream head per task, this paper separates representation learning from task adaptation and delegates head selection to LLM agents, so adding a task is a search rather than a redesign. The interface layer is also different: instead of a static prompt template, a Personal Health Agent composes the selected predictors and is checked against clinician judgment. The denoising-free self-supervised objective scales with compute and data in a way the smaller predecessors did not test.
