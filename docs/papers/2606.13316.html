ReSum: Synergizing LLM Reasoning and Summarization with Reinforcement Learning

Wang, Ma, Wang, Yang, Huang, Li, Wang, Chu · arXiv 2026-06-11 · arXiv:2606.13316 · Appears in: Reasoning

What

ReSum is a reinforcement-learning method that trains a language model to compress its own reasoning trace through self-summarization. During training with verifiable rewards, the model learns when to stop, write a short summary of the work so far, and continue from that summary instead of the full transcript. A summarization-aware adaptive rollout creates contrastive branches (masking the summary phrase where the model produced one, injecting it where it did not) and a summarization-aware advantage compares them. Reported results are an average accuracy gain of 4 percent with an 18.6 percent reduction in rollout length.

Why

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is the standard way to train long-horizon reasoning, but it tends to reward ever longer rollouts. Long traces exhaust the context budget and let an early error propagate through the rest of the chain. Prior fixes for long-context organization rely on external scaffolds that manage the rollout from outside the model, rather than letting the model manage its own trajectory. The paper's goal is to give the model an internal, learned way to keep its reasoning short and coherent.

How

Pilot studies show that self-summarization lowers token-level entropy and that inserting a summarization phrase reduces errors carried from a wrong prefix. ReSum builds on this: when the model spontaneously summarizes, the method masks that phrase to form a contrastive branch; at non-summarization points it randomly injects the phrase to form a matched branch. A summarization-aware advantage then scores whether summarizing helped, and the RLVR update rewards summarization only when it improves the trajectory.

Builds on

  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)Established that RLVR alone can drive long-horizon reasoning; ReSum keeps that setup and adds length control.
  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023)Origin of explicit step-by-step reasoning that ReSum learns to compress.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 produces long reasoning chains but has no mechanism to bound their length, so the context budget fills and early mistakes propagate. ReSum fixes that specific failure mode by teaching the model to summarize and restart from the summary, cutting rollout length 18.6 percent while raising accuracy 4 percent. It addresses the "trace grows without limit" problem that DeepSeek-R1 leaves open.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to DeepSeek-R1's outcome-reward RLVR, ReSum adds a learned control action (self-summarization) and a contrastive advantage that isolates the value of that action, rather than changing the reward itself. Compared to Chain-of-Thought, which fixes a single forward natural-language trace, ReSum lets the model re-anchor its trace partway through. This extends the DeepSeek-R1 to Chain-of-Thought bridge in the library (reasoning-via-RL versus prompting) toward learned management of reasoning length.
