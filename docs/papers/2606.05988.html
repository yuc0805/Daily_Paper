Compress-Distill: Reasoning Trace Compression for Efficient Knowledge Distillation

Griot, Scotti, Abraham · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM

What

This paper compresses chain-of-thought reasoning traces from large teacher models (Qwen3.5-397B, gpt-oss-120B) to 8.6-21.0% of their original length before distilling into smaller students. Compressed traces cut training tokens to 12-30%, speed up training by 2.0-7.6x, and shorten inference outputs by 3-19x while retaining up to 96% of raw-trace accuracy.

Why

Reasoning models trained with RL (such as DeepSeek-R1) produce long chain-of-thought traces. Using these traces directly as training data for knowledge distillation is expensive in both compute and storage. Prior distillation work assumed raw traces were the only option; this paper asks whether intelligent compression can preserve reasoning structure while reducing cost.

How

Two teacher models each generate roughly 283k correct reasoning traces. Two instruction-tuned compressor models then reduce these traces to 8.6-21.0% of their character length. A 48-run grid evaluates student accuracy across model sizes, with additional truncation ablations to isolate compression quality from length reduction. The key finding is that model-compressed traces outperform length-matched naive truncation, especially at smaller student scales.

Builds on

  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023)Established chain-of-thought as the dominant reasoning format, creating the long traces this paper compresses.
  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)Trained models via RL to produce extended reasoning traces, creating the distillation cost problem this paper addresses.

Solves vs predecessors

CoT prompting and DeepSeek-R1 focused on generating longer, more detailed reasoning to improve accuracy. This creates a cost problem downstream: distilling those long traces into smaller models is expensive. Compress-Distill shows that model-based compression can reduce this cost substantially (up to 18x per-token efficiency) while preserving most of the reasoning quality, something naive truncation cannot achieve.

Method vs predecessors

Where DeepSeek-R1 distilled raw reasoning traces directly, Compress-Distill inserts a compression step between teacher generation and student training. The compressor is itself an instruction-tuned LLM that learns to keep structurally important reasoning steps while discarding redundant elaboration. This is different from simply shortening the trace — the compressor preserves logical dependencies that truncation would sever.
