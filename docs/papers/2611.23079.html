A Unified Choice Model of News Selection: Experimental Evidence on Stance, Rank, and Engagement Cues

Jacopo D'Ignazi, Andreas Kaltenbrunner, Michele Starnini, Vicenç Gómez · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.23079

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Social media platforms often display visual engagement cues alongside ranked news articles, yet their influence on clicks remains poorly understood when considered jointly with ranking position and users' political preferences. We propose a unified discrete choice framework using a conditional multinomial logit model to jointly estimate and compare the influence of these factors on news selection. We evaluate the model using 1,480 choices from 370 human participants across four news topics in a controlled study with and without visual engagement cues. The framework allows us to assess the incremental contribution of each factor through nested-model likelihood-ratio tests. We find that including visual engagement cues significantly improves model fit across all topics after accounting for rank and stance. Engagement cues alone also predict clicks better than either of these features, although the full model achieves the highest held-out log-likelihood. Finally, we quantify via counterfactual analysis the impact of ranking position and displayed engagement counts on click probabilities. These findings can inform platform owners, researchers, and policymakers evaluating how platform design affects the selection of political news.
