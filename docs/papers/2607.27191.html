Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies

Kirgis, Kapoor, Schwartz, Rabanser, Voudouris, Toner, Hadfield, Lazar et al. · arXiv 2026-07-29 · arXiv:2607.27191 · Appears in: Agent

What

The paper introduces "shadow evaluations": a frontier agent takes the central open-ended research question of a high-quality unpublished paper, and the paper's original authors grade the agent's output. On two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions, given six days and thousands of dollars of compute, agents completed all of the engineering without human help but could not make substantial progress on the research questions, and both outputs were unambiguously rejected by the authors. The authors name five recurring failure modes and reproduce the pattern with a second model and scaffold.

Why

Forecasts of fast AI progress assume agents will automate AI research, but the evidence is thin. Existing evaluations either test agents on narrow, verifiable tasks, which excludes open-ended research, or submit AI-generated papers to blind peer review, which is overstretched, stochastic, and of uneven quality. The paper's goal is a third measurement method that is grounded in a real research question and graded by the people who know the answer.

How

The authors take unpublished papers, extract the central open-ended research question, and give a frontier agent six days and a large compute budget to answer it. The original authors then grade the output against what genuine progress would look like. They run two case studies, catalog the failure modes, and repeat with a second model and scaffold as a robustness check. They release the expert reviews, survey responses, agent repositories, and logs.

Builds on

  • Agent AI: Surveying Multimodal Interaction (Durante 2024)Maps what agents can do; this paper tests the harder open-research end of that map.
  • Rethinking Memory Mechanisms of Foundation Agents (Huang 2026)Surveys long-horizon agent behavior, which the failure modes here (backtracking, resource awareness, instruction drift) probe directly.

Solves vs predecessors

Prior evaluations measure either narrow verifiable tasks or noisy peer review, so neither captures whether an agent can drive an open research question. Shadow evaluations fix the failure mode "we cannot grade open-ended research reliably" by using the original authors as expert graders of a real, high-quality question, which yields a clear pass/fail with named reasons.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to benchmark-style agent evaluations, this uses a single deep, open question graded by experts rather than many shallow verifiable tasks. Compared to peer-review-based evaluation, it removes the review-quality and throughput problems by fixing the grader as the person who posed the question, and it releases the full trace so the judgment can be inspected.
