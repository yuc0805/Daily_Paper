CogOmniControl: Reasoning-Driven Controllable Video Generation via Creative Intent Cognition

arXiv 2026-05-19 · arXiv:2605.19995 · Appears in: Generative CV

What

CogOmniControl factorizes controllable video generation into two stages: first infer the user's creative intent through a reasoning step, then generate the video conditioned on that inferred intent. The split aims to give more faithful control when the prompt is abstract or underspecified, rather than mapping a control signal straight to pixels.

Why

Controllable video generation usually maps a control signal directly to output, which struggles when the user's intent is not fully spelled out. A diffusion backbone follows the condition it is given but has no stage that decides what an underspecified prompt should mean, so abstract prompts produce weak or off-target control. The paper's motivation is to add that missing interpretation step.

How

The framework runs a creative-intent cognition stage that reasons about what the user wants, producing an explicit intent representation, and then conditions the video generator on that representation. The generation stage uses a diffusion-based backbone; the new part is the reasoning stage placed in front of it.

Builds on

  • DiT (Peebles 2023)Diffusion-transformer backbone for scalable generation that CogOmniControl conditions on inferred intent.
  • DDPM (Ho 2020)The denoising diffusion process underlying the generation stage.
  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023)Step-before-acting reasoning, applied here to generation control instead of text answers.

Solves vs predecessors

DiT and DDPM generate directly from a conditioning signal and do not decide what an abstract prompt should mean, so underspecified control is mapped poorly. CogOmniControl fixes that by inferring intent first, giving the generator a concrete target even when the prompt is vague.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to DiT and DDPM, which map condition to output through denoising, CogOmniControl inserts a reasoning stage ahead of generation, borrowing the step-before-acting idea from Chain-of-Thought. The generative backbone is familiar; the contribution is the cognition stage that produces an explicit intent before any pixels are generated.
