Learning to Foresee: Unveiling the Unlocking Efficiency of On-Policy Distillation

Cai et al. (Tencent Hunyuan) · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM

What

This paper provides the first mechanistic explanation of why on-policy distillation (OPD) is more sample-efficient than off-policy distillation for LLM post-training. It shows OPD achieves "foresight" — establishing stable update trajectories toward the final model from early in training — at both the module-allocation and update-direction levels.

Why

On-policy distillation is widely used for LLM post-training but its efficiency advantage over off-policy methods has lacked a parameter-level explanation. Understanding why it works is necessary for designing even more efficient training methods, rather than relying on empirical recipe tuning.

How

The paper analyzes parameter update dynamics across training. At the module-allocation level, OPD identifies low-marginal-utility regions early and concentrates updates on reasoning-critical modules. At the update-direction level, OPD produces updates with stronger low-rank concentration whose dominant subspaces align closely with the final update subspace from early training. The paper proposes cheaply predicting OPD-aligned update directions to accelerate convergence further.

Builds on

Solves vs predecessors

Prior work attributed OPD's advantage to denser and more stable supervision from the teacher model. This paper shows the real mechanism is structural: OPD creates an early alignment between the current update direction and the final update direction, which off-policy distillation does not achieve because its training distribution drifts from the student's.

Method vs predecessors

Where standard distillation analysis compares loss curves, this paper analyzes the singular value decomposition of parameter updates across training, comparing subspace alignment between early and final checkpoints. This SVD-based analysis methodology could apply to understanding training dynamics in other settings.
