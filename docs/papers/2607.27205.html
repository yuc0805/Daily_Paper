TurboVLA: Real-Time Vision-Language-Action Model at 32 Hz on an RTX 4090 with under 1 GB VRAM

H-EmbodVis · arXiv 2026-07 · arXiv:2607.27205 · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

TurboVLA reports a vision-language-action policy that runs at 32 Hz on a single consumer RTX 4090 while using under 1 GB of GPU memory. The contribution is on the deployment side of the VLA line of work rather than new task capability: the aim is real-time closed-loop control on commodity hardware.

Why

Prior open vision-language-action models are accurate but too heavy and too slow for closed-loop control on commodity hardware, which keeps them off real robots. Control has to close the loop many times per second, so latency and memory, not only accuracy, decide whether a policy is usable. The paper is motivated by making a VLA small and fast enough to run in real time without a datacenter GPU.

How

The paper targets the inference budget directly, reporting 32 Hz throughput and a sub-1 GB memory footprint on an RTX 4090. The headline result is the combination of real-time rate and small memory, which places the policy in the regime that on-device and edge inference need. (Method-level detail beyond the reported efficiency figures is summarized from the announcement; see the paper for the full architecture.)

Solves vs predecessors

Flamingo answered questions about an image but did not act, and open VLA successors that added an action head are too heavy for real-time control. TurboVLA targets that specific gap: it keeps the vision-language-plus-action structure but makes inference fit a hard latency and memory budget, so the policy can close a control loop on a consumer GPU.

Method vs predecessors

The novelty relative to the vision-language line is not the input encoder but the compression and scheduling that let the policy run in real time within sub-1 GB memory. Where Flamingo and its descendants scaled up for capability, TurboVLA scales down for deployment, trading model size for a usable inference rate.
