Can Verifiable PIR Match the Online Efficiency of SimplePIR?

Xinxuan Zhang, Ruida Wang, Zirui Wang, Yuming Guo et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.22911

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Verifiable Private Information Retrieval (vPIR) ensures that a malicious server cannot return tampered database records, but it traditionally incurs significant overhead compared to classical PIR.Recent advancements, notably VeriSimplePIR, approach the online efficiency of SimplePIR by introducing a proof preprocessing phase and requiring only lightweight client-side online verification. However, this scheme relies on an additional Short Integer Solution (SIS) assumption and SIS-based commitments, which only support relaxed range proofs. Consequently, these schemes are forced to adopt significantly weaker parameter choices, resulting in additional online overhead. In this paper, we propose a novel vPIR protocol that employs a newly designed error-correcting code-based commitment scheme as the database digest. This approach (i)supports efficient proofs of linear relations, (ii) achieves precise range proofs and (iii) eliminates the reliance on the SIS assumption. Thus, our scheme successfully adopts parameter choices identical to those of SimplePIR. As a result, our vPIR protocol is the first to achieve exactly the same online communication and server computation costs as SimplePIR, while keeping online client computation nearly identical. Additionally, compared to VeriSimplePIR,our protocol reduces online communication and client-side computation by approximately 20%, and decreases online server-side computation by over 35%.
