Symmetric Structure and Motivic Transformation in Melodies, Phrases, and Themes

Alfredo González-Espinoza · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2610.40303

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Transposition, inversion, and retrograde are fundamental operations in music theory, from Schoenberg's twelve-tone technique to Forte's set classes and Lewin's transformational theory, yet their use in melodies has rarely been quantified. We read a melodic line through the orbits and stabilizers of its windows of $m = 3-6$ notes under the group generated by these operations and cyclic shift. The window entropy splits into an orbit entropy (motivic vocabulary) and a within-orbit entropy normalized by orbit size (deployment), and the group elements relating windows of one orbit give the motivic transformations. In 852 keyboard pieces spanning four centuries, at least 97% of melodies have lower orbit entropy than surrogates preserving their pitch content, spectrum, or one-step transitions ($m \geq 4$). Periods differ in vocabulary and symmetry types; musical forms differ in deployment. Recurring motifs are transposed 1.6 times more often than in random permutations of their forms, and transposed by a fifth or fourth 2.8 times more often, a preference that weakens from the Baroque to the 20th century and from Bach's fugues to Shostakovich's. Folk phrases and instrumental themes are rarely symmetric (2-10%), but are 1.6-5 times more often symmetric than note rearrangements. The framework extends to any symbolic sequence with a symmetry group.
