TS-Agent: A Time Series Reasoning Agent with Iterative Statistical Insight Gathering

Liu, Fons, Vyetrenko, Borrajo, Potluru, Veloso · arXiv 2025-10-08 · arXiv:2510.07432 · zotero_key: I2CIT4I7 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

TS-Agent is an LLM-driven agent for reasoning over raw numeric time series. It does not encode the series as text tokens, images, or learned embeddings; instead, the LLM acts as a controller that calls a fixed set of atomic time-series analysis operators (e.g. statistical summaries, change-point detection, decomposition), accumulates their outputs in an explicit evidence log, and iterates with a self-critic loop and a final quality gate before emitting an answer.

Why

Recent work shows that frontier LLMs perform poorly on time-series reasoning: numeric inputs are tokenised inefficiently, and benchmark answers are often produced by hallucination or knowledge leakage from training data rather than genuine analysis. Multi-modal alignment approaches (Time-LLM, ChatTS, Sensor2Text) try to fix this by training a series-to-language bridge, but this requires paired data, costs training compute, loses the native numeric form, and is hard to verify after deployment. The paper's motivation is to obtain time-series reasoning capability with no alignment training and with auditable intermediate reasoning steps.

How

The agent operates in an observe-think-act loop. At each step the LLM chooses an operator from a fixed library, the operator runs on the raw numeric input, and the result is written to an evidence log that the LLM can read on subsequent steps. A self-critic prompt periodically asks whether the current evidence is sufficient and consistent; if not, the agent issues another operator call. A final quality gate validates the answer against the evidence before output. Empirically the system matches state-of-the-art LLMs on understanding benchmarks and outperforms them on reasoning benchmarks where memorisation is insufficient.

Solves vs predecessors

Chain-of-Thought (Wei 2023) showed that prompting an LLM to produce intermediate reasoning steps improves performance on arithmetic and commonsense tasks, but the reasoning happens entirely in natural-language token space. On time series this fails because the LLM has no reliable way to extract numerical features from a raw sequence by reading it as text. TS-Agent fixes the specific failure mode — "the model invents statistical claims that are not in the data" — by forcing every numerical claim to come from an operator call recorded in the evidence log, so the reasoning chain becomes verifiable rather than purely generative.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to Chain-of-Thought, TS-Agent replaces the single-pass natural-language reasoning trace with an iterative tool-using loop, and adds two control mechanisms that CoT lacks: a self-critic that decides whether more evidence is needed, and a quality gate that validates the answer against the evidence log. Compared to time-series-LLM alignment work in the same area (e.g. Time-LLM, ChatTS, Sensor2Text — all in your library), TS-Agent moves the integration boundary: rather than training a model to see the series, it gives an off-the-shelf LLM tools that see the series. This avoids alignment training and preserves the native numeric representation.
