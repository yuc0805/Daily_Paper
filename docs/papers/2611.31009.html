Origins of City Reputation: Measuring Trust and Bias in Online Reviews Using Agentic AI and NLP

Rafiazka Hilman, Júlia Koltai · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.31009

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Online reviews aggregate millions of individual judgments into signals that shape where people eat, stay and travel to, and increasingly read them as evidence of the reputation of places. Nevertheless, whether such reputations really reflect the places or more the instruments used to measure them is unclear. Here we compare two instruments for measuring trust and bias in 100,000 Yelp reviews from ten US cities: a large language model based agentic system including an evaluator critic loop, and a corpus-based tool built on pretrained classifiers, lexicons and rule-based features. Our results show that the two instruments agree on credibility, but not at all on plausibility. They also respond to different use of words and phrases. The corpus-based better flagged complaints and generalizations, whereas the agent better quoted references to social groups. We also found that with five examples, the agent treats ordinary superlatives as bias far more often than with none or one. Aggregated to cities, both instruments find little variation as less than half a percent of the variance of review level reputation lies between cities. This small differences between cities mostly arise from different levels of trust (in case of the agentic tool) and from bias (in case of the corpus-based tool), and the two instruments rank the cities differently. Reputation derived from platform reviews therefore depends on how the reviews are processed and analyzed. As platforms delegate this to language models, the definitions and examples built into these models become part of how places are valued.
