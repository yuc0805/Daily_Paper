Mamoda2.5: Enhancing a Unified Multimodal Model with DiT-MoE

Shi, Zhu, Shen et al. (ByteDance) · arXiv 2026-05-04 · arXiv:2605.02641 · Appears in: Generative CV

What

Mamoda2.5 is a single AR-Diffusion architecture for both multimodal understanding and generation. Its generation backbone is a Diffusion Transformer equipped with a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts layer (128 experts, top-8 routing), producing a 25B-parameter model that activates only 3B parameters per step. It reaches top-tier scores on VBench 2.0 and a video-editing record on OpenVE-Bench, and a joint few-step distillation plus reinforcement-learning stage compresses a 30-step editing model to 4 steps, reported as up to 95.9 times faster inference.

Why

Unified multimodal models want both broad capacity and affordable training and inference, but a dense diffusion backbone pays the full parameter cost on every step. Scaling such a model up makes training expensive and inference slow, which is a problem for deployed video editing. The paper aims to raise capacity without paying dense compute, and to cut the many-step sampling cost that makes diffusion editing slow.

How

The Diffusion Transformer backbone is made sparse with a fine-grained MoE layer: 128 experts with top-8 routing, so only 3B of 25B parameters fire per step. Understanding and generation share one architecture through an AR-Diffusion formulation. For speed, a joint few-step distillation and reinforcement-learning stage compresses a 30-step editor to 4 steps. The result matches strong proprietary systems on VBench 2.0 and OpenVE-Bench and reports up to 95.9 times faster editing inference than open-source baselines, with a 98% success rate in an internal advertising-editing deployment.

Builds on

  • DiT (Peebles 2023)Transformer diffusion backbone with clean scaling; Mamoda2.5 makes it sparse with expert routing.
  • DDPM (Ho 2020)The underlying iterative denoising process the backbone runs.

Solves vs predecessors

DiT scaled diffusion cleanly but with a dense transformer, so every parameter is active on every denoising step. Mamoda2.5 fixes that compute-per-step problem with sparse expert routing, activating a fraction of parameters while keeping nominal capacity high. It also addresses a cost DiT and DDPM left untouched: the many-step sampling loop, which the distillation-plus-RL stage cuts to four steps.

Method vs predecessors

The structural change from DiT is the MoE layer inside the transformer, turning a dense backbone into a conditionally sparse one. The denoising mechanism is unchanged from DDPM in principle. The inference-acceleration recipe (few-step distillation combined with reinforcement learning) is new relative to both parents and is what delivers the large speedups.
