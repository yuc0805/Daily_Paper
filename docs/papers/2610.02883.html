Structural crossover of complex networks: bridging degree correlation and fractality

Shogo Mizutaka, Jun Yamamoto, Kousuke Yakubo · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2610.02883

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

We investigate the relationship between long-range degree correlations and fractality in scale-free random networks. By analyzing degree correlations in scale-free random networks at the percolation threshold, at which a giant component emerges, we derive the ball volume $\tildeν_k(l)$, defined as the average number of nodes within distance $l$ from a root node with degree $k$. The resulting expression predicts a degree-dependent structural crossover. For $l\ll k^{1/(\df-1)}$, the ball volume grows as $\tildeν_k(l)\sim kl$, whereas for $l\gg k^{1/(\df-1)}$, it crosses over to the global fractal scaling $\tildeν_k(l)\sim l^{\df}$. Here, $\df$ is the fractal dimension of critical scale-free random networks. We further show that the structural crossover can be naturally understood in terms of critical branching processes and is also observed in empirical fractal networks.
