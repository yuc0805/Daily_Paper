PerceptionDLM: Parallel Region Perception with Multimodal Diffusion Language Models

PerceptionDLM authors · arXiv 2606.19534 2026 · link · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

PerceptionDLM is a multimodal diffusion language model that describes several image regions in parallel rather than one token at a time. It uses structured attention masking so multiple masked regions are decoded together at the sequence and token level. The base model outperforms LLaDA-V on 15 of 16 multimodal benchmarks and stays competitive with autoregressive vision-language models (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) at the same scale. A new benchmark, ParaDLC-Bench, scores caption quality and inference speed jointly.

Why

Most multimodal models generate text one token at a time, which is slow when a task asks for captions of many image regions at once. Sequential decoding wastes time re-running the model per region, and existing benchmarks score caption quality without accounting for the throughput cost. The paper targets the case where a single image needs many region answers together.

How

A diffusion language model decodes text by denoising masked tokens rather than left-to-right. PerceptionDLM adds efficient prompting and structured attention masking so several masked regions are denoised in parallel at both the sequence and token level. ParaDLC-Bench scales an earlier localized-captioning benchmark to multiple region masks per image and reports caption quality and speed together.

Solves vs predecessors

Autoregressive multimodal models decode regions one after another; PerceptionDLM decodes them in parallel, which fixes the throughput problem on multi-region perception while keeping caption quality competitive (15 of 16 benchmarks beat LLaDA-V). Diffusion here is used for decoding speed rather than image fidelity, the use it had in DiT and DDPM.

Method vs predecessors

Where DiT and DDPM apply diffusion to image generation, PerceptionDLM applies diffusion to the language side of a multimodal model, so the parallel-decoding property of diffusion buys speed on a text-output perception task. The vision-language encoder line traces to SigLIP, which is not in the library; the closest tracked neighbor is DALL-E 2 on the text-to-vision side.
