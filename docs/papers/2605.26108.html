Reinforcing Few-step Generators via Reward-Tilted Distribution Matching

Huang, Zhou, Wang, Pang et al. · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: Generative CV

What

RTDMD is a two-stage method that aligns few-step image generators with human preferences. It shows that minimizing KL divergence to a reward-tilted teacher distribution splits into a distribution-matching term and a reward-maximization term. With 4 inference steps it sets new state of the art on SD3, SD3.5, and FLUX.2 across preference, aesthetic, and compositional metrics; the distilled FLUX.2 4B beats the 9B teacher at 50 steps on most rewards.

Why

Few-step diffusion distillation makes image generation efficient, but distilled models are hard to align with human preferences: the speed-up discards the iterative sampling that preference tuning relied on. Combining distillation with reward optimization is unstable because the fake score model must track a generator that shifts under very few updates.

How

Stage one, Ambient-Consistent Distribution Matching Distillation, does subinterval-wise matching and adds a consistency regularizer so the fake score tracks the shifting generator. Stage two jointly optimizes both terms with a hybrid policy gradient: a GRPO-style estimator for stochastic intermediate transitions plus direct reward backprop through the deterministic final step, with step-subset GRPO (SubGRPO) to reduce variance.

Builds on

  • DDPM (Ho 2020)Defined the iterative denoising process that distillation compresses to four steps.
  • DiT (Peebles 2023)Made the diffusion backbone a scalable transformer, the class of generator distilled here.

Solves vs predecessors

DDPM produced high quality but needed many steps; prior few-step distillation recovered speed but lost human-preference alignment. RTDMD fixes the alignment failure mode by deriving a reward-tilted objective that keeps distribution matching while adding reward maximization, so a 4-step generator stays preference-aligned rather than degrading.

Method vs predecessors

Earlier distillation matched the teacher distribution alone; earlier reward tuning assumed a full sampler. RTDMD unifies both by tilting the teacher with a reward and splitting the resulting KL, then handling the mixed stochastic-deterministic short sampler with a hybrid gradient. This is what lets the distilled model beat its larger, slower teacher on most reward metrics.
