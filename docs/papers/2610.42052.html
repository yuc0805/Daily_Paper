Valid GMM Inference with Non-parametrically Generated Discrete Type

Taegyu Yang · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2610.42052

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

The recent progress in machine learning and classification methods has enabled researchers to predict economic discrete attributes that are not observed in standard datasets. These range from missing demographics, such as ethnicity and poverty status, to economic categories, such as innovative firms, metropolitan areas, occupational groups, binary treatment status, and counterfactual status. Although the plug-in procedure is widely used in empirical applications, nonparametric first-stage estimation recovering the labels can introduce regularization bias because of its slow convergence rate. Even if the double/debiased machine learning approach provides a way to correct the regularization bias, the according bias-correction term is not well defined in our problem, because even a small first-stage error can change the type discontinuously. To address this problem, we smooth the discontinuous type indicators using a smooth kernel function and a shrinking tuning parameter $b$. We then construct a well-defined Neyman-orthogonal score and define the appropriate debiased estimator. We show that this debiased estimator satisfies $\sqrt{Nb}$-consistency and asymptotic normality under regularity conditions. The slower $\sqrt{Nb}$ convergence rate is the cost of correcting the regularization bias when the nonparametric first-stage learner enters the moment function through discontinuous functions.
