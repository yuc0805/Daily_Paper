Testing Density Discontinuity Using Spacings

William T. Firmin · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.16199

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

In the regression discontinuity design, researchers assess the credibility of assumptions by testing the running variable's density for a discontinuity at the cutoff. This paper proposes a novel such test. The test uses q spacings on each side of the cutoff, where spacings are the widths between ordered observations. Spacings yield a continuous statistic that behaves well under weak assumptions. I study its properties in two asymptotic frameworks: one where q is fixed as the sample size increases and one where q slowly diverges to infinity. With q fixed, the test achieves exact asymptotic size control without user randomization. When q increases slowly, the test becomes consistent without sacrificing exact asymptotic size. Compared to the preferred version of Bugni and Canay's (2021) test, the only other test with comparably weak assumptions, this test reduces sensitivity to the tuning parameter and performs similarly when theirs is well-chosen.
