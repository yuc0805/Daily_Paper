On Information Self-Locking in Reinforcement Learning for Active Reasoning of LLM Agents

Zou, Chen, Feng, Li, Gong, Cheng · arXiv 2026-03-12 · arXiv:2603.12109 · Appears in: Agent

What

The paper identifies and fixes "information self-locking" in reinforcement-learning training of question-asking agents. Under self-locking the agent stops asking informative questions and stops internalizing evidence it already has, settling into a low-information loop. The authors decompose active reasoning into action selection (which queries to issue) and belief tracking (how to update from answers), show that weakness in either one starves exploration during training, and inject cheap directional critiques to break the loop, which measurably reduces self-locking.

Why

Reinforcement learning has improved single-pass reasoning, but multi-turn agents that must gather information behave differently: a weak policy explores little, little exploration gives a weak training signal, and the two reinforce each other. Prior reasoning-RL work does not address this interactive trap.

How

The authors analyze the feedback loop between action selection and belief tracking and show each limits the other during training. Their remedy reallocates the learning signal by adding easy-to-obtain directional critiques that tell the agent which direction of behavior would raise information gain, pulling it out of the locked regime without an expensive reward redesign.

Builds on

  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)Trains reasoning through reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards; the single-pass case this paper extends to multi-turn information gathering.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 shows reinforcement learning can produce strong reasoning in a single pass, where the reward applies to a final answer. In the interactive setting that signal collapses exploration: the agent stops querying and stops updating. This paper fixes that specific failure mode by reallocating the learning signal with directional critiques, restoring exploration so action selection and belief tracking can improve together.

Method vs predecessors

Compared with DeepSeek-R1, the work keeps reinforcement learning as the training method but shifts the target from one-shot answers to multi-turn evidence gathering, and adds a diagnosis (the action-selection / belief-tracking loop) plus a lightweight critique signal that R1-style outcome rewards lack.
