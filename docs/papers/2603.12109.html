On Information Self-Locking in Reinforcement Learning for Active Reasoning of LLM agents

Zou, Chen et al. · arXiv 2026-03 · arXiv:2603.12109 · Appears in: Agent

What

The paper identifies "information self-locking": LLM agents trained with outcome-reward reinforcement learning stop asking informative questions and fail to use evidence they already hold in active-reasoning settings. It decomposes active reasoning into Action Selection, which determines the observation stream through queries, and Belief Tracking, which updates the agent's belief from collected evidence, and explains how weak versions of both trap the agent in a low-information state.

Why

Reinforcement learning with outcome rewards trains strong reasoning on closed tasks, but active reasoning is different: the agent must ask questions to gather information before it can answer. Outcome rewards do not credit the act of asking a good question, so the agent has no direct signal to keep exploring. Prior RL reasoning work does not explain why agents collapse to non-informative behavior in this setting.

How

The authors analyze the two capabilities separately. They show that deficient Action Selection and Belief Tracking limit information exploration during RL training, and that insufficient exploration in turn prevents either capability from improving. This creates a feedback loop that locks the agent in a low-information regime. The decomposition gives a diagnosis of where the standard training recipe breaks rather than a single new reward term.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 produces strong reasoning when the full problem is given up front. This paper addresses the failure that appears when the problem is partly hidden and the agent must query for the rest: outcome-reward RL then locks into non-informative behavior. The contribution is naming and decomposing that failure, which DeepSeek-R1 style training does not surface.

Method vs predecessors

Where LATS bolts on a search procedure to force exploration, this paper keeps the plain RL agent and analyzes the coupled dynamics of Action Selection and Belief Tracking, showing that both must improve together. The approach is diagnostic and structural rather than a new search algorithm or reward shaping recipe.
