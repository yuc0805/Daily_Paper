Foundation Models Defining A New Era In Sensor-based Human Activity Recognition: A Survey And Outlook

survey authors · arXiv 2026 (ACM IMWUT, conditional) · arXiv:2604.02711 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

This survey reviews 132 papers on foundation models for sensor-based human activity recognition and organizes the field with a lifecycle taxonomy across input design, pretraining, adaptation, and utilization, structured along nine technical and operational axes. It names three dominant directions: HAR-specific foundation models built from scratch, general time-series and multimodal foundation models adapted to sensor data, and large language models used for reasoning, annotation, and human-AI interaction. It lists open challenges in data curation, multimodal alignment, on-device personalization, and responsible deployment.

Why

Foundation models for sensor HAR have grown quickly but without a shared structure to compare input choices, pretraining objectives, and adaptation strategies. Earlier HAR surveys predate the foundation-model shift. The paper sets out to give one taxonomy and a curated reference base so the field can be navigated and compared.

How

The authors collect 132 representative papers and place each on a lifecycle of input design, pretraining, adaptation, and utilization, with nine axes. They group the work into the three directions above and use the taxonomy to read off where the open problems sit. A companion repository indexes the surveyed models.
