Decision Columns as an Index: Interpretable Embeddings for Explainable Entity Matching and Search

Aadharsh Kannan, Robert Bruckner, Philip A. Bernstein · 2026 · arXiv · arXiv:2611.27910

Why recommended

Very recent in your research areas.

Abstract

Business data systems must determine whether records refer to the same entity and retrieve records satisfying requests whose decisive properties are implicit. As agents act on these records, both tasks require auditable reasons. Dense embeddings perform well but offer opaque coordinates, cannot be filtered by meaning, and require corpus re-encoding to add new properties. We propose interpretable embeddings built from decision columns: each column stores a decision model's calibrated answer to a natural-language question, with outcomes yes, no, or not stated. A record's embedding is its answers across an ordered set of questions. Similarity decomposes exactly into per-question contributions, and new questions add coordinates without altering existing ones. On public product benchmarks, question-space retrieval matches dense candidate generation statistically in a retrieve-then-verify entity matcher, while providing exact explanations for accepted matches. Questions derived from retrieval failures triple mean reciprocal rank without re-encoding prior answers. For search, inferring the attributes of a suitable result outperforms encoding only the literal request, and combining question-space and dense scores improves retrieval, though not at our prespecified confidence threshold. A single small neural network predicts the full interpretable vector from an existing dense embedding, enabling corpus backfilling from a few thousand labeled rows at roughly 1/200 the cost. We position interpretable embeddings as a complementary, readable retrieval channel for explaining matches, filtering on attributes, and extending indexes as failures expose missing facts.
