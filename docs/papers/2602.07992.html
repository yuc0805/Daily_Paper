When Is Compositional Reasoning Learnable from Verifiable Rewards?

Barzilai, Wolf, Basri · arXiv 2026-02 · arXiv:2602.07992 · Appears in: Reasoning

What

The paper is a theoretical study of which compositional reasoning problems a language model can learn under reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where the only feedback is a verifier's check on the final answer. It defines the "task-advantage ratio", a joint property of the problem and the base model, and uses it to characterize learnable versus non-learnable cases.

Why

RLVR has driven much of the recent gain in step-by-step reasoning, but it remains unclear which compositional problems are actually learnable from outcome-level feedback alone. Empirical results (for example DeepSeek-R1) show RLVR works, yet they do not say when it will fail. Without a condition that separates the two regimes, practitioners cannot predict whether outcome-only rewards will reinforce correct intermediate steps or settle on a shortcut.

How

The authors model an autoregressive policy that performs sequential task selection at each chain-of-thought step and receives reward only at the end. They prove that when correct intermediate steps clearly raise the probability of a verified answer, the task-advantage ratio is favorable and the problem is efficiently learnable. When that structural advantage is absent, RLVR can converge to a suboptimal composition, and in some cases the base model's quality alone determines whether the advantage exists at all.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that outcome-only RL can extend reasoning ability, but it did not explain the conditions under which the gain appears or fails. This paper fixes the gap by isolating the structural property a task must have for outcome-level feedback to reinforce correct intermediate steps, turning an empirical observation into a checkable condition.

Method vs predecessors

Where Chain-of-Thought and DeepSeek-R1 are empirical, this work is analytical: it abstracts RLVR into sequential task selection and derives the task-advantage ratio as the deciding quantity. It extends the reasoning-via-RL versus prompting bridge in the library (DeepSeek-R1 to Chain-of-Thought) by giving a formal boundary between problems where RL with verifiers succeeds and where it converges to a suboptimal solution.
