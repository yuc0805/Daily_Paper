Retweeting patterns reveal partisan asymmetries in information sharing and signal state-level electoral outcomes in the 2020 US presidential election

Dawei Li, Qinjun Wan, Xin Wang, Dahe Jiang et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.12384

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

As social media has become a primary source of political information, concerns are growing that online information consumption may intensify political polarization and reshape democratic processes. Yet how large-scale online information consumption organizes polarized political ecosystems and relates to offline voting remains unclear. Here we reconstruct partisan information ecosystems from more than 500 million tweets generated during the 2020 US presidential election, integrating information diffusion across media outlets, influencers, and social peers. Contrary to the conventional expectation that broader information exposure promotes moderation, we find that retweeting a wider range of political information is associated with stronger political polarization. We further show systematic asymmetries in political information ecosystems: the Republican ecosystem is highly concentrated and ideologically biased, whereas the Democratic ecosystem is more dispersed and primarily organized around relatively moderate left-leaning sources. Finally, we demonstrate that several shared retweeting signatures robustly map onto state-level electoral outcomes despite cross-state heterogeneity. Our findings reveal asymmetric political information ecosystems as a key organizing principle of online political communication and identify collective retweeting patterns as a scalable signal of real-world voting behaviours.
