Source Detection in Complex Networks: Observability, Identifiability, and Inference Frameworks

Xiu-Xiu Zhan, Qiao Ke, Chengjun Zhang, Miao He et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.34652

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Tracing diffusion to its origin is a central inverse problem in complex networks, with applications ranging from epidemic investigation and information spreading to cyber incidents and disruptions in interconnected systems. Although source-detection methods have advanced along diverse methodological lines, their capabilities cannot be assessed independently of the evidence on which they operate. Network structure and diffusion dynamics determine which propagation histories are possible, while the observation mechanism determines which traces of those histories are retained. This review develops an information-centric and identifiability-aware perspective that connects these elements to source inference. We organize existing research around three overlapping observation settings: complete state snapshots, incomplete or noisy observations, and sensor-based observation and design. Within this organization, we examine structural, probabilistic, message-passing, simulation-based, and learning-based methods, comparing their required inputs, underlying assumptions, inference mechanisms, and computational demands. System-theoretic observability, statistical identifiability, and information-theoretic constraints provide complementary ways to examine what the available observations can reveal, while separating limitations of the evidence from those of estimation. Drawing on applications across epidemiological, information, biological, neural, and socioeconomic systems, we identify challenges in uncertainty assessment, model robustness, transfer across networks, and the acquisition of informative observations. This synthesis clarifies the conditions under which diffusion sources can be identified and how inference methods should be selected according to the available observations.
