Rethinking RL for LLM Reasoning: It is Sparse Policy Selection, Not Capability Learning

Akgül, Kannan, Neiswanger, Prasanna · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2605.06241 · Appears in: Reasoning

What

The paper measures, at the token level, what reinforcement learning actually changes in a reasoning model. Across several model families and RL algorithms it finds the effect is sparse: only 1 to 3 percent of token positions change, those positions sit at high-entropy decision points, and the promoted token almost always lies within the base model's top-5 candidates. RL therefore selects among solutions the base model already contains rather than teaching new ones.

Why

Reinforcement learning is widely described as the step that gives reasoning models new strategies, following results such as DeepSeek-R1. If that description is wrong, the field is spending large optimization budgets on something a cheaper, targeted method could approximate. The paper tests the "RL teaches capability" claim directly instead of accepting it from aggregate benchmark gains.

How

The authors compare base and RL-trained models token by token, locate the positions where outputs diverge, and characterize those positions by entropy and by the base model's candidate ranking. The pattern (sparse, high-entropy, within top-5) is consistent enough that they propose targeted corrections at those uncertain points as a substitute for a full RL loop.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated strong reasoning after RL but did not isolate where the gain comes from. This paper addresses that failure mode by localizing the change to a few uncertain decision points, turning a global "RL made it smarter" claim into a specific, measurable correction. It extends the graphify bridge between DeepSeek-R1 and Chain-of-Thought (reasoning-via-RL versus prompting) toward "RL as selection over what prompting could already reach."

Method vs predecessors

Where DeepSeek-R1 contributes a training method and Chain-of-Thought a prompting method, this paper contributes an analysis method: token-level divergence and entropy attribution. Its proposed alternative (sparse targeted correction) is lighter than full RL post-training.
