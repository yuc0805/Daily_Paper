RecField: Embedding Recommendations into Spatial Movie Organization as Affordances for Serendipitous Encounters

Kenta Oku · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.34330

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Research on serendipity-oriented recommender systems has focused on algorithms that recommend unexpected yet useful items. However, serendipity is increasingly viewed as a user experience shaped by both algorithmic outputs and system features. This study positions recommendations not as the primary goal users pursue but as an environmental affordance that creates opportunities for discovery during other activities. We propose RecField, a spatial movie organization interface where users freely arrange movies into collection zones. For each collection, RecField provides attribute-based recommendation lists labeled according to their recommendation perspectives. A between-subjects experiment ($N$ = 57) examined the effects of recommendations on discoveries made during a foreground task of creating and organizing personal movie profiles. Although recommendations did not significantly affect environment-level perceived serendipity, ratings of encounter-related aspects tended to be higher, with small effect sizes. At the individual-movie level, favorites in the recommendation condition received significantly higher ratings for stimulating the desire to watch and broadening preferences, but not for novelty or unexpectedness. Notably, only 14\% of evaluated favorites in the recommendation condition were added directly from recommendation lists. Differences in ratings showed similar effect sizes even when the analysis was restricted to favorites added through other routes. These findings suggest that embedded recommendations can contribute to serendipitous experiences both directly through item acceptance and indirectly by prompting users' own exploration and re-evaluation. They also highlight the value of evaluating serendipity at both the environment and item levels and the limitations of relying solely on direct recommendation acceptance rates.
