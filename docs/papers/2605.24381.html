Assessing the Operational Viability of Foundation Models for Time Series Forecasting

Soni, Das, Guduguntla (Google) · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

The paper measures whether large time-series foundation models earn their inference cost in production. It compares zero-shot foundation models against industry-standard supervised forecasters on real workloads and finds the trade-off depends on series complexity. It then proposes a Complexity Router that reads simple empirical features of each series and assigns it to the cheapest adequate model class.

Why

Foundation models promise zero-shot forecasting without task-specific training, which is attractive, but deploying one universal large model on every series can cost far more per forecast than a light supervised model, and prior work reports accuracy without this cost accounting. The open question is when the heavy model is actually worth it.

How

The Complexity Router classifies each series by empirical features and routes easy series to light supervised models while reserving foundation models for hard cases. The reported effect is higher overall accuracy and much lower inference cost than deploying a single universal foundation model across all series.

Solves vs predecessors

The survey and TS-Agent argued for larger language-model-style time-series systems but did not weigh deployment cost. This paper shows that a universal foundation model is often not worth its inference cost on easy series and recovers both accuracy and cost by routing, fixing the silent assumption that bigger is always better.

Method vs predecessors

Where prior work added model capacity and language integration, this paper adds a cheap feature-based router in front of a model zoo. The contribution is a systems mechanism (cost-aware selection), not a new forecaster, which is a different axis from the predecessors' modeling focus.
