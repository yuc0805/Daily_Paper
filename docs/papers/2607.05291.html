Forecasting Realized Volatility with Time Series Foundation Models: A Comparison with Econometric Benchmarks

arXiv 2026-07 · arXiv:2607.05291 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

The paper tests whether pretrained time-series foundation models beat classical econometric models at forecasting realized volatility. It finds that performance varies a lot across foundation-model architectures, and that picking the right architecture matters more than the broad choice between a foundation model and an econometric baseline.

Why

Time-series foundation models show strong zero-shot forecasting, but volatility is a hard, non-stationary target with a long econometric tradition. Whether a general pretrained forecaster actually improves on tuned statistical models here is an open question, and treating "foundation model" as one uniform option hides large differences between architectures.

How

The authors run several time-series foundation models zero-shot against econometric benchmarks on realized-volatility forecasting and compare accuracy. The takeaway is a ranking observation rather than a new method: architecture choice within the foundation-model family dominates the foundation-versus-econometric distinction, so a poorly matched foundation model can lose to a simple statistical model.

Solves vs predecessors

Chronos is presented as a general zero-shot forecaster, but this paper measures where that generality fails on a non-stationary financial target. It does not fix the failure; it documents it, which is the missing evidence for whether an off-the-shelf foundation model is the right base at all.

Method vs predecessors

Rather than propose a forecaster, the paper is an evaluation. It connects to the Post-Training framework by showing that the frozen model's architecture, not only its weights, sets the accuracy ceiling, which motivates the adaptation and output-processing families that framework catalogs and that DEFT (arXiv:2607.19659) instantiates.
