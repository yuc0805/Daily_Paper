Forecasting Realized Volatility with Time Series Foundation Models: A Comparison with Econometric Benchmarks

Brini · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2607.05291 · Appears in: Signal (Time Series)

What

The paper tests whether zero-shot time-series foundation models beat standard econometric volatility models. On the VOLARE dataset it compares nine zero-shot foundation models against eight econometric specifications, including the Heterogeneous Autoregressive family, across 50 assets in equities, foreign exchange, and futures and three horizons, with formal forecast-comparison tests.

Why

Realized-volatility forecasting has decades of well-specified econometric baselines, so it is a hard and fair test of whether general pretrained forecasters add value. Pooled loss can favor foundation models simply because a few volatile assets dominate the average, so the paper asks whether the advantage survives a per-asset accounting and a recalibration that removes scale bias.

How

The authors average each asset's loss ratio against a Log-HAR benchmark so no single asset dominates, apply a Mincer-Zarnowitz recalibration to strip level and scale bias, and run Model Confidence Set tests. Only Tiny Time Mixers beats the benchmark at every horizon, and narrowly; an equal-weight average of Tiny Time Mixers and Log-HAR enters the Model Confidence Set for 98 to 100 percent of assets.

Builds on

  • ChronosAnchors the zero-shot foundation-model group evaluated against the econometric baselines.
  • PatchTSTNeural supervised forecaster representing the model line the foundation models descend from.

Solves vs predecessors

General benchmarks let a few assets carry an apparent foundation-model win. This paper fixes that by averaging per-asset loss ratios and recalibrating, which reveals that much of the short-horizon gain was better scaling rather than better prediction of volatility dynamics.

Method vs predecessors

Where Chronos reported broad zero-shot performance, this work holds the task fixed (realized volatility) and varies the evaluation protocol, showing that architecture choice among foundation models matters more than the foundation-versus-econometric split. The methodological contribution is the averaging and recalibration procedure, not a new model.
