"I Just Don't Want to Be a Burden": Investigating AI Chatbot Emotional Support Among People with Suicidal Ideation and Other Mental Health Concerns

Vasudha Varadarajan, Nathan S. Fishbein, Andrew Aquilina, Jordan Taylor et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.23190

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

People experiencing high-acuity mental health concerns are turning to AI chatbots for emotional support, but little is known about what drives this use, their experiences, and their preferences. In a sequential mixed-methods study, we inductively analyzed Reddit posts and used the findings to develop a survey. We surveyed 202 adults with recent mental health concerns who had used AI for emotional support. Most participants were already receiving clinical care and reported turning to AI, most commonly because they did not want to burden others, rather than being driven by barriers to care. Benefits were broadly shared, but participants reporting past-year suicidal ideation described more harm-facilitating AI behavior and reliance than those without. Overall, participants ranked honesty above warmth, and preference for crisis escalation declined as clinical acuity rose. These findings motivate AI support that addresses perceived costs of disclosure, evaluates benefits alongside harms, and widens feasible paths to human connection.
