MicroWorld: Bridging the Microscopic Domain Gap for Multimodal LLMs

Anonymous et al. · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2605.10120 · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

MicroWorld addresses the failure of general multimodal LLMs on microscopy images, a visual domain far from natural photos. It builds a multimodal attributed property graph from large-scale scientific image-caption corpora and uses that graph to ground a multimodal LLM in microscopy-specific concepts and relations, so the model can describe and reason about microscopic structures it was not pretrained on.

Why

Multimodal LLMs are pretrained mostly on natural images and text, so they transfer poorly to specialist scientific domains where the visual statistics and vocabulary differ. Microscopy is a sharp case: cells, stains, and instruments look nothing like web photos. Simply adding more contrastive pretraining does not reliably close this gap, because the labeled scientific data is limited and the relevant knowledge is relational, not just perceptual.

How

MicroWorld constructs a multimodal attributed property graph from scientific image-caption corpora, encoding domain concepts and the relations among them. This graph grounds the multimodal LLM, supplying structured prior knowledge that the model can attach to image regions and captions. The grounding step lets the model handle microscopy description and reasoning beyond what its general pretraining supports.

Builds on

  • DALL-E 2 (Ramesh 2022)Tied text and image representations; MicroWorld adds a knowledge graph to extend this to a specialist domain.
  • UNI Pathology (Chen 2023)Closed a related medical-imaging domain gap, but through self-supervised pretraining rather than a graph.

Solves vs predecessors

DALL-E 2 aligned text with natural images and does not transfer to microscopy. UNI closed a medical-imaging gap with large-scale self-supervised pretraining on pathology slides, which needs a large in-domain image corpus. MicroWorld targets the same domain-gap failure but with an explicit attributed graph of concepts, so it can supply structured knowledge where in-domain image data is scarce.

Method vs predecessors

Against DALL-E 2's contrastive and generative alignment, MicroWorld adds a relational knowledge structure on top of vision-language grounding. Against UNI's pretraining-heavy route, it trades additional data for an explicit graph, a different point on the cost-accuracy curve that may suit low-data scientific domains.
