Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO

Ba, Zheng, Xie, Li, Tong, Zhong, Yuan, Lu, Wu, Wang · arXiv 2026-08-27 · arXiv:2608.27351 · Appears in: Reasoning

What

The paper treats evolution strategies as a distinct reasoning post-training paradigm rather than as a memory-saving fallback for gradient-based reinforcement learning. Theoretically it shows that verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon diversity across the population raises Pass@K. Empirically, evolution strategies improve Pass@1 while reaching higher Pass@K than group relative policy optimisation, which shows entropy collapse instead. A second result finds that although the whole model drifts substantially in parameter space, the task gains come from a sparse subset of large-magnitude updates, and held-out evaluation shows no catastrophic forgetting. A sequential GRPO-then-ES schedule combines the Pass@1 strength of the first with the Pass@K strength of the second, and larger models are found to need smaller populations.

Why

Evolution strategies entered LLM post-training as a way to avoid storing gradients, and were therefore benchmarked as a cheaper approximation to GRPO and judged by how little Pass@1 they gave up. That framing hides the question of whether the two methods explore differently, which is what determines the shape of the resulting policy. GRPO's entropy collapse is a known and reported behaviour, but it has been treated as a tuning problem rather than as a property of the estimator, so no one has asked what an estimator without that property would buy. Without a diversity measure that the verifier can see, there was also no way to connect exploration behaviour to a downstream number.

How

The diversity measure is the key construction: Jensen-Shannon divergence across the population, projected through the verifier so that it counts only differences the task can distinguish, rather than raw distributional spread that may be irrelevant. That projection is what lets the theory connect population diversity to Pass@K. On the empirical side, the paper tracks entropy and Pass@K jointly through training for both methods, which is what exposes the divergence in behaviour, and then measures per-parameter update magnitudes to establish the functional-sparsity claim. Held-out evaluation on unrelated tasks tests whether the large parameter drift damages capabilities elsewhere, and it does not.

Solves vs predecessors

GRPO and its variants estimate an advantage from token-level log-probability ratios within a rollout group, and that estimator concentrates probability mass on whichever solution path the group currently favours. The consequence is entropy collapse: Pass@1 rises while Pass@K stagnates or falls, so a downstream verifier or search procedure has fewer distinct candidates to work with over time. Evolution strategies perturb parameters directly and never form that ratio, so the concentrating pressure has no analogue, and the paper shows Pass@K rising alongside Pass@1. It also removes the standing objection to gradient-free post-training at scale by showing that the large parameter drift is functionally sparse and does not cause forgetting.

Method vs predecessors

GRPO needs rollouts, per-token log-probabilities and a backward pass, so memory scales with sequence length and model size together. Evolution strategies need only forward evaluations of perturbed parameter vectors and a scalar fitness per member, which trades gradient memory for population sampling cost and makes the wall-clock profile depend on how many members are needed. The paper's hyperparameter study reports that requirement falling as the model grows, which is the opposite of the usual expectation and is what makes the method practical at scale. The sequential GRPO-then-ES schedule is the concrete recommendation, since the two estimators optimise different things and running them in order retains both.
