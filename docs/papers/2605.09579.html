Biosignal Fingerprinting: A Cross-Modal PPG-ECG Foundation Model

arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM-Health, Time Series

What

A Multi-modal Masked Autoencoder (M2AE) trained on 3.4 million paired ECG and PPG signal segments. The model combines cross-modal contrastive learning with signal reconstruction to learn shared biosignal representations. It achieves AUROC 0.974 for 5-class cardiovascular disease classification and 0.877 for hypertension detection. Critically, it works with a single modality at inference time — a PPG-only wearable can use ECG-informed representations without ever seeing ECG data at deployment.

Why

ECG provides high-fidelity cardiac electrical activity but requires clinical-grade sensors. PPG is available on consumer wearables but carries less diagnostic information. Training on paired data forces the model to learn modality-invariant features — representations that capture the underlying cardiac physiology regardless of whether it was observed electrically or optically. This means the representational quality of clinical ECG can be transferred to consumer PPG devices, which is the practical value proposition.

How

The architecture is a dual-encoder masked autoencoder. Each modality (ECG, PPG) has its own encoder that processes masked input patches. A shared decoder reconstructs both modalities from the joint latent space. The training objective combines three losses: (1) per-modality masked reconstruction, (2) cross-modal reconstruction (reconstruct ECG from PPG latents and vice versa), and (3) contrastive alignment that pulls paired ECG-PPG representations together and pushes unpaired ones apart. The 3.4 million paired segments come from clinical datasets where both ECG and PPG were recorded simultaneously. At inference, only one encoder is needed — the model accepts whichever modality is available and produces the shared representation.

Solves vs predecessors

Single-modality MAEs (zhang2023_mae) learn good representations within one signal type but cannot transfer across modalities. ECG-specific models (HEAR) require ECG at inference. This paper solves the modality gap: pretrain on paired data, deploy on either modality alone. The 0.974 AUROC for CVD classification from a self-supervised foundation model also exceeds what prior supervised approaches achieved on the same task categories.

Method vs predecessors

The key methodological difference from zhang2023_mae is the cross-modal reconstruction and contrastive objectives. A standard MAE reconstructs masked patches within a single signal. This paper adds cross-modal reconstruction (predict ECG from PPG and vice versa) and a contrastive loss that aligns the two modalities in latent space. The dual-encoder design with a shared decoder is architecturally straightforward, but the training objective design — particularly the balance between within-modal reconstruction, cross-modal reconstruction, and contrastive alignment — is where the engineering contribution lies.
