Biosignal Fingerprinting: A Cross-Modal PPG-ECG Foundation Model

arxiv 2605.09579 · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM Health, Signal/Time Series

What

A Multi-modal Masked Autoencoder (M2AE) pretrained on 3.4 million paired ECG and PPG signal segments. The training objective combines cross-modal contrastive learning with masked reconstruction, forcing the encoders to learn modality-invariant cardiovascular features. The model achieves AUROC 0.974 for 5-class cardiovascular disease classification and 0.877 for hypertension detection. The critical deployment feature is single-modality inference: after paired pretraining, the model works with PPG alone, so consumer wearables can benefit from hospital-grade ECG-informed representations.

Why

ECG provides rich cardiovascular diagnostic information but requires clinical-grade sensors. PPG is available on consumer wearables (smartwatches, fitness bands) but carries a weaker signal. The gap between clinical ECG and consumer PPG limits wearable health applications. If a model pretrained on paired ECG-PPG data can transfer ECG-level diagnostic quality to PPG-only inference, consumer wearables gain clinical-grade capability without hardware changes.

How

The architecture has two parallel encoders (one for ECG, one for PPG) that share a latent representation space enforced by a cross-modal contrastive loss. During pretraining, both modalities are present and random patches are masked from each. The model must reconstruct the masked patches using information from both modalities, which forces cross-modal feature transfer. The contrastive loss aligns the ECG and PPG encoder outputs for the same physiological event, creating a shared representation where modality-specific artifacts are suppressed and cardiovascular state information is preserved. At deployment, only the PPG encoder is used, but it produces representations aligned with the ECG feature space — effectively inheriting the diagnostic power of ECG through the shared pretraining.

Solves vs predecessors

Zhang et al. applied masked autoencoders to single-modality time-series data but could not transfer diagnostic quality across modalities. Yuan et al. demonstrated SSL for wearable sensors within a single modality but did not exploit cross-modal pairing. Gu et al. identified cross-modal biosignal pretraining as an open challenge. This paper solves the challenge by using cross-modal contrastive learning to force modality-invariant features, enabling single-sensor deployment with multi-sensor pretraining quality.

Method vs predecessors

The key departure from standard masked autoencoders (Zhang 2023) is the dual-encoder architecture with cross-modal contrastive alignment. Where Zhang et al. masked and reconstructed within a single modality, this paper masks patches across both ECG and PPG and reconstructs using information from the complementary modality. The contrastive loss is the critical addition — without it, the two encoders could learn modality-specific shortcuts that break single-modality inference. With it, the PPG encoder is forced to produce representations that are indistinguishable from ECG encoder outputs for the same cardiovascular event.
