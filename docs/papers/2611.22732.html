Provable Mixing Properties of Parallel Mixnets Revisited

Pierfrancesco Ingo, Peter Druschel, Deepak Garg · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.22732

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

A mix-network (mixnet) is a routing network that breaks source-to-destination message trackability by indistinguishably mixing the routes of concurrently transmitted messages, thus providing anonymity to senders, receivers and sender-receiver pairs. Given their potential use in high-stakes scenarios like protecting whistleblowers, one would like to prove formally that sender anonymity holds in a mixnet, even against strong adversaries that can compromise subsets of mixnet servers. However, existing work on anonymity in mixnets is either limited to linear mixnets (which do not scale horizontally), relies on special provisions such as additional noise or chaff messages, or is empirical -- typically based on entropy, which does not provide strong guarantees if the adversary has auxiliary information. In this paper, we present a new analysis of parallel mixnets which scale horizontally, showing that these networks can provide strong indistinguishability for messages even without additional noise messages. Our analysis works by bounding the statistical variance of the adversary's posterior likelihood of where a message of interest is at a given point in time. Using concentration bounds on this distribution, we show that parallel mixnets satisfy $(\varepsilon, δ)$-differential indistinguishability for messages routed through the mixnet (similar to $(\varepsilon, δ)$-differential privacy) with very small failure probabilities $δ$ and small privacy parameters $\varepsilon$ under reasonable operating conditions. Our model also enables an examination of the quantitative trade-off between anonymity, latency and per-router bandwidth in parallel mixnets.
