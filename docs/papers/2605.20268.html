Chronicle: A Multimodal Foundation Model for Joint Language and Time Series Understanding

Quinlan, Levasseur, Li, Zhu · arXiv 2026-05-18 · arXiv:2605.20268 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

A 324M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained from scratch on both natural language and time series in a single unified architecture. Both modalities share the same transformer blocks, attention mechanism, and residual stream. It matches Gemma-3-270M-PT on 19 NLU tasks, sets a new bar for frozen-embedding time series classification on 24 UCR/UEA datasets, and beats every supervised fusion baseline on Time-MMD multimodal forecasts.

Why

Existing multimodal text-and-time-series models adapt a pretrained language model post hoc, inheriting representations shaped without ever seeing temporal data. These models are also evaluated only against other multimodal baselines, not against the strongest unimodal foundation models in either domain. Whether joint training from scratch is needed — or even helpful — remained an open question.

How

Both modalities share the same transformer blocks. The bulk of pretraining uses unimodal batches so cross-modal capability emerges purely from shared parameters. A short alignment stage interleaves the two modalities. No pretrained language model is used as initialization — everything is trained from scratch on a mixed corpus.

Builds on

  • ChatTS (Xie 2025)Aligned time series with pretrained LLMs via post-hoc adaptation; Chronicle replaces this with joint pretraining.
  • TS-Agent (Liu 2025)Used LLM reasoning for TS via tool-use agents; Chronicle takes a different path by unifying modalities in one model.

Solves vs predecessors

ChatTS and TS-Agent both start from pretrained language models and bolt on time series capability. The failure mode is that the resulting representations are biased toward text structure and may not capture temporal dynamics well. Chronicle solves this by training from scratch, ensuring the shared representation is shaped by both modalities equally from the start.

Method vs predecessors

ChatTS uses a pretrained LLM with a time series tokenizer added post hoc. TS-Agent uses LLM reasoning with external time series tools. Chronicle instead uses a single decoder-only transformer with no pretrained initialization, shared parameters across modalities, and a two-phase training (unimodal then interleaved alignment). The 324M parameter count is also much smaller than the LLMs used by ChatTS and TS-Agent.
