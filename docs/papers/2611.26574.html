Classical Oracle Separation of QMA from the QCMA Hierarchy and Quantum Cryptography Even When P = QCMA

Alper Cakan · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.26574

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Remark: This work is in submission at STOC'27 and thus the full manuscript has been publicly posted on the conference website since November 3rd, 2026. Inspired by the Codeword-Hash Structure problem recently introduced by Cakan (arXiV, 2026) to separate $\mathsf{QMA}$ from $\mathsf{QCIP}$, we introduce a new problem called Codeword-Hash pairs in Noise ($\mathsf{CHN}$) and prove that, in the query complexity model, it is in $\mathsf{QMA}$ but hard for the $\mathsf{QCMA}$ hierarchy $\mathsf{QCMAH} = \bigcup_{d\geq1}\mathsf{QCMA}^{\mathsf{QCMA}^{\cdots^{\mathsf{QCMA}}}}$, the classical-witness quantum-verifier analogue of $\mathsf{PH}=\bigcup_{d\ge1}\mathsf{NP}^{\mathsf{NP}^{\cdots^{\mathsf{NP}}}}$. Using this problem, we show that there exists a classical oracle ${O}$ such that $\mathsf{P}^{O}=\mathsf{BQP}^{O}=\mathsf{QCMA}^{O} =\mathsf{QCMAH}^{O} \subset \mathsf{QMA}^{O} \text{ but } \mathsf{QMA}^{O}\not\subseteq\mathsf{QCMAH}^{O},$ thus showing that quantum state or quantum proof advantage could exist even if classical computers can efficiently solve any problem that can be quantumly verified with a classical proof. Further, using our problem, we show that various quantum cryptographic primitives can exist even when $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{QCMA}$ and thus neither quantum-computable one-way functions nor (verifiable) quantum computational advantage exist. In particular, relative to a classical oracle ${O}$ with $\mathsf{P}^{O}=\mathsf{BQP}^{O}=\mathsf{QCMA}^{O}=\mathsf{QCMAH}^{O}$, there exist pseudorandom states with classical keys and public-key encryption with copy-protected quantum decryption keys and classical public keys and ciphertexts, among other primitives, answering an open question of Kretschmer, Qian, and Tal (STOC 2025).
