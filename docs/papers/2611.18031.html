Latent resonances in oscillator networks

Junjie Xiao, Renaud Lambiotte, Hiroshi Kori · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.18031

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

In networks of coupled oscillators, from neural circuits to power grids, damping is expected to suppress oscillations because of its dissipativity. Here we show that local damping can, counterintuitively, amplify oscillations along some input-output channels, while the network's global oscillation energy decreases and its spectral properties improve. The amplified oscillation arises from a latent resonance that lies in the spectrum yet barely manifests. The added passive damping reshapes this resonance by mixing it with adjacent eigenmodes. In random power-grid networks, damping can boost weakly responding channels by more than an order of magnitude, while subdominant channels can grow to exceed the originally strongest channel. Damping thus not only suppresses oscillations but also provides a way to redistribute oscillatory energy. For any passive network, the amplified response and its upper bound follow exactly from baseline quantities, offering a principled route for network design and control.
