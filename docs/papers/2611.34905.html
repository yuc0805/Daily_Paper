Sliding-Window Detection of Hidden Regimes under Stochastic Switching

Arina Pambukyan, Hayk Nersisyan · 2026 · arXiv · arXiv:2611.34905

Why recommended

Very recent in your research areas.

Abstract

A sliding-window average is a simple estimator of the current state of a hidden regime-switching process, such as a hidden Markov model. A longer window averages out more noise but is more likely to contain a switch, and the question is what such a switch costs. We analyse the decoder that assigns the average of the last $m$ observations to the nearest regime mean. For two regimes with conditionally independent, stochastically ordered observations, a switch inside the window, averaged uniformly over its positions and directions, causes an error with probability exactly $1/2$. Consequently, for every stationary switching law, the error is at most $\bar P(m)+\bar s(m-1)/2$, where $\bar P(m)$ is the stationary-weighted single-regime error and $\bar s$ the switch rate. Under Markov switching, time reversal shows that a window whose oldest and current regimes differ is misclassified with probability exactly $1/2$, however many switches it contains, and the bound is attained up to a factor $p_{\min}^{m-1}$. For Gaussian noise with common variance, minimizing the bound gives an explicit window length which, in units of $σ^2/Δ^2$, depends only on the expected number of switches in that time. As switching probabilities vanish proportionally, the optimal error is $\bar s\log(1/\bar s)/(2I)(1+o(1))$, with $I$ the single-regime Chernoff exponent. In the Gaussian case this is twice the Bayes error. For symmetric switching, exponentially weighted moving averages reduce this factor to at most $2\log2\approx1.39$, and two-block weights adapted to the switch rate attain the Bayes rate. For three or more regimes, long-window switch costs are set by the geometry of the regime means. For Markov-modulated random walks with $\pm1$ steps on a reflecting lattice, a position-aware decoder learns as fast as without walls under holding boundaries, while steps at bouncing boundaries carry none.
