Scarce or Missed Opportunities? Structural Peers and Latin America's Diversification Frontier

Saangkeub Lee, Dawoon Jeong, Wonsub Eum, Jeong-Dong Lee · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.09727

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Latin America's limited export upgrading is often attributed to failures to seize available diversification opportunities. We distinguish two possibilities: countries failed to realize opportunities demonstrated by structurally similar economies, or upgrading-oriented options were scarce within this peer-revealed set. Using export data for 105 countries from 1962 to 2024, we construct country-specific opportunity sets with an imitation-density measure associated with subsequent product entry. Latin America's deficit arose mainly from scarce upgrading-oriented candidates rather than weak aggregate conversion. Conversion rates matched the global benchmark, but regional frontiers contained few manufacturing products and almost no higher-complexity manufacturing candidates and, unlike East and Southeast Asia, did not shift toward them over time. An illustrative decomposition associates most of the regional upgrading-entry gap with candidate availability. The findings imply that industrial policy should expand feasible upgrading pathways rather than focus only on converting existing opportunities.
