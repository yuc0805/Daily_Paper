Bio-Inspired Self-Supervised Learning for Wrist-worn IMU Signals (Bio-PM)

Tarale, Chu, Varghese, Liu, Xu, Iyyer, Lee · arXiv 2026-03 · arXiv:2603.10961 · Appears in: Self-Supervised Framework

What

Bio-PM is a self-supervised encoder for wrist accelerometer signals. Rather than splitting the stream into fixed-length windows, it tokenizes the signal into "movement segments" defined by acceleration zero-crossings, a unit motivated by the submovement theory of motor control. Each segment is resampled, encoded by a small 1D CNN, tagged with its axis and duration, and ordered in time; a 5-layer Transformer with time-aware positional encodings then models the segment sequence under a masked-reconstruction objective. The encoder is pretrained on the NHANES corpus (about 28,000 hours, 11,000 participants, 10 million windows).

Why

Labels for human activity recognition are scarce, so self-supervised learning is the standard route to representations from unlabeled wearable data. The authors argue that existing methods share a hidden weakness: they treat the accelerometer stream as an unstructured time series and choose token boundaries by arbitrary rules, so the model spends capacity on local waveform shape and never learns how movements compose into activities. Prior objectives (contrastive learning, augmentation prediction, raw masked reconstruction) all leave the tokenizer untouched, which the paper identifies as the actual bottleneck.

How

Token boundaries are placed at acceleration zero-crossings after a 0.5 Hz high-pass filter and a two-stage hysteresis to suppress noise crossings. Each segment is resampled to length 32, passed through a 1D CNN, and concatenated with a learned axis embedding and its duration. Tokens from all three axes are merged and sorted by time, then fed to a 5-layer Transformer with absolute and relative time-aware positional encodings. Pretraining masks half the segments under a hybrid random-plus-contiguous policy and also corrupts a fraction of visible tokens to block trivial copying; an L1 loss reconstructs the masked segment waveforms. Downstream, the frozen encoder is linear-probed with the low-frequency gravity component re-added for posture context. Across six subject-disjoint benchmarks, macro-F1 improves by an average of 6 percent (range 4 to 12 percent) over baselines pretrained on the same corpus.

Solves vs predecessors

Yuan 2024 reached strong wearable HAR with a fixed-window augmentation-prediction objective, but the model never had pressure to learn event boundaries because the windows were fixed. Bio-PM fixes the specific failure mode that representations focus on local waveform morphology rather than on the compositional structure of movement. By forcing tokens to align to movement events, the objective rewards modeling the temporal relations among meaningful units, which is what the downstream activity labels depend on.

Method vs predecessors

Compared to Yuan 2024 and to a same-paper equal-length-chunk baseline, Bio-PM holds the masked-reconstruction objective fixed and changes only the tokenization, which isolates the gain to the input unit rather than to the objective or the corpus. Compared to Zhang 2023, which reconstructs masked raw patches, Bio-PM reconstructs masked movement segments, so the prediction target carries an event boundary. Compared to generic time-series foundation models (Chronos, Moment) used as reference points, the domain-aware tokenization gives a representation better matched to wrist motion.
