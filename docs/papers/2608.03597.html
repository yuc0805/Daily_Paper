FOUND-AF: Benchmarking ECG Foundation Models for Atrial Fibrillation Detection

Anonymous et al. · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2608.03597 · Appears in: LLM-Health

What

FOUND-AF is a unified, leakage-controlled, deployment-oriented benchmark for pretrained ECG representations. Nine public foundation models from five families, including HuBERT-ECG, CLEF, ST-MEM, ECG-JEPA and ECGFounder, are evaluated under identical conditions across four heterogeneous datasets: AFDB, CinC2017, CPSC2021 and LTAFDB. ECGFounder is strongest overall while also sitting at a favourable point on the trade-off between accuracy, model size, inference time and memory.

Why

Published ECG foundation model results are not comparable to each other. Each paper chooses its own preprocessing, its own downstream head, its own fine-tuning budget and its own split policy, so a reported accuracy difference between two encoders may reflect any of those choices rather than representation quality. The problem is sharper in physiological data than in vision or language because a single recording yields many overlapping windows, and splitting at the window level leaks subject identity across train and test, inflating results in a way that is easy to miss.

How

Every model is used as a frozen feature extractor, with standardised preprocessing, model-native resampling, a fixed XGBoost classifier on top, and recording-level grouped cross-validation so no recording spans a split boundary. Reporting covers classification metrics, receiver operating characteristic analysis, paired recording-level bootstrap comparisons with Holm correction for multiple testing, embedding-space visualisation, and computational efficiency profiling. Freezing the encoder and fixing the downstream classifier is what makes the comparison a statement about representation quality; the grouped cross-validation is what makes it honest.

Solves vs predecessors

The biosignals survey collected each model's own claims, which means it inherited each paper's evaluation setup and therefore could not say which encoder is actually better. FOUND-AF fixes that by re-running everything under one protocol and producing a ranking with statistical comparison. HeAR and SSL-for-HAR each validated one encoder against baselines its own authors selected, so neither can be read as evidence about relative representation quality across the field. FOUND-AF also addresses a failure mode specific to physiological data that none of the three predecessors controlled for consistently: within-subject leakage from window-level splitting, removed here by recording-level grouping.

Method vs predecessors

The methodological import is the frozen-encoder plus fixed-downstream-classifier protocol, standard practice in vision and language representation research, applied to ECG. Where the predecessors each trained or fine-tuned a model and reported task performance, FOUND-AF trains nothing and varies only the encoder, so every reported difference is attributable to the representation. The additions specific to this setting are model-native resampling, so no encoder is penalised for a preprocessing mismatch, recording-level grouped cross-validation, and paired bootstrap comparison with Holm correction across nine models. Note that gu2025_biosignals appears on both the time-series and llm-health area pages and so already bridges Community 1 (Health AI and Self-Supervised) and Community 5 (Wearable Sensing and Behavior); FOUND-AF strengthens that bridge on the evaluation side rather than the modelling side, supplying the shared protocol the two communities lacked.
