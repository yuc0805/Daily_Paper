Funding Landscape of Early-Career Scientists: A Perspective from the AGU SPA Early Career Leadership Advisory Committee

Savvas Raptis, Claire Gasque, Samuel Hart, Yi Qi et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.34392

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Like researchers across many scientific fields, early-career researchers in heliophysics face a critical transition from supervised research to independent scientific inquiry. However, the funding landscape that should support this transition is fragmented and unpredictable. Here we examine the current funding opportunities available to early-career researchers in heliophysics alongside the historical experiences of established scientists who navigated similar transitions. We catalog the major U.S. federal programs targeting early-career space physicists, documenting their eligibility constraints, selection rates, and accessibility. Access varies with career stage, appointment type, citizenship requirements, and solicitation schedules, and selection rates can swing sharply: the NASA Heliophysics Early Career Investigator Program (ECIP) selected 28% of proposals in 2022 but only 6% in 2024. To provide historical context, we collected survey responses from 30 researchers whose PhDs span 1972-2016 and conducted eight interviews with mid- and senior-career researchers. Most respondents viewed the present funding environment as more difficult than their own early careers, identifying predictability of opportunity, not ease, as what enabled earlier generations to build independence. Salary support alone does not guarantee opportunities to develop scientific leadership; accessible PI awards and real responsibility within collaborative projects can both contribute to that development. We recommend predictable, adequately sized grant programs for early-career principal investigators, transparent reporting of selection statistics across all early-career programs, continued use of dual-anonymous peer review, and named leadership roles for early-career researchers within large projects.
