Citizen-informed scenarios to shape energy demand reduction strategies

Andy Yuille, Oliver Broad, John Barrett, Christian Brand et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.27834

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Growing evidence has demonstrated the important role that energy demand reduction can play in achieving net zero. Despite this, the adoption of such measures in government strategies often remains limited, in part based on perceived public resistance to change. Using a novel combined deliberative-modelling approach, which fills a key gap in the literature, we find this is unfounded. Citizens both recognise the importance of and support demand-side interventions to achieve the UK's net-zero goals. This support, however, is conditional. If policy interventions can make demand-side choices practical and fair, we find citizens are prepared to reduce energy demand by at least 37% between 2025 and 2050, providing 20% of the abatement required to meet net-zero targets. Policymakers could thus unlock a wide range of demand reduction measures by acting on a more comprehensive understanding of public views and needs. Moreover, our approach offers a powerful framework, applicable in other contexts, to underpin policymaking with socially-informed quantitative modelling.
