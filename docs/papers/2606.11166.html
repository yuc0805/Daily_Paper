Flaws in the LLM Automation Narrative

Perrett, Elliott, Hill, Scott · arXiv 2026-06 · arXiv:2606.11166 · Appears in: LLM

What

The paper tests the claim that frontier LLMs perform at the level of human experts on knowledge-work tasks. It builds a benchmark that asks for computer code to complete a real data-analysis task, compares a frontier model against human expert submissions, and explicitly measures the variance of responses and the magnitude of errors rather than only average accuracy.

Why

Strong "expert-level" claims rest on benchmarks that report mean accuracy on standardized datasets. Those benchmarks often overlap with training data and rarely report how reliable a model is or how large its errors are, both of which matter in high-stakes settings. The authors argue this evaluation style has overstated model capability.

How

Human experts and a frontier model both complete the same code-writing data-analysis task. The authors score not just whether the answer is correct on average but how much performance varies and how big the errors are when they occur. Human experts come out ahead on average across several metrics and vary less, which contradicts the reading that the model is already an expert substitute.

Builds on

  • CounselBench (Li 2025)Benchmarking LLMs in a high-stakes domain; same concern about evaluation quality, raised here for knowledge work.
  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)A frontier reasoning model of the kind whose benchmark-driven capability claims this paper tests.

Solves vs predecessors

CounselBench built a domain benchmark and scored models on it. This paper turns the lens on benchmarking itself, showing that mean accuracy hides the variance and error magnitude that decide whether a model is safe to deploy, and that on those measures human experts still lead.

Method vs predecessors

Standard benchmarks report a single proportion-correct number. This work adds variance and error-magnitude as first-class metrics and uses a task with expert human baselines, so the comparison is against people rather than against a fixed answer key.
