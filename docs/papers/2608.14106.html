Forecast Collapse in Time-Series Foundation Models

Wan, Ma, Zhu, Liu, Wang, Wen, Liu · arXiv 2026-08-14 · arXiv:2608.14106 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

The paper names and measures a failure mode of time-series foundation models: when forecasting hourly returns for 1,000 US equities, predictions become nearly flat and rank the series poorly under cross-sectional correlation. The same models do not collapse when the target is trading volume in the same setting. The authors reproduce the effect across several TSFMs, twelve deep-learning forecasters, and 97 public benchmark configurations, and introduce CalibRank, an objective mixing calibration and ranking, which nearly triples cross-sectional correlation on the Finance1K dataset while keeping forecast amplitude close to the target.

Why

Modern forecasting benchmarks score models one series at a time with squared error or its relatives. That protocol cannot see whether a model preserves the relationship between series at a given timestamp, which is exactly what downstream decisions such as ranking, triage, or allocation depend on. Pretrained forecasters such as Chronos and Moirai were validated under per-series metrics, and channel-independent designs such as PatchTST made per-series modelling the default, so no existing evaluation would have caught a model that produces well-calibrated but nearly constant forecasts.

How

The authors first characterise when collapse happens by sweeping target predictability across models and benchmark configurations, isolating two mechanisms: low predictability bounds the amplitude a calibrated point forecast can take, and per-series objectives leave cross-series structure unidentified. They then quantify the calibration-ranking tradeoff, showing that optimising squared error flattens predictions while optimising cross-sectional correlation alone inflates amplitude by more than an order of magnitude. CalibRank combines both terms so that ranking is recovered without amplitude blowing up, and it is applied on top of every model tested rather than requiring a new architecture.

Builds on

  • Chronos (Ansari 2024)Established pretrained tokenised forecasting as a general TSFM baseline, evaluated entirely with per-series error metrics.
  • Moirai (Woo 2024)Extended TSFM pretraining to any-variate settings, still trained and scored one series at a time.
  • PatchTST (Nie 2023)Introduced channel-independent patching, which made per-series modelling the default and removed the cross-series coupling this paper needs.

Solves vs predecessors

Chronos and Moirai showed a single pretrained model can forecast many series and reported strong per-series error, but neither checked whether the ordering across series survives. This paper shows it often does not, and that the failure is invisible to the metrics those papers used. PatchTST argued channel independence improves accuracy and stability; this paper shows the same independence is the reason cross-series structure is unidentified, and supplies an objective that restores ranking without abandoning calibration.

Method vs predecessors

Chronos and Moirai change the model, the tokenisation, and the pretraining corpus while keeping the per-series objective fixed. This paper keeps the models fixed and changes the objective, which is why CalibRank improves cross-sectional correlation on every model tested rather than on one architecture. Where PatchTST modifies the input representation to decouple channels, CalibRank adds a term that explicitly couples them at each timestamp during training.
