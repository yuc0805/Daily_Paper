Time-Series Foundation Model Embeddings for Remaining Useful Life Estimation

El-Ghoussani, De Vita, Naumann, Belagiannis · arXiv 2606.11990 2026 · link · Appears in: Signal/Time Series

What

The paper freezes a pretrained time-series foundation model (Chronos-2) and trains only a small regression head to predict remaining useful life from multivariate sensor streams. The frozen-backbone embeddings beat recurrent, convolutional, Transformer, and gradient-boosting baselines, and accuracy improves as the input history grows. The message is that a general time-series foundation model carries enough structure that a light head is competitive with task-specific architectures.

Why

Remaining-useful-life models are usually trained from scratch with task-specific architectures, which is costly and does not reuse general time-series knowledge. It was unclear whether a general foundation model's embeddings, with no fine-tuning, carry enough signal for a regression task on industrial sensor data. The paper tests the plainest version of transfer to settle that.

How

A pretrained time-series foundation model is frozen and used only as a feature extractor; a small regression head is trained on its embeddings to predict remaining useful life. The authors vary the length of input history and compare against recurrent, convolutional, Transformer, and gradient-boosting baselines.

Solves vs predecessors

Where the survey catalogued attachment methods and TS-Agent built an agent on top of a foundation model, this paper removes everything except a frozen backbone and a one-layer head, which fixes the open question of whether the embeddings alone transfer. The clean ablation shows they do, beating task-specific baselines without fine-tuning.

Method vs predecessors

The technical approach is deliberately minimal: no fine-tuning, no agent, no prompting, just frozen embeddings plus a linear regression head. This contrasts with TS-Agent's heavier agentic stack and gives a baseline recipe that any added complexity must beat.
