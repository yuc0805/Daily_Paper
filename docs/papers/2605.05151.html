Superposition Is Not Necessary: A Mechanistic Interpretability Analysis of Transformer Representations for Time Series Forecasting

Yildirim · arXiv 2026-05-06 · arXiv:2605.05151 · Appears in: Signal/Time Series, Interpretable ML

What

Applies sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to probe PatchTST internal FFN representations and finds that standard time series forecasting benchmarks do not require superposition. Dictionary expansion from 0.5x to 4.0x produces negligible performance change (0.214% average), overcomplete dictionaries remain largely inactive, and causal interventions on dominant features cause minimal forecast perturbation.

Why

Transformers dominate time series forecasting leaderboards, yet simple linear models like DLinear remain competitive. No mechanistic explanation for this phenomenon had been offered. Understanding whether transformers actually use their representational capacity on TS data has direct implications for model selection and architecture design.

How

SAEs are trained on post-GELU intermediate FFN activations of PatchTST with dictionary sizes from 0.5x to 4.0x the native dimensionality. The authors first show that a single-layer narrow transformer matches deeper configurations. They then measure dictionary utilization, reconstruction quality, downstream performance sensitivity to dictionary expansion, and causal intervention effects on dominant latent features.

Builds on

  • PatchTST (Nie 2023)The transformer architecture being probed; established patching as the standard TS transformer input method.
  • DLinear (Zeng 2023)The simple linear baseline whose persistent competitiveness is mechanistically explained here.

Solves vs predecessors

The debate between PatchTST-class transformers and DLinear-class linear models was empirical: sometimes one wins, sometimes the other, with no explanation of why. This paper provides the mechanistic explanation: PatchTST does not use superposition on standard benchmarks, meaning its excess capacity over DLinear is wasted on these tasks.

Method vs predecessors

PatchTST and DLinear are both evaluated only on forecasting accuracy. This paper adds a layer of analysis using SAEs from the mechanistic interpretability literature (typically applied to language models) and applies causal intervention techniques. The methodological contribution is porting NLP interpretability tools to the TS domain.
