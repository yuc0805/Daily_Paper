Why wait? Optimal rules for intertemporal decisions across life history, economics and machine learning

Elias Tannira, Luís M. A. Bettencourt · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.32748

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

When should we take a smaller reward now rather than wait for a larger one later? This question recurs across evolutionary biology, behavioral ecology, psychology, economics and artificial intelligence, yet each field treats temporal discounting differently. We show that intertemporal decisions across these disciplines share a common structure and a single optimal rule: wait while the growth rate of the reward exceeds the discount rate, and act when the two rates become equal. For a single decision, this rule defines an optimal waiting time; for a continuous reward stream, it becomes an equation of motion for quantities apportioned over time, such as consumption or fertility. We derive reward and discount rates from explicit mechanisms, including mortality and other hazards, uncertainty and risk aversion, population growth, capital depreciation, dread, subsistence thresholds and the value of information, with examples from laboratory choice, retirement, maturation, resource extraction, capital accumulation and exploration. The framework shows how apparent anomalies, including hyperbolic discounting, the sign effect and the impatience of deprivation, can arise from the relative structure of rewards and hazards rather than from fixed preferences. Finally, we identify what must be measured to explain and modify temporal preferences in experiments and policy.
