BenchHAR: Benchmarking Self-Supervised Learning for Generalizable Sensor-based Activity Recognition

Cai, Feng, Yu, Guo, Hong · 2026 · paper · Appears in: Signal/Time Series, Self-supervised

What

BenchHAR is a unified benchmark for evaluating the generalization of self-supervised learning methods for sensor-based human activity recognition. It curates roughly 258K samples and evaluates eight SSL methods across 12 encoder-classifier architectures, finding that hybrid pretraining (reconstruction + contrastive) with CNN encoders generalizes best.

Why

SSL methods developed for vision and language have been adopted for HAR, but no systematic comparison existed for how well they generalize to unseen target distributions in the sensor domain. Prior work demonstrated that SSL helps HAR but did not identify which paradigm, encoder, or data scaling strategy produces the best out-of-distribution generalization.

How

The framework standardizes evaluation by fixing the dataset, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol across all methods. It varies three axes: SSL paradigm (contrastive, reconstructive, hybrid), encoder architecture (CNN, Transformer, etc.), and classifier expressiveness. Data scaling experiments control for activity class overlap between pretraining and downstream data. Device type (custom-grade vs. research-grade) and body position transfer are also evaluated.

Builds on

  • SSL for HAR with 700K Person-days (Yuan 2024)Demonstrated large-scale SSL pretraining for HAR. BenchHAR refines the scaling story by showing that domain-matched data matters more than raw volume.

Solves vs predecessors

Yuan 2024 showed that scaling SSL pretraining to 700K person-days improves HAR but did not isolate which SSL paradigm generalizes best or whether all types of additional data help equally. BenchHAR provides the missing controlled comparison and reveals a key negative result: unlabeled data from non-downstream activity classes does not improve generalization, qualifying the "more data is better" narrative.

Method vs predecessors

Where Yuan 2024 used a single SSL approach at large scale, BenchHAR systematically compares eight methods (spanning contrastive, reconstructive, and hybrid paradigms) under a standardized protocol. The hybrid paradigm (combining both reconstruction and contrastive objectives) consistently outperforms either alone, and CNN encoders outperform Transformers for generalizable HAR representations — a finding that goes against the trend in vision and NLP where Transformers dominate.
