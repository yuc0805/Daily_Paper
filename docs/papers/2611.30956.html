Leveraging Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) for the Exchange of Wearable Inertial Sensor Data: Design and Development Study

Gabriela Ciortuz, Joshua Wiedekopf, Hannes Ulrich, Hawzhin Hozhabr Pour et al. · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.30956

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Continuous monitoring with wearables can support the assessment of frailty, functional decline, and movement disorders, but raw inertial measurement unit (IMU) recordings are typically stored in proprietary files that electronic health records cannot exchange or interpret. HL7 Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) standardizes health data exchange, yet existing FHIR profiles for personal health devices target spot measurements and short waveforms, not long multichannel motion recordings with their recording context and activity labels. We present WHIR (Wearable Health Interoperable Recordings), a FHIR R5-based information model and implementation guide for wearable IMU recordings. Metadata requirements were derived from a review of 23 published human activity recognition datasets and formalized in a FHIR-independent information model, which was then mapped to FHIR R5 and authored in FHIR Shorthand. Raw signals are kept in files referenced through DocumentReference resources, so that FHIR holds the metadata and activity labels. Terminology reuses SNOMED CT, LOINC, and ISO/IEEE 11073 concepts wherever available. The implementation guide defines 9 profiles, 4 extensions, 10 code systems, and 15 value sets; all artifacts and 67 example instances passed the conformance checks of the HL7 FHIR IG Publisher without errors. Two use cases, machine learning training data and automated home-based geriatric assessment, were implemented as conformant example instances, and we compare the model with the HL7 Personal Health Device Implementation Guide. WHIR provides a FHIR R5-conformant representation of wearable IMU recordings together with device metadata, sensor placement, and time-aligned activity labels, covering concepts that the Personal Health Device Implementation Guide does not address. The implementation guide and its sources are openly available for reuse and extension.
