When the Norm is Wrong: Social Norm Misspecification and Targeting with an Application to Microfinance Adoption

Shantanu Chadha · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.12255

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Structural models of peer effects typically capture peer influence through the average, aggregate, or in some cases through extreme peer actions. Such specifications, however, are imposed ex ante and are typically ad hoc. We study the consequences of social norm misspecification by extending the constant elasticity of substitution (CES) norm from continuous to discrete choices. Considering a binary-choice game with incomplete information, we derive results on existence and uniqueness of a rational expectations equilibrium and establish conditions for identification of the structural parameters. We estimate the model by the Nested Pseudo-Likelihood estimator, implemented using the spectral algorithm. Monte Carlo simulations show that the CES estimates of the peer effects approach their true values as the sample grows, whereas imposing a linear norm biases them even in large samples. We apply the framework to microfinance adoption in India and find a strongly max-leaning norm, with significant heterogeneity in curvature and peer effects across villages grouped by caste composition and wealth. In a counterfactual exercise that allocates a fixed subsidy budget to maximise adoption, the estimated and linear norms target very different households, with the former favouring village "leaders", who are more likely to adopt and were the first to be informed about microfinance.
