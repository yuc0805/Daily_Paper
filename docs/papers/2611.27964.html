Your Camera Can Hear You: Side-Channel Acoustic Eavesdropping via Android Camera2 Metadata

Guangyuan Ji, Wenjing Wang, Bingsheng Zhang · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.27964

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

To prevent motion sensors from acting as covert microphones, Android 12+ caps SensorManager gyroscope and accelerometer rates at 200 Hz by default, requires HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS for higher rates, and throttles sensors back to 200 Hz when the microphone toggle is off. We show that Android's Camera2 subsystem bypasses these defenses. During an active Camera2 session, Camera HAL stabilization pipelines record high-rate gyroscope measurements and export them via vendor CaptureResult metadata. Loudspeaker speech excites chassis vibrations that couple into this path. Across 50 smartphones (2020-2026) from 10 vendors, 37 expose gyroscope metadata via Camera2, and 26 exceed 200 Hz--including six flagship models at 800-1000 Hz, double SensorManager's 400-500 Hz ceiling. This channel requires neither RECORD_AUDIO nor HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS and defeats privacy switches: turning off the microphone toggle does not throttle metadata, and turning off the camera toggle blanks only video frames while 800-1000 Hz metadata keeps streaming. To build a zero-profiling attack that generalizes to unseen phones, we introduce Source-to-Leakage Learning (S2L). Prior attacks train on real sensor traces, entangling speech with a specific phone's mechanical resonances and sampling rate. S2L decouples speech content from hardware acoustics using a physics-constrained forward simulator. Trained exclusively on simulated leakage without target-phone traces, our universal recognizer CamEar achieves 52.1% mean word accuracy (up to 61.8%) and 74.3% character accuracy (up to 80.7%) across the six 800-1000 Hz phones. In contrast, ISpyU models fine-tuned on real target-phone data drop to 25.9% word accuracy on unseen phones; CamEar improves cross-device accuracy by 101.4% and outperforms ISpyU's same-device models on every tested phone.
