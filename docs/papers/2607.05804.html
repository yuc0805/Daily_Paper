TurnOPD: Making On-Policy Distillation Turn-Aware for Efficient Long-Horizon Agent Training

Zhou, Zheng, Li, Peng, Xu, Chen (Tencent Hunyuan) · arXiv 2026 · arXiv:2607.05804 · Appears in: Agent

What

TurnOPD improves on-policy distillation for multi-turn agents by budgeting supervision across turns. On-policy distillation trains a student agent to match a stronger teacher on the student's own trajectories. TurnOPD adds adaptive rollout-depth budgeting, which uses probe statistics to set how many turns to collect, and progressive turn-normalized loss budgeting, which shifts loss weighting from token-level to turn-balanced over training. On ALFWorld, WebShop, and Multi-Hop Search it reaches higher validation accuracy for the same wall-clock training budget.

Why

Plain on-policy distillation for long-horizon agents wastes effort in two ways. Full-length rollouts spend wall-clock time on late turns that give weak, noisy supervision, and a trajectory-level objective concentrates most of the loss on shallow early tokens, so deep decision turns stay under-trained once early behavior is aligned. Prior work applied on-policy distillation to agents without addressing where in a long trajectory the training effort should go.

How

Two controllers reallocate effort across turns. Adaptive rollout-depth budgeting uses probe-based turn statistics to decide the rollout length, so compute is not spent collecting low-value tail turns. Progressive turn-normalized loss budgeting gradually moves the KL weighting from token-level toward turn-balanced supervision, so deep decision turns receive gradient once the early turns are aligned. Together they advance the accuracy-versus-time frontier beyond plain on-policy distillation.

Builds on

  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)Reinforcement learning shapes a single reasoning trace; TurnOPD moves up to the multi-turn trajectory and budgets supervision within it.
  • ADaPT (Prasad 2023)Decomposed long tasks into sub-tasks; TurnOPD keeps the task fixed and budgets training effort across its turns.

Solves vs predecessors

Trajectory-level on-policy distillation, in the spirit of single-trace reinforcement methods such as DeepSeek-R1, spends its supervision unevenly: early tokens get most of the loss and late turns get noisy signal. TurnOPD fixes this failure by making the objective turn-aware, so deep decision turns are trained and tail turns do not drain the wall-clock budget.

Method vs predecessors

Where DeepSeek-R1 optimizes one reasoning trace with reinforcement learning, TurnOPD distills a teacher into a student across many turns and controls two knobs, rollout depth and per-turn loss weight, that earlier agent distillation left uniform. Where ADaPT restructured the task into sub-tasks, TurnOPD changes the training schedule rather than the task structure.
