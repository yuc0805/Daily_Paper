GRAIL: Gradient-Reweighted Advantages for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

Pala, Toh, Poria · arXiv 2026-06-03 · arXiv:2606.04889 · Appears in: Reasoning

What

GRAIL introduces token-wise advantage reweighting for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) in LLM reasoning training. Instead of broadcasting one sequence-level advantage to all tokens as GRPO does, it uses gradient-activation saliency to assign higher weight to tokens whose hidden-state activations are most sensitive to the final answer. Evaluated on five models from the Qwen3, R1-distilled, and OctoThinker families, it achieves +3.60% accuracy and +3.05% Pass@3 over standard GRPO without requiring a process reward model.

Why

GRPO and similar RLVR methods treat all tokens in a response equally when computing policy gradient updates. This dilutes the training signal: filler words and flawed reasoning steps receive the same gradient magnitude as the tokens that actually drove the correct inference. Process reward models can provide step-level supervision but are expensive to train, require human-annotated step labels, and add a separate model to maintain. The field needed a way to get fine-grained credit assignment without these costs.

How

For each token in a sampled response, GRAIL computes a saliency score by measuring the product of the token's activation and its gradient with respect to the final-answer logit. Tokens with higher saliency receive proportionally larger advantage weights in the GRPO policy gradient update. This requires one additional backward pass per batch — no separate reward model, no step-level annotations. The method is architecture-agnostic and plugs into existing GRPO training pipelines.

Builds on

  • DeepSeek-R1 (2025)Established GRPO as the practical RL algorithm for LLM reasoning, replacing PPO with group relative policy optimization and uniform sequence-level advantage.
  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting (Wei 2023)Showed that step-by-step reasoning traces improve LLM performance; GRAIL makes RL training aware of which steps matter most.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that GRPO with uniform advantage broadcasting produces strong reasoning models, but the uniform distribution means every token — including filler and incorrect reasoning steps — gets the same gradient push. GRAIL fixes this specific failure mode by using gradient-activation saliency as an intrinsic credit-assignment signal, concentrating the training update on the tokens that actually drive correctness. This achieves what process reward models do (fine-grained credit) without what they cost (separate model training and human step annotations).

Method vs predecessors

Compared to standard GRPO (DeepSeek-R1), GRAIL adds one backward pass per batch to compute token-level saliency scores, then multiplies these into the advantage before the policy gradient step. The overhead is roughly 30-40% more compute per training step, but this is far cheaper than training and running a separate PRM. Compared to Chain-of-Thought prompting, GRAIL operates at training time rather than inference time — it shapes which reasoning patterns the model learns rather than which patterns it expresses at test time.
