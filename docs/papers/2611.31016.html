CenShaper: Shaping Proxy Traffic for Censorship Circumvention

Shabbir Hussain, Aaron Ortwein, Roya Ensafi · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.31016

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Modern commodity DPI systems are capable of analyzing packet sizes, directions, and timings to detect circumvention tools even when those tools use sophisticated obfuscation. The anti-censorship community has called for innovation in traffic shaping and built an ecosystem that facilitates the adoption of new shaping techniques. However, a fundamental gap is halting progress: \textit{what} should circumvention traffic look like? Past experience with traffic shaping has underscored that effective shaping must produce traffic that looks like legitimate Internet traffic while limiting the ability of censors to identify multiple proxies through shared fingerprints. In this paper, we use our access to large-scale real-world TLS traffic at a regional ISP to analyze approximately 12 million connections, finding that individual servers differ substantially from the broader TLS population and that traffic patterns vary across services. Combining these empirical observations with past experience with traffic shaping, we build CenShaper, which shapes each proxy's traffic to look like a selected TLS server. To reproduce these server-specific patterns, CenShaper trains a shared generative model using traffic from TLS connections to many servers and adapts it for each proxy using a small sample of recorded connections to its selected server. Our model evaluation shows that fingerprints learned from known proxies transfer poorly to proxies imitating previously unseen TLS services. In end-to-end testing, CenShaper reduces the detection rate of TLS-over-TLS fingerprinting from over 85\% to approximately 3\% at a 1\% false positive rate. Finally, we show how running CenShaper as a sidecar process simplifies integration with existing circumvention tools, making it a practical defense against increasingly powerful traffic analysis attacks.
