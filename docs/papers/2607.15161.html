On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD²)

Heo, Hwang, Yun, Han (NAVER AI Lab) · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: LLM

What

On-policy distillation transfers a teacher's reasoning by giving token-level supervision on the student's own rollouts, which avoids the need for a reward model. OPD² changes the target: instead of imitating the teacher's full output distribution, it distills the delta signal, the difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher and its base model before instruction tuning. Across mathematics, science, and code-reasoning benchmarks OPD² beats standard on-policy distillation, with gains holding across several Qwen3 sizes and transferring to Gemma 4.

Why

On-policy distillation has been applied widely, but its core design (imitate the teacher distribution) is underexplored. Copying the whole teacher distribution also copies base-model behavior the student already has, which dilutes the signal that matters, namely the reasoning ability added by tuning. A target that isolates that added ability should transfer more directly.

How

The delta signal is defined as the difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher and its base model prior to instruction tuning, so it captures the change induced by reasoning tuning. OPD² uses this delta, rather than the raw teacher output, as the distillation reward on the student's on-policy rollouts. Experiments span math, science, and code reasoning across multiple Qwen3 sizes in thinking and non-thinking modes, plus a Gemma 4 transfer check.

Solves vs predecessors

DeepSeek-R1 produced a costly reasoning teacher; standard distillation of that teacher wastes capacity re-copying base behavior. OPD² transfers only the reasoning delta, reaching strong performance with a short post-training period, which lowers the cost of moving reasoning from a large teacher to a small student.

Method vs predecessors

Where prior on-policy distillation imitated the teacher's full distribution, OPD² subtracts the base model from the tuned teacher and distills the difference. This is the same "measure what tuning added" move that the day's chess study makes by comparing fine-tuned and post-RL policies, applied here as a transfer signal rather than an analysis tool.
