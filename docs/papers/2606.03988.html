Imaginative Perception Tokens Enhance Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

Bigverdi, Krishna et al. · arXiv 2026-06 · arXiv:2606.03988 · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

The paper introduces Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPT), intermediate tokens that represent what a vision language model would perceive under a different spatial configuration while staying consistent with the observed input. Built on the unified vision language model BAGEL and trained on roughly 20K examples across three tasks (perspective taking, path tracing, and multiview counting), IPT supervision beats textual chain-of-thought training and raises multiview-counting accuracy by 3.4%, without generating any image at inference.

Why

Vision language models do well on direct perception but fail when the needed information is not visible, such as inferring a view from an unseen angle or tracing a path through an occluded space. The usual fix is textual chain-of-thought, which writes the reasoning out in words. The authors find that forcing spatial computation through language can make performance worse, which points to a mismatch between spatial problems and a text-only intermediate representation.

How

IPT supplies a perceptual intermediate representation rather than a textual one. During training the model learns to emit tokens that externalize what it would perceive under an alternative spatial configuration, supervised against ground-truth imaginations in the constructed datasets. At inference the model uses these tokens directly and does not need to render an image, so the spatial reasoning stays in token space. The authors also show that combining IPT with label-only supervision yields further gains.

Solves vs predecessors

Chain-of-thought improves reasoning by writing intermediate steps in language, which works for symbolic and arithmetic tasks. For spatial problems that route loses information, and the paper shows textual chain-of-thought can lower accuracy. IPT fixes that failure by keeping the intermediate representation perceptual, so the model reasons about unseen viewpoints without compressing them into words.

Method vs predecessors

Where chain-of-thought produces a text trace, IPT produces perceptual tokens that stand for alternative spatial configurations, supervised with ground-truth imaginations. The result is interpretable intermediate states that improve generalization on perspective taking, path tracing, and multiview counting, in contrast to a language-only trace.
