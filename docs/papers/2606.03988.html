Imaginative Perception Tokens Enhance Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

Bigverdi, Li, Huang, Cho, Zhang, Krishna et al. · arXiv 2026-06-02 · arXiv:2606.03988 · Appears in: Multi-Modal

What

The paper introduces Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPT), intermediate perceptual representations that a vision-language model is trained to produce. They externalize what the model would perceive under an alternative spatial configuration (an unseen viewpoint, an occluded path, a set of partial views) while staying consistent with the observed input. On three tasks built for this study — Perspective Taking, Path Tracing, and Multiview Counting, each with about 20,000 examples — IPT supervision on the unified vision-language model BAGEL improves spatial reasoning and on Multiview Counting raises accuracy by 3.4 percent, without generating any image at inference time.

Why

Vision-language models struggle when the answer depends on something not directly visible and so requires picturing an unobserved spatial state. The standard fix is to make the model write a chain-of-thought in words. The paper questions that default: spatial computation may not belong in the language channel at all. It finds that textual chain-of-thought can substantially lower performance on these tasks, which prior work had not flagged.

How

The authors construct datasets with ground-truth "imaginations", answers, and benchmarks, and train BAGEL to emit IPT as a supervision signal. They compare IPT against textual chain-of-thought training and against answer-only training, and also test combining IPT with answer-only supervision. IPT consistently helps, the combination helps more, and the text trace hurts, which the authors read as a modality mismatch when spatial computation is forced through language.

Solves vs predecessors

Chain-of-Thought (Wei 2023) improves reasoning by making the model produce intermediate steps in natural language. On spatial problems this fails because the steps that matter are perceptual, not verbal, and the paper shows the language trace can actively reduce accuracy. IPT fixes that specific failure mode by recording the intermediate state as a perceptual representation in the native visual modality, so the reasoning happens where the relevant structure lives.

Method vs predecessors

Compared with Chain-of-Thought, IPT keeps the idea of an explicit intermediate trace but changes its modality: instead of words, the trace is a perceptual representation supervised against ground-truth imaginations. The model does not need to render images at inference time; the benefit comes from having been trained to form the representation. This places the work as a small bridge from the reasoning cluster (graphify community 0) into the vision-language cluster (community 3), with the finding that the text version of the trace is the wrong tool in that setting.
