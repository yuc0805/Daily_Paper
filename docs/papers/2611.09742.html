Regime change in a model for political opinion dynamics

Alexander Jochim, Stefan Bornholdt · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.09742

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Political crises involving a series of violent events are the rule rather than the exception in history. Here, we use a minimal opinion dynamics model to describe regime change as a contested transition between dominant shared ideas in a society. New opinions spread with a cumulative advantage, and agents do not return to opinions they held before, resulting in recurrent boom-and-bust dynamics without tuned parameters. During a transition, opinion changes grow approximately exponentially and then saturate in the finite system, declining approximately linearly as spreading is limited by the shrinking interfaces between opinion groups. We compare qualitative features of these transitions with historical data of political violence, used as an empirical proxy for contested transitions. A permutation test shows that violent events cluster in time in the dense Chinese and US records. Around their peaks, violent events rise in an accelerating way and then decline; in the Chinese data, the decline is consistent with the approximately linear decline in the model. We also use a fragmentation measure that serves as an early warning signal for transitions in the model. Overall, the patterns in the empirical data and the dynamical behavior of the model could contribute to a better understanding of long-term historical processes.
