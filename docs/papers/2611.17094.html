GRID-MRI: Resilience Assessment of Power Systems under Coordinated Cyber-Physical Attacks

Isaac Ortega Romero, Ioannis Zografopoulos · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.17094

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

The increasing penetration of inverter-based resources and growing cyber-physical interdependencies expose electrical power systems to disturbances that challenge conventional resilience assessments. Existing approaches often evaluate resilience dimensions independently, overlooking the non-additive effects of coordinated disruptions and the influence of exogenous stressors. To address this gap, this paper proposes a Grid Multidimensional Resilience Index (GRID-MRI) that integrates endogenous disruption severity, coordination effects, and climatic and economic-regulatory conditions. The framework quantifies physical, cyber, and operational degradation, identifies interaction effects through counterfactual (i.e., what-if), simulations of individual and coordinated attacks, and incorporates exogenous stressors as scenario-dependent scaling factors. Its application to a modified IEEE 39-bus system under three escalating cyber-physical attack scenarios, informed by the December 2025 Polish cyberattack incident, reveals that coordinated attacks induce system collapse despite stable individual and additive responses. The corresponding resilience losses are 3.72 and 4.09 times their additive estimates, while GRID-MRI increases from 0.062 to 1.188 and reaches 6.081 under additional exogenous stress. These findings demonstrate the limitations of additive assessments and establish a quantitative basis for distinguishing the dominant mechanisms of multidimensional resilience degradation.
