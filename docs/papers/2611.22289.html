Estimation and Model Selection in Factor Models with Long Memory

Dominik Ammon, Tobias Hartl, Rolf Tschernig · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.22289

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Panels in economics, finance and climate science are often driven by common components that differ in memory, such as common trends, cycles, or joint shocks. We study approximate factor models whose common factors are fractionally integrated with heterogeneous and unknown memory and develop the asymptotic theory of principal components for the panel in levels. Our theory builds on the eigenvalues of the sample covariance matrix being ordered by memory asymptotically: a nonstationary factor group with memory $d>1/2$ generates eigenvalues of order $NT^{2d-1}$, whereas stationary factors generate eigenvalues of order $N$. Principal components estimate the factors consistently up to rotation, while adjacent factor groups are separated at a rate given by their memory gap. The information criteria of Bai and Ng remain consistent for the total number of factors, with their penalties acting as thresholds on eigenvalues that are asymptotically ordered by factor memory. When the nonstationary factors lose their variance advantage, estimation in levels remains consistent, whereas after differencing the factors can become undetectable. A Monte Carlo study shows that factor-number selection in levels tolerates more idiosyncratic noise than selection after first differencing. For monthly temperatures at $308$ European weather stations, our methods select two factors. The first is a persistent common warming factor. The estimated common component implies that 2021-2025 was $2.15^\circ\mathrm{C}$ warmer than 1960-1990, with the strongest warming in Eastern Europe. After first differencing, the criterion selects one factor, and loses regional heterogeneity as compared to the model in levels.
