How Much Does Saliency Accuracy Matter for Saliency-Guided 360-degree Image Compression?

Sakib Ahmed, Sidi-Mohammed Senouci, Philippe Brunet, Guillaume Caron et al. · 2026 · arXiv · arXiv:2611.21532

Why recommended

Very recent in your research areas.

Abstract

Saliency-guided coding allocates bits to the regions viewers are most likely to attend, but accurate 360-degree saliency prediction is computationally expensive, an obstacle for low-latency immersive streaming applications such as robot teleoperation. We quantify how much saliency-prediction accuracy actually translates into compression gain. Building on a saliency-guided rate-distortion-optimized (RDO) learned codec, modified so that the training-time latent mask is driven by ground-truth saliency, which yields a single, predictor-agnostic codec, we evaluate saliency predictors spanning two orders of magnitude in compute on both the learned codec and H.265/HEVC. The results show that higher saliency-prediction accuracy yields diminishing compression gains: a lightweight predictor fast enough for live use (52 frames per second) captures 65% of the ground-truth oracle's bit-rate saving, a 3.3x heavier one reaches 74%, and a further 38x increase in compute adds no measurable benefit, on either codec. Therefore, lightweight predictors might also be a practical choice for latency-constrained 360-degree streaming.
