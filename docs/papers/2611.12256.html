Predictability of Collective Behavior in Networks of LLM Agents

Fabian Baumann, Michele Starnini · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.12256

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas. Somewhat similar to something you passed on before.

Abstract

As AI agents built on large language models (LLMs) increasingly interact at scale, anticipating their collective behavior becomes necessary. Unlike humans, individual LLM agents can be probed in {controlled scenarios}; however, the predictability of their collective outcomes hinges on what such measurements capture. We study information cascades on networks of LLM agents that decide whether to share a post given how many of their contacts have shared it. At the same fraction of sharing contacts, an agent's response depends on how many contacts it has and on the post. Responses that ignore the number of contacts mispredict the cascades, increasingly on heterogeneous networks, by an error we derive analytically and that network topology alone cannot reveal. Measured in context, the response predicts, through mean-field theory, the cascades of full LLM simulations, tipping points included. Anticipating the collective behavior of AI agents thus requires evaluating them in their local network context.
