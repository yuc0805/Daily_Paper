Network topology imposes spectral constraints on collective nonequilibrium responses

Dingjie Wu, Yaozu Wang, Rong Wang, Ying Wu · 2026 · Explore · arXiv:2611.32130

Why recommended

Picked for exploration — from another field, outside your usual research areas.

Abstract

Collective nonequilibrium dynamics in Langevin systems depend on both network topology and stochastic driving. Understanding the role of topology in responses to stochastic driving and in the emergence of nonequilibrium is essential. Starting from the principle of microscopic reversibility and linear Langevin dynamics on networks, we derive a mode decomposition for both the asymmetry of short-time cross-correlations and heat currents at steady state. This decomposition reveals two sources of nonequilibrium---fluctuations of individual modes and correlated fluctuations between distinct modes. For reciprocal networks with independent local driving, we derive two nonequilibrium response operators that yield explicit expressions for the global asymmetry intensity and the entropy production rate. These operators are weighted Laplacians independent of stochastic driving. Their spectra determine attainable upper and lower bounds on nonequilibrium at fixed driving heterogeneity. These bounds are exact for asymmetry intensity at arbitrary deviations from equilibrium and hold to leading order near equilibrium for entropy production rate. Finally, we find that the hierarchical modular organization of human structural connectomes broadens the brain's nonequilibrium operating range, with resting-state brain activity supporting this topological advantage for sustaining a lower nonequilibrium level. Our study provides a basis for understanding biological network organization and designing networks with targeted nonequilibrium operating points.
