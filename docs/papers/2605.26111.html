Squeezing Capacity from Multimodal Large Language Models for Subject-driven Generation

Anonymous et al. · arXiv 2026 · link · Appears in: Generative CV

What

The paper does subject-driven image generation: placing a specific subject from a few reference images into new scenes. It conditions a diffusion generator on a multimodal language model that jointly encodes the text prompt and reference images, adds VAE-based identity conditioning, and introduces a Dual Layer Aggregation module that combines features from several language-model layers rather than only the last one.

Why

Subject-driven generation needs to keep a subject's identity while following a new prompt, and conditioning a generator on a single pooled embedding from a multimodal model loses layer-specific detail that identity depends on. Prior approaches read only the final embedding, which limits how much identity information reaches the generator.

How

The Dual Layer Aggregation module pulls multi-level features from the multimodal model and combines them into the conditioning signal, while VAE-based identity conditioning supplies appearance detail. The combined signal improves identity preservation and prompt-following on subject-driven benchmarks relative to last-layer conditioning.

Builds on

  • DALL-E 2 (Ramesh 2022)Conditioned image generation on a single CLIP text-image embedding; this paper aggregates many layers instead.
  • DiT (Peebles 2023)Transformer diffusion backbone of the generation family.
  • DDPM (Ho 2020)The denoising-diffusion generative base.

Solves vs predecessors

DALL-E 2 conditioned on one pooled embedding, which preserves coarse semantics but loses fine identity detail. This paper recovers that detail by aggregating across language-model layers, improving identity preservation that single-vector conditioning could not hold.

Method vs predecessors

Where DALL-E 2 changed the conditioning source and DiT changed the backbone, this paper changes the conditioning interface from one vector to a multi-level stack, plus VAE identity cues. The modeling change is in how the frozen multimodal model is read, not in the diffusion process itself.
